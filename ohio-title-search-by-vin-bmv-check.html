Ohio Title Search by VIN: How to Use the Ohio BMV Title Check (and What It Won't Show)

Direct answer: Ohio runs a free online title search by VIN. From the BMV Online Services hub at bmvonline.dps.ohio.gov, open the Ohio Title Portal (Vehicle/Watercraft Title Search), choose the Vehicle/VIN search, and enter the 17-character VIN. It needs no account — but by its own disclaimer it discloses only titles issued in Ohio. It does not show out-of-state title history, and it cannot display the NMVTIS junk/salvage-yard records government agencies are barred from sharing with the public.

Key facts

  • Free tool: Ohio BMV "Online Services Vehicle/Watercraft Title Search," reached from the Ohio Title Portal at bmvonline.dps.ohio.gov. Free public lookup, no account or login - each search tab does carry a one-click "I'm not a robot" check (as of 2026-11-05). Not a certified record.
  • Search types: Title Number, VIN, HIN, MIN. For cars and light trucks use the Vehicle / VIN search. HIN and MIN are for watercraft and outboard motors and are out of scope here.
  • Hard scope limit (verbatim from the tool): it "ONLY discloses details about titles issued in the State of Ohio… It does not show whether a vehicle was titled in another state/jurisdiction." NMVTIS brand and junk/salvage data are collected by county Clerks of Courts but "government agencies are prohibited from sharing the NMVTIS information with the Public," so they are not shown.
  • Salvage trigger (R.C. 4505.11): an Ohio salvage certificate issues when an insurer "declares it economically impractical to repair" the vehicle and settles with the owner. There is no fixed percentage threshold in Ohio law.
  • Rebuilt salvage (R.C. 4505.11(E)): after repair and an Ohio State Highway Patrol inspection, the salvage certificate is surrendered and a new title issues marked "REBUILT SALVAGE" in black boldface.
  • Certified record (R.C. 4505.14): a certified Ohio title/ownership record costs $5.00 per copy (form BMV 1174 states "$5.00 Fee per Record"); request via BMV form BMV 1173 or county Clerk of Courts form BMV 1174. It shows title number, title status, owner and address, previous owner and address, and lienholder. It is not an open lookup: Section 4 of the form makes you qualify under R.C. 4501.27, and a requester who is not the owner generally needs the owner's notarized written consent (BMV form 5008). The form also warns the search "may not provide last known address of vehicle owner(s)."
  • Cross-state brand authority: NMVTIS (vehiclehistory.gov → vehiclehistory.bja.ojp.gov), run under the U.S. DOJ Bureau of Justice Assistance, is the federal title-brand backstop. Consumers buy it through an approved provider.

How do I run an Ohio title search by VIN for free?

Ohio publishes a free online title search keyed on the VIN, and it is the right first stop for the Ohio legal title. Open the BMV Online Services hub at bmvonline.dps.ohio.gov, follow the link to the Ohio Title Portal (the "Online Services Vehicle/Watercraft Title Search"), pick the Vehicle / VIN search, and enter the 17-character VIN. It is a free public lookup — no sign-up, no login, no fee to run it — as of 2026-11-05, with a one-click "I'm not a robot" check on each search tab. The .gov page itself does not print the word "free" or a $0 figure; it only links a separate "BMV Fees" page, so treat the lookup as free-to-use but not a certified record.

The tool offers four search types, each with its own button: Title Number, VIN, HIN (Hull Identification Number), and MIN (Motor Identification Number). For a car or light truck you only need the VIN search; the HIN and MIN searches are for watercraft and outboard motors, which this guide excludes. This is the same free-state-lookup pattern mapped for other states, including the companion florida title check and nys title check guides. What does the Ohio BMV title search actually show — and what does it leave out?

The free Ohio search is authoritative for the Ohio-issued title record and blind to everything that happened outside Ohio. Its own disclaimer, headed "THIS WEBSITE PROVIDES LIMITED INFORMATION," states that it "ONLY discloses details about titles issued in the State of Ohio" and "does not show whether a vehicle was titled in another state/jurisdiction for any period of time." That is the single most important fact about this tool, and it is why the free search is not a complete legal-brand lookup.

There is a second, deliberate gap. Ohio County Clerks of Courts issue titles through the Ohio Automated Title Processing System (ATPS) and, through NMVTIS, are given a history of brands and junk/salvage-yard notations applied by other states. But the disclaimer is explicit: "government agencies are prohibited from sharing the NMVTIS information with the Public, so that information will not be displayed in the title search results." So the free tool shows you the current Ohio title and any Ohio-applied brand or lien in ATPS — it does not show out-of-state brands or the federal junk/salvage history the clerk can see. The BMV also notes it "does not endorse any specific NMVTIS Data Provider." The .gov page does not enumerate exactly which fields a result returns, so confirm the on-screen fields by running a VIN rather than assuming it shows a specific brand or lien line.

Why doesn't the free Ohio search show out-of-state or NMVTIS brands, and how do I get them?

Because Ohio's free tool is Ohio-only and federal law blocks it from publishing NMVTIS data, you need two other sources to see the full legal picture. First, the authoritative Ohio record: a certified Ohio title record shows owner, previous owner and lienholder information. Under Ohio Revised Code 4505.14 the fee is $5.00 per copy ("For each report of a search of the records, the fee is five dollars per copy"). You request it from the BMV (form BMV 1173) or from a county Clerk of Courts (form BMV 1174); confirm the current fee on the BMV record-request page before you pay. Read this before planning on it as a buyer: Section 4 of form BMV 1174 requires you to qualify under R.C. 4501.27, and its categories are things like requesting your own record, holding the other person's notarized written consent (BMV form 5008), acting for a government agency, or working under a court order - there is no general "I am thinking of buying this car" category. In practice a private buyer either gets the seller to consent in writing or does without this record.

Second, the cross-state brand history: an NMVTIS report. The National Motor Vehicle Title Information System sits under the U.S. Department of Justice Bureau of Justice Assistance at vehiclehistory.gov (which redirects to vehiclehistory.bja.ojp.gov). State titling agencies report title and brand data to it; federal law requires monthly reporting, under rules published in the Federal Register on 30 January 2009: insurance carriers report the junk and salvage vehicles they obtain (28 CFR 25.55, with no vehicle-count threshold), while junk yards, salvage yards and recyclers report a monthly inventory and are exempt only if they handle fewer than five such vehicles a year (28 CFR 25.56). Consumers cannot query NMVTIS directly — you buy a report from an Approved NMVTIS Data Provider — and it shows title brands, salvage/total-loss, junk, and brand history across states. The DOJ site does not publish a price; each provider sets its own. For how a carried-over brand reads, see what is a branded title.

How can I tell if an Ohio car has a salvage or rebuilt title?

In Ohio the two brands mean different things, and both are set by statute, not by a VIN report. Under R.C. 4505.11, a salvage certificate of title is issued by a clerk of a court of common pleas when an insurance company (or a self-insurer, lessor or secured creditor) "declares it economically impractical to repair" the vehicle and pays an agreed settlement. The salvage certificate carries the notice "SALVAGE MOTOR VEHICLE — PURSUANT TO R.C. 4738.01." A salvage vehicle is not road-legal as-is.

Once a salvage vehicle is restored for road use, it must be inspected by the Ohio State Highway Patrol; the salvage certificate is surrendered and a new certificate of title issues bearing the words "REBUILT SALVAGE" in black boldface on its face (R.C. 4505.11(E)). One point corrects a common myth: Ohio sets no fixed percentage-of-value threshold. The trigger is the insurer's "economically impractical to repair" determination — not a 70% or 75% rule. Neither R.C. 4505.11 nor the R.C. 4738.01 definition it points to contains a percentage figure, so any such number you see quoted is insurer practice rather than a threshold in those statutes. Who should use which

You need the Ohio legal title and any Ohio-applied brand or lien: run the free Ohio BMV VIN title search first. It is the live Ohio authority and costs nothing.

You need the authoritative Ohio record on paper, with owner and lienholder history: order a certified Ohio title record ($5.00 per copy under R.C. 4505.14) from the BMV or a county Clerk of Courts - but only if you can qualify under R.C. 4501.27, which for a non-owner usually means the owner's notarized written consent on BMV form 5008.

You need the cross-state legal brand — including a brand Ohio inherited from another state: pull an NMVTIS report through an approved provider. This is the gap the free Ohio tool openly refuses to fill.

You need the car's damage, mileage and ownership history, not just title status: you need a paid VIN history report. The free Ohio search holds no accidents, no odometer trail and no auction records — that on-the-ground history is where a title-washing concern actually gets resolved.

What to verify by VIN before you pay in Ohio

  1. Current Ohio title and brand — run the free Ohio BMV VIN title search; confirm the Ohio title status and any Ohio-applied brand or lien note.
  2. Cross-state brand — because the free tool is Ohio-only, pull an NMVTIS report for any salvage, junk or total-loss brand applied by another state.
  3. Lien and ownership chain — a certified Ohio title record ($5) carries the owner, previous owner and lienholder, the fields the free tool may not fully display — but you must qualify under R.C. 4501.27 to get it, so as a buyer plan on asking the seller for written consent (BMV form 5008) or on going without it.
  4. Salvage / rebuilt status — look for the "REBUILT SALVAGE" marking and for junk/salvage auction records that precede a brand.
  5. Odometer and accidents — pull a paid VIN report for rollback screening and accident/airbag-deployment records the free check never shows.
  6. Listing and ownership trail — review prior owners and past sales listings for a story that does not add up before you commit.
