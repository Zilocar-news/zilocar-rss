Ford Filed 32% Fewer Recalls in 2026 — and Recalled a Record 14.1 Million Vehicles Doing It

Ford filed 75 vehicle-recall campaigns with NHTSA between January 1 and October 6, 2026, down 32.4% from the 111 it filed in the same window of 2025. Over the same period the number of vehicles those recalls cover rose 43.7%, to 14,087,510 — already more than Ford's record full year of 2025, with nearly three months left to run. The widely repeated line that 2026 is Ford's worst recall year is right about how many cars are affected and wrong about how often Ford is filing.

Key facts

  • 75 Ford vehicle-recall campaigns filed Jan 1–Oct 6, 2026, against 111 in the same 2025 window — −32.4%.
  • 14,087,510 vehicles covered, against 9,800,194 — +43.7%, and the highest Jan–Oct total for Ford in the 16 years the file covers.
  • Ford's 2026 total already exceeds its entire record year 2025 (12,914,776 vehicles across 146 campaigns) by 9.1%.
  • Ford is 43.1% of all vehicles covered by 2026 US vehicle recalls (14,087,510 of 32,668,351) while filing 12.3% of the campaigns.
  • 31.1% of Ford's 2026 units sit in a single campaign, 26V104000 (4,381,878 vehicles, integrated trailer brake control software).
  • The divergence is not unique to Ford: four filers with 10+ campaigns show count-down, units-up — Ford, Honda, Mercedes-Benz and Kia.
  • Every one of Ford's 75 2026 campaigns was re-verified against NHTSA's live API: 75 exact matches, 0 mismatches.

Is Ford filing more recalls in 2026, or fewer?

Fewer — markedly fewer. Ford filed 75 vehicle-recall campaigns in the first nine months and six days of 2026 against 111 in the identical 2025 window, a 32.4% decline. That is the opposite direction from the dominant narrative of the year, which has run on campaign counts since the spring: trade and consumer coverage in March 2026 noted Ford had "17 recalls issued so far this year" and was on track for "its worst year for recalls ever."

What has risen, steeply, is scope. The same 75 campaigns cover 14,087,510 vehicles, up 43.7% from 9,800,194. Ford is recalling far more cars across far fewer filings — each 2026 campaign averages 187,833 vehicles against 88,290 in 2025, more than double.

Has Ford already beaten its record year?

On vehicles, yes, with a quarter of the year still to go. Ford's record full-year total — the 12.92 million figure quoted widely through 2026 — resolves in the flat file to 12,914,776 vehicles across 146 vehicle-recall campaigns for calendar 2025. Ford's 2026 figure passed that on the strength of the first nine months alone, and stands 9.1% above it.

On campaign counts the picture inverts again: 146 campaigns for all of 2025 against 75 so far in 2026, a pace that would land 2026 well short.

Which manufacturers recalled the most vehicles in 2026?

Ford, by a margin no other filer approaches. Ford's 14,087,510 vehicles are 43.1% of the 32,668,351 covered by every US vehicle-recall campaign filed in 2026 — from 12.3% of the campaigns. Chrysler is second on units at 5,655,743.

The industry as a whole shows the same count-versus-scope split as Ford, in milder form: 611 campaigns in 2026 against 650 in 2025 (−6.0%), covering 32,668,351 vehicles against 21,024,569 (+55.4%).

Is Ford the only automaker whose count fell while its vehicles rose?

No, and this is the check that changed the finding. The tempting claim — that Ford alone is deflating its campaign count while inflating its exposure — does not survive a stated universe. Among the 23 manufacturers filing 10 or more vehicle-recall campaigns in either window, four show count-down and units-up: Ford (111→75, units +43.7%), Honda (16→12, +261.3%), Mercedes-Benz (16→10, +253.8%) and Kia (10→9, +6.7%). Restricting to filers covering 100,000+ vehicles returns the same four.

That is a 2026 pattern across several major automakers, not a Ford peculiarity. Ford is simply its largest and steepest instance. Two other large filers moved the opposite way — Volkswagen (−35.0% campaigns, −11.8% units) and BMW (−14.3%, −50.6%) — where count and scope both fell, which is not the same phenomenon at all.

Method: how we counted this, and how to verify it yourself

The source is NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation recall flat file, FLAT_RCL_POST_2010.txt, downloaded 2026-10-06 at 07:14 UTC. The server reported Last-Modified: Tue, 06 Oct 2026 07:04:59 GMT. The file holds 245,719 tab-separated rows and the latest RCDATE in it is 2026-10-01.

The step that decides whether the numbers are right: the flat file carries one row per model-year per model inside a campaign, while POTAFF (potentially affected units) is a campaign-level figure repeated identically on each of those rows. Campaign 26V606000 appears on 42 rows, each reading 41,748. Summing the column would multiply-count every campaign. So each campaign is collapsed to a single record keyed on CAMPNO before any aggregation.

From there: keep RCLTYPECD = 'V' (vehicle recalls, excluding equipment, tyre and child-seat campaigns); bucket on RCDATE into January 1–October 6 of 2025 and of 2026; aggregate campaign counts and POTAFF by the MFGTXT filer name. The comparison universe is 1,261 campaigns — 650 in 2025 and 611 in 2026. Ford's own figures rest on 186 campaigns.

Corroboration against a second NHTSA system. Every one of Ford's 75 2026 campaigns was queried individually against api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber and its PotentialNumberofUnitsAffected compared with the flat file: 75 exact matches, 0 mismatches, 0 campaigns missing from the API.

Validation against a published figure. Run over calendar 2025, the same pipeline returns 12,914,776 vehicles for Ford — reproducing the "12.92 million" total reported independently in March 2026 — across 146 vehicle-type campaigns, or 153 including equipment-type filings against the 152 campaigns that coverage cited. The method lands on an externally published number without being fitted to it.

Known limits, each of which bounds a claim above.

  • Concentration. 31.1% of Ford's 2026 units are campaign 26V104000 alone. With each year's largest campaign removed, Ford's unit growth falls from +43.7% to +16.3%. The direction survives; the magnitude is heavily leveraged on one filing.
  • Which date column. Counting Ford's 2026 campaigns by RCDATE (the filing date used throughout) gives 75; by ODATE (owner-notification date) it gives 81. Any published "Ford's Nth recall of 2026" figure depends on an unstated column choice, which is one reason such counts disagree across outlets.
  • Scoping is a choice, not a measurement. One campaign spanning six nameplates and six separate campaigns can describe the same underlying defects. Campaign counts are therefore partly an artefact of manufacturer and agency practice, and neither counts nor unit totals measure defect rates, failure frequency or injuries.
  • POTAFF is an estimate of population, not of faults. It is the number of vehicles that may contain the defect, filed by the manufacturer. It is not a count of defective vehicles and not a count of vehicles repaired.
  • Filer names are not corporate families. MFGTXT separates, for example, Honda's US arm from Honda Motor Co.; figures here are per filer name as NHTSA records it, not consolidated by parent group.

The frozen dataset behind every figure is published alongside this article: the per-filer comparison for all 172 filers, Ford's 186 campaign-level rows, and Ford's 16-year January–October series.

Why this is in the news: the headlight recall that was two weeks old

Ford's recall activity returned to national coverage on October 5–6, 2026 with campaign 26V606000 — 41,748 vehicles, covering 2025–2027 Expedition and 2026 F-250, F-350, F-450 and F-550 Super Duty trucks, over a contaminated LED chip in the headlight assembly that can cause loss of daytime running lights, parking lights, high beams or low beams. Ford's internal reference is 26C43 and owner letters were expected from October 26.

Two details put it in proportion. NHTSA recorded the campaign on September 22, 2026 — it was roughly two weeks old by the time it was news, because recalls generally surface publicly when NHTSA publishes its weekly summary rather than when a manufacturer files. And at 41,748 vehicles it is 0.3% of Ford's 2026 total: a campaign that drew coverage across at least eight national outlets accounts for three-thousandths of the year's exposure, while 26V104000, which is 105 times larger, drew a fraction of the attention.

What a VIN check can and can't tell you here

A used Ford from these model years is a reasonable buy, and a 4.4-million-vehicle campaign is not a verdict on an individual truck. What matters is the specific VIN.

A Zilocar VIN check can show: whether open recalls exist on that vehicle and how many; accident and damage records including airbag deployment; odometer readings and rollback indicators; theft records (NICB); junk, salvage and auction records; ownership history; sales-listing history; specifications; NHTSA and IIHS ratings; and valuation.

It cannot show: whether a recall remedy was actually performed, whether a software or over-the-air fix was flashed to that particular vehicle, open NHTSA investigations that have not yet become recalls, per-unit firmware state, or a legal title-brand classification. For repair status, NHTSA's own VIN lookup and a franchised Ford dealer are the authorities, and for title brands it is NMVTIS and the state DMV.

The honest division of labour: use a VIN check to find out what has happened to the car and whether recalls are outstanding, then use the dealer or NHTSA to confirm what has been fixed.

Frequently asked questions

How many vehicles has Ford recalled in 2026? 14,087,510 vehicles across 75 vehicle-recall campaigns filed January 1 to October 6, 2026, per NHTSA's recall flat file retrieved October 6, 2026. That already exceeds Ford's record full year of 2025 (12,914,776 across 146 campaigns) with nearly three months still to run.

Is Ford filing more recalls in 2026 than in 2025? No — fewer. 75 campaigns against 111 in the same 2025 window, down 32.4%. The vehicles covered rose 43.7%. Coverage calling 2026 Ford's worst recall year is right about exposure and wrong about frequency.

What share of all 2026 US recall activity is Ford? 43.1% of every vehicle covered by a 2026 US vehicle-recall campaign (14,087,510 of 32,668,351), from 12.3% of the campaigns (75 of 611).

What is Ford's largest 2026 recall? Campaign 26V104000, filed February 20, 2026: 4,381,878 E-Transit, Expedition, F-150, F-250 SD, F-350 SD and Maverick vehicles, over integrated trailer brake control software. It alone is 31.1% of Ford's 2026 unit total.

Is Ford the only automaker whose recall count fell while its recalled vehicles rose? No. Four of the 23 filers with 10+ campaigns in either window show it: Ford, Honda, Mercedes-Benz and Kia. Ford is the largest and steepest case, not a unique one.

Why did the Expedition and Super Duty headlight recall make news in October if it was filed in September? Campaign 26V606000 was received by NHTSA on September 22, 2026 and reached national coverage on October 5–6, because recalls typically become news when NHTSA publishes its weekly summary. It covers 41,748 vehicles, or 0.3% of Ford's 2026 total.

Does a falling recall count mean Ford's cars got safer? The data cannot support that. Counts measure filings, and how broadly each campaign is scoped is a practice choice. Nothing here measures defect rates, failures per vehicle or injuries.

Can a VIN check tell me whether a specific used Ford was repaired under one of these recalls? No. A VIN check shows whether open recalls exist — the screening step before buying. Repair and software-flash status is not in any commercial VIN feed; NHTSA's VIN lookup and a Ford dealer are the authorities.
