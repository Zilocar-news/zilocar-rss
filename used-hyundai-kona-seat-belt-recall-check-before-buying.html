Used Hyundai Kona Seat-Belt Recall (26V452000): What to Check by VIN Before Buying

TL;DR: Hyundai recall 26V452000, filed with NHTSA on July 14, 2026, covers 47,749 vehicles — 47,733 model-year 2026 Kona (gasoline) and 16 model-year 2025 Kona Electric — because the rear-center seat-belt buckle may not properly restrain an occupant in a crash. Dealers replace the buckle free; owner letters are due September 11, 2026. A VIN check shows the recall is present, but only NHTSA's lookup or a Hyundai dealer confirms the buckle was replaced.

Key facts

  • NHTSA campaign number: 26V452000 (news often shortens to "26V452"); Hyundai reference number 306.
  • Vehicles covered: 47,749 total — 47,733 MY2026 Hyundai Kona (gasoline) + 16 MY2025 Hyundai Kona Electric. No older Kona years are covered.
  • Component: Rear-center seat-belt buckle assembly (NHTSA class: "SEAT BELTS: REAR/OTHER: BUCKLE ASSEMBLY").
  • Defect: The rear-center buckle may not properly restrain an occupant in a crash, increasing injury risk.
  • Root cause (per news/trade reporting): Out-of-spec buckles from supplier Joyson Safety Systems; worn metal stamping dies stayed in service past their intended lifespan during a defined production window. Joyson notified Hyundai in February 2026.
  • Interim guidance: Do not use the rear-center seat until the buckle is replaced.
  • Remedy: Dealers replace the buckle assembly free of charge (a physical part, not a software fix); Hyundai reimburses prior out-of-pocket repairs.
  • Timeline: Filed with NHTSA July 14, 2026; VINs searchable July 15, 2026; owner letters due September 11, 2026.
  • Risk status: No confirmed crashes, injuries, or fires; Hyundai says it acted "out of an abundance of caution."
  • Hyundai customer service: 1-855-371-9460 (reference recall 306).

Which Hyundai Konas are covered by recall 26V452000?

Recall 26V452000 covers exactly two configurations: the 2026 Hyundai Kona (gasoline) and the 2025 Hyundai Kona Electric. It does not cover all Kona model years, and it is not a broad "2025–2026 Kona" recall — the gasoline Kona is affected only for model year 2026, and the Kona Electric only for model year 2025. If you are looking at an older Kona, or a 2025 gasoline Kona, this specific recall does not apply.

What is actually wrong with the seat belt?

The problem is the rear-center seat-belt buckle — the receiver that the latch plate clicks into for the middle rear seating position. On affected cars it may not properly restrain an occupant during a crash, which raises the risk of injury. The other seating positions are not part of this recall.

According to news and trade summaries of Hyundai's chronology, the buckles were manufactured out of specification by supplier Joyson Safety Systems: inadequate inspection controls let metal stamping dies stay in service beyond their intended lifespan, so excessive die wear produced out-of-spec parts during a defined production window. Joyson flagged the issue to Hyundai in February 2026 after testing. The supplier identity and worn-die cause are high-confidence but drawn from reporting, not a primary document read directly.

Is it safe to drive a recalled Kona before the fix?

Yes, with one restriction: NHTSA's remedy guidance says passengers should not use the rear-center seat until the buckle assembly is replaced. The rest of the vehicle is not subject to this recall, and at the time of filing there were no confirmed crashes, injuries, or fires linked to the defect — Hyundai described the action as being taken "out of an abundance of caution." If you or a passenger would regularly use the middle rear seat (for a car seat, for example), factor the repair timing into your decision.

How do I check a used Kona's VIN for this recall?

Enter the car's 17-character VIN into a free recall lookup before you buy. The most authoritative option is NHTSA's tool at NHTSA.gov/recalls, which shows open recalls by VIN and, importantly, whether the repair has already been completed. Hyundai's own lookup at owners.hyundaiusa.com does the same for Hyundai vehicles. A Zilocar VIN check will also surface whether an open recall is present on the car, alongside the history data a listing hides — but for confirming that this specific buckle repair was performed, rely on NHTSA or a Hyundai dealer.

This step matters for used buyers because Hyundai's owner letters are not due until September 11, 2026. Many current listings predate that notice, so a private seller may genuinely not know the rear-center buckle is under recall. Do not take "no recalls that I know of" at face value — check the VIN yourself.

What else should I check on a used Kona before buying?

Beyond this recall, the same due diligence applies to any used Kona: pull the accident and damage history (including whether airbags ever deployed), check for salvage or junk auction records, verify the odometer against service and listing records for signs of rollback, confirm the number of prior owners, and review past sales listings for price and mileage consistency. Cross-reference NHTSA and IIHS safety ratings for the specific model year, and compare the asking price against market valuation. These history signals — not the seat-belt recall, which is a free and straightforward fix — are what typically separate a good used Kona from one to walk away from.

Frequently asked questions

Is the 2026 Kona or 2025 Kona Electric I'm looking at part of the recall? Recall 26V452000 covers 47,733 model-year 2026 Kona (gasoline) and 16 model-year 2025 Kona Electric vehicles built roughly from July 8, 2025. Only those two configurations are affected. Confirm any specific car by entering its VIN at NHTSA.gov/recalls.

How do I check a used Kona's VIN for open recalls? Enter the 17-character VIN at NHTSA.gov/recalls or owners.hyundaiusa.com — both are free, and NHTSA shows whether the repair was completed. A VIN history report surfaces recall presence too, plus accident, salvage-auction, odometer, and ownership history.

Can a VIN check confirm the buckle was actually fixed? No. A VIN history report shows a recall is present, not whether it was remedied. To confirm the rear-center buckle was replaced, use NHTSA's VIN lookup (which shows completed-repair status) or call a Hyundai dealer with the VIN.

Does the recall repair cost me anything as a used buyer? No. Hyundai dealers replace the rear-center seat-belt buckle assembly free of charge, regardless of how many owners the car has had. Hyundai will also reimburse owners who previously paid out of pocket. Reference recall 306 or campaign 26V452000.

Is it safe to ride in a recalled Kona before the fix? Per NHTSA guidance, passengers should not use the rear-center seat until the buckle is replaced. Other seating positions are not covered, and no crashes or injuries had been reported when the recall was filed.

Will a private seller even know about the recall? Possibly not. Hyundai's owner letters are not due until September 11, 2026, so a seller who bought earlier may be unaware of the defect. Verify the VIN yourself at NHTSA.gov/recalls rather than relying on the seller.

Can a prior owner get reimbursed if they already paid for the repair? Yes. Hyundai has said it will reimburse owners who previously paid out of pocket for the buckle repair. Keep the receipt and reference recall 306 when contacting Hyundai at 1-855-371-9460.

Should this recall make me negotiate the price or walk away? The recall by itself is not a dealbreaker — the fix is free at any Hyundai dealer. More serious concerns are prior accidents, airbag deployment, salvage-auction records, or odometer rollback, which a full history report can surface before you commit.
