The Used Cars Sold With No Title: Inside the 2025-2026 Dealer-Fraud Cluster Leaving Buyers Stuck With Loans They Can't Drive

TL;DR: Across 2025 and 2026, three separate dealer-fraud cases exposed the same buyer trap: a car whose paperwork is broken before you ever get the keys. Colorado seized Off-Road Automotive after finding it had no title for 48 of 52 cars sold; an Iowa dealer allegedly pledged the same 81 vehicles to two lenders at once; and subprime giant Tricolor collapsed after duplicating VINs to raise multiple loans on single cars. A VIN history report can flag a dealer you shouldn't trust, but it cannot read the legal title brand or confirm a lien — for that, pair it with a state DMV/title check before you pay.

Key facts

  • No NHTSA campaign applies. This is a title-integrity and consumer-protection story, not a defect recall.
  • Off-Road Automotive (Fort Lupton, CO): Colorado's Department of Revenue found the dealer lacked titles for 48 of 52 vehicles (~92%) sold between Dec. 4, 2024 and Aug. 13, 2025. The Motor Vehicle Dealer Board voted unanimously to suspend its license on Sept. 30, 2025; the case drew national coverage on Feb. 12, 2026.
  • Sky Auto Mall (Center Point & Newhall, Iowa): Ford Credit and Stellantis Financial each identified the same 81 vehicles allegedly financed to both lenders — "floorplan fraud." Stellantis sued Mar. 2, 2026 (~$12.3M claimed); Ford Credit sued Mar. 5, 2026 (~$6.6M). Combined exposure exceeds $18M. Roughly 76 employees were laid off days later.
  • Tricolor Holdings LLC: The subprime dealer/lender (~65 stores across TX/CA/NV/AZ, 60,000+ loans) filed Chapter 7 on Sept. 10, 2025. DOJ unsealed an indictment on Dec. 17, 2025 charging founder Daniel Chu; the alleged scheme duplicated VINs and double-pledged collateral. JPMorgan disclosed a ~$170M loss.
  • The buyer, not the manufacturer, absorbs the harm in the title-integrity cases: you can still owe the loan while being unable to register or drive the car.
  • Correction to early framing: these are a 2025-2026 cluster, not simultaneous August 2026 events.

What actually happened — three cases, one buyer lesson

These three cases are unrelated companies in different states, but they share a spine: the defect is in the paperwork, not the sheet metal. A car can pass a test drive and still carry a broken or missing title, a hidden lender's lien, or a VIN duplicated to raise loans elsewhere. That is why an accident-and-odometer check alone is not enough here.

Can a dealer legally sell you a car it doesn't hold the title to?

No — a licensed dealer is expected to deliver a clean, transferable title, and failing to do so can cost the dealer its license. Colorado's Department of Revenue seized Off-Road Automotive precisely because it could not produce titles for most of what it sold. At the Sept. 30, 2025 Dealer Board meeting, the state presented an affidavit of probable cause, and the board found the dealer "engaged in deliberate and willful acts" posing "a threat to the public health, safety, and welfare."

The buyer harm is brutal in its simplicity. Because titles were never transferred, buyers could not register the cars, yet they remained obligated on their auto loans. Greeley buyers Mindy Gomer and Brian Martin bought two trucks; one Dodge could not be registered. The dealership website went dark and the owner was unreachable by press.

What happens to your loan if the title never comes?

You generally keep owing the loan even if you cannot legally drive the car. The financing contract and the title transfer are separate obligations, so a dealer's failure to deliver the title does not cancel your debt to the lender. That gap — money owed on a car you can't use — is the single most damaging feature of the titleless-sales case.

If you are already in this position, file a complaint with your state motor vehicle regulator or dealer board, preserve every document, and ask whether a state dealer-bond or recovery fund covers your loss. Whether Colorado's dealer bond covers Off-Road's buyers has not been confirmed, so do not assume restitution is automatic.

How to check a used car's history before buying from a dealer

Work through a fixed sequence before any money changes hands. No single tool covers everything, which is exactly the gap these victims fell into. Use two lookups that answer two different questions.

  1. Pull the VIN history report first. Read the ownership trail and the sales-listing history — prior and current listings, asking prices, mileage and days-on-market. A car quietly relisted again and again, or whose mileage and price don't reconcile, is telling you something about the seller. Check junk/salvage auction records (a car auctioned as salvage but sold "clean"), the odometer/rollback check, accident and theft records, and recall presence. Free federal tools like NHTSA's recall lookup, an NMVTIS-approved provider, and a VIN history service such as Zilocar all live at this step; use the free authoritative sources first and the history report to judge the dealer's story.
  2. Then run a state DMV / title-and-lien lookup. This is the step the VIN history cannot do for you: confirm the legal title is present, unbranded, and free of an active lien before you sign.
  3. Verify the dealer's license and complaint record with your state motor vehicle regulator.
  4. Never accept "we're still getting the title." Delayed paperwork is the exact pattern that sank Off-Road's buyers. No title in hand, no money.

What a VIN check can and can't tell you here

Be precise about the limits, because this is a title-and-lien story and a VIN history report does not read the legal title. It cannot classify the title brand, confirm whether a lien or floorplan loan is active or discharged, or prove a car is "titleless." It would not have caught the missing titles at Off-Road or the double-pledged VINs at Sky Auto Mall on its own.

What it can honestly do is expose a dealer whose story doesn't hold up.

Is there a cooling-off period to return a used car?

There is no nationwide right to return a used car, and no cooling-off period fixes a missing title or a hidden lien by itself. California is the notable exception: its existing Car Buyer's Bill of Rights offers only an optional, buyer-paid two-day cancellation option on used cars under $40,000. That is being replaced by the CARS Act (Combating Auto Retail Scams Act), signed by Gov. Newsom, which takes effect October 2026 and creates a mandatory three-business-day cooling-off period for qualifying used vehicles (reported at or below roughly $48,000, driven under 400 miles), with a refund minus a restocking fee, mileage deduction and daily-use fee. It is California-only. Do not treat a three-day return as a national backstop.

A Zilocar VIN check screens for recall presence and surfaces accident and airbag-deployment records, salvage/junk-auction history, odometer/rollback flags, theft (NICB) records, ownership history, sales-listing history, specs, NHTSA and IIHS ratings, and market valuation — enough to judge whether a dealer's "clean car" claim survives contact with the record. Frequently asked questions

Can a dealer legally sell me a car it doesn't hold the title to? No. A licensed dealer must be able to transfer a clean title. Colorado regulators seized Off-Road Automotive after finding it lacked titles for 48 of 52 cars sold. Buyers still owed their loans but couldn't register or legally drive the vehicles.

What's the difference between a VIN history report and a state DMV title check? A VIN history report shows the car's past: ownership, listing history, salvage-auction records, odometer, accidents and recall presence. A DMV or NMVTIS-backed check confirms the legal title brand and whether a lien is active. Use both.

What happens to my loan if the dealer never transfers the title? You typically remain obligated on the loan even if you can't register the car. That's the harm Colorado buyers suffered: the debt stayed, the usable car did not.

How do I find out if a used car still has a lien against it? A VIN history report does not confirm an active lien. Use your state DMV or an NMVTIS-based title check to see the recorded lienholder. Cars sold "out of trust" can carry an undischarged floorplan lien.

How do I check if a car went through a salvage or junk auction if it's advertised as clean? A VIN history report can surface junk and salvage auction records even when a car is marketed as clean. If auction records conflict with a "clean" pitch, confirm the legal title brand with your state DMV.

Is there a cooling-off period to return a used car? No nationwide right exists. California's CARS Act creates a mandatory three-business-day cooling-off period for qualifying used vehicles, effective October 2026, but it's California-only and won't fix a missing title or hidden lien.

What do I do right now if I already bought a car and the title never came? File a complaint with your state motor vehicle regulator or dealer board, keep every document, and check whether a state dealer-bond or recovery fund applies. Keep addressing the loan with your lender; the debt does not vanish.
