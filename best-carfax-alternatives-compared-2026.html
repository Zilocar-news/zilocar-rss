Best Carfax Alternatives in 2026: Carfax vs AutoCheck vs Bumper vs Zilocar Compared

The four vehicle history reports a US used-car buyer actually chooses between in 2026 are Carfax, AutoCheck, Bumper and Zilocar. As of 2026-09-20, AutoCheck's single report is reported lowest at $29.99, below Zilocar's $39.50 and Carfax's reported $44.99; Bumper is subscription-only from a reported $19.99 per 30-day period. No report is authoritative on the legal title brand or on whether a recall was actually fixed — NMVTIS and NHTSA are.

  • AutoCheck's strength is Experian-backed wholesale and salvage auction data (Manheim/ADESA feeds) plus the AutoCheck Score, a 0-100 relative-risk rating Carfax does not provide.
  • Bumper is a subscription aggregator of public and partner records; its FAQ lists $19.99 per 30-day period or $12.99 per period on a three-period plan ($38.97), as of 2026-09-20.
  • Zilocar's differentiator is sales-listing history (past and current listings with prices, mileage and days-on-market) alongside accident, salvage-auction, odometer and ownership history.
  • No paid report confirms a recall was remedied, flags NHTSA investigations, or issues the legal title brand — those belong to NHTSA, the dealer, and NMVTIS/the state DMV.

What's the difference between Carfax and AutoCheck?

The core difference is the data each company owns. Carfax runs a proprietary network weighted toward service and maintenance records and dealer-reported events, reported at 150,000-plus sources including direct integrations with service and repair facilities. AutoCheck is backed by Experian Automotive and is strongest at wholesale auction data through Experian's ties to Manheim and ADESA, plus salvage-auction feeds — it surfaces auction sale events, loss and damage type, and run-and-drive status, and it is the report used at Manheim auctions. AutoCheck also assigns each vehicle an AutoCheck Score, a 0-100 relative-risk rating that Carfax does not offer.

Neither catches everything. Both depend on reported records and miss events that were never reported — a cash accident with no police or insurance filing leaves no trace in either. One third-party spot-check cited across the industry flagged Carfax at roughly 93% and AutoCheck at roughly 87% of 250 known-salvage VINs; treat that as a cited figure, not a controlled lab result. For the salvage and flood trail specifically, see how to check if a used car has flood or salvage history.

Is Bumper legit, and how does its subscription work?

Bumper is a legitimate subscription vehicle-history service that aggregates public records and third-party partner data — accident, title, theft, salvage, sales-listing, recall and market-value records — rather than operating a Carfax-scale proprietary service network. Its FAQ states the membership is $19.99 per 30-day billing period, or $12.99 per period when you commit to three billing periods ($38.97 total), as of 2026-09-20. Multiple review sites report a $1 seven-day introductory trial that auto-renews into the monthly membership, though that trial is not stated on the FAQ page, and Bumper's own homepage separately quotes "Plans start at $27.99 per month" — so confirm the live figure before you sign up.

The practical caution is the model, not the legitimacy: Bumper is subscription-only, with no true single-report purchase, and it auto-renews. If you are checking one car, a one-off report from another provider avoids a recurring charge; if you are shopping several, the membership can pay off — provided you cancel when done.

Where Zilocar fits, and where it doesn't

Zilocar is a mid-priced alternative built around history depth: for $39.50 a single report (Zilocar pricing, as of 2026-09-20) surfaces accident and damage records (location, type, severity, run-and-drive and airbag-deployment status), an odometer history with a possible-rollback flag, an NICB theft check, junk and salvage auction/total-loss records, ownership history, and its standout feature — sales-listing history, showing past and current listings with prices, mileage and days-on-market. It also lists recall presence, specs, NHTSA and IIHS safety ratings, and a market valuation. A subscription runs $29.50 for the first month, then $59.75 per month for 20 reports a month.

Be clear on the honest boundaries. Zilocar is not the lowest-priced single report — AutoCheck's is less — and it does not confirm whether a recall was remedied, flag NHTSA investigations, show dealer firmware status, or issue the legal title brand. It shows the junk/salvage auction record that precedes a brand, not the brand itself. Its own sample page notes that "damage reporting is not comprehensive" and recommends an independent inspection. The sales-listing angle is where it separates from rivals; how to use it as leverage is covered in how to check a used car's listing and price history before buying.

Which report should you use?

Match the report to the decision, not the brand name:

  • Buying one car and want the deepest service history: Carfax, if the seller does not already provide a free one.
  • Lowest-priced one-off report, or you weight auction/salvage data: AutoCheck — lowest-priced single report today and strongest on wholesale and salvage-auction records, with the AutoCheck Score.
  • Shopping several cars at once: a subscription — Bumper (a reported $19.99/30-day) or Zilocar ($29.50 first month, then $59.75/month for 20 reports) — spreads the cost across many VINs.
  • You care most about listing/price history and ownership trail under Carfax's single price: Zilocar.
  • You only need recalls, theft, or the legal title brand: skip the paid reports and use the free authoritative tools below.

What to verify by VIN before you pay

Whichever report you buy, confirm these six things — and start every one with the authoritative free source:

  1. Open recalls — check NHTSA's free VIN lookup at nhtsa.gov/recalls first; it is the authority on whether a recall is open and, with the dealer, whether it was actually fixed.
  2. Legal title brand — pull a NMVTIS report via vehiclehistory.gov; it, not any commercial report, is the system of record for salvage, rebuilt, junk and flood brands.
  3. Reported theft and total-loss — run the free NICB VINCheck, then confirm against a paid report.
  4. Accident and airbag-deployment history — a prior deployment signals a serious impact and possible structural repair.
  5. Odometer trail — compare readings across records for a rollback flag.
  6. Ownership and listing history — a car that bounced across state lines or was relisted repeatedly warrants extra scrutiny.

This is also the point in the process where a paid history report earns its cost: a Zilocar VIN report is one option that screens for recall presence and pulls accident, airbag-deployment, salvage/junk-auction, odometer, theft, ownership and sales-listing history in one place — run it alongside NHTSA and NMVTIS, not instead of them.

What a VIN check can and can't tell you here

Every report on this page — Carfax, AutoCheck, Bumper and Zilocar — is built on reported records, so all of them share the same hard limits. They can surface open recalls by presence, accident and damage records, odometer readings, theft flags, auction and total-loss events, ownership history and (for some) listing history. They cannot prove a recall was remedied or flashed, track an NHTSA investigation (PE/EA), show per-unit dealer firmware, or catch an accident or salvage event that was never reported to any database.

Is AutoCheck as good as Carfax? They lead in different areas. Both are used in the trade, but AutoCheck is the report tied to wholesale auctions — it is used at Manheim, and its Experian data draws on Manheim and ADESA auction feeds. Many franchised dealers show buyers a free Carfax on their listings. Which a dealer provides free depends on the dealer, not the car.

Is Bumper legit, and how does its subscription work? Bumper is a subscription vehicle-history service. Can I get a free vehicle history report or free VIN check? Partly.
