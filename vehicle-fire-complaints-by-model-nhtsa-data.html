Kia and Hyundai Are 11% of NHTSA Complaints and 26% of the Fire Complaints

Every "worst vehicles" list built on raw complaint counts is really a bestseller list. The Ford F-150 tops those tables because there are more F-150s, not because it is the worst vehicle.

So this study does not count complaints. It asks a different question, one that needs no sales figures at all: when owners of a given vehicle complain to NHTSA, what are they complaining about? Specifically, what share of a model-year's complaints describe a fire.

That ratio is immune to the volume artifact. A vehicle that sells in tiny numbers and one that sells in millions can be compared directly.

Key findings

  • Across the 2015 to 2023 model years, 1.41% of NHTSA complaints describe a fire. That is

the baseline every figure below is measured against.

  • Kia complaints are 3.09x as likely to describe a fire as the average vehicle's (4.36%,

from 925 fire complaints out of 21,205). BMW is second at 2.64x, Hyundai fourth at 1.66x.

  • **Kia and Hyundai together are 11.3% of all complaints in the cohort but 26.2% of all the

fire complaints.** Their combined fire share is 3.27%, or 2.32x baseline.

  • The single worst model-year is the 2015 Toyota RAV4 at 15.68%: 61 of its 389 complaints

describe a fire, eleven times the baseline.

  • On crashes the picture inverts. Tesla's complaints are 2.63x as likely to describe a crash

(12.43%, from 2,263 of 18,206), with Toyota second at 9.54%. The 2015 Toyota Corolla is the worst single model-year at 34.78%.

What this measures, and what it does not

This is the part most coverage of complaint data gets wrong, so it is stated plainly.

This measures the composition of a vehicle's complaints, not its risk. A 9.33% fire share for the 2015 Kia Soul means that of the complaints owners filed about that vehicle, 9.33% described a fire. It does not mean 9.33% of 2015 Kia Souls caught fire, and it does not support a claim that any vehicle here is "X times more likely to catch fire."

NHTSA complaints are unverified consumer reports. They are subject to publicity effects: a widely reported recall drives more owners to file, which can move a vehicle's numbers in either direction. Treat this as a map of where owner-reported fire experience concentrates, and a prompt for further reporting, not as an incidence rate.

Toyota and Tesla dominating a crash-share table is consistent with complaint clusters around driver-assistance behaviour rather than around structural safety, and the concentration in specific model-years rather than across a marque supports that reading. It is a lead worth reporting out, not a conclusion this data can close.

Method

  • Source: NHTSA ODI consumer complaints, the agency's full public flat file, published by the

National Highway Traffic Safety Administration at static.nhtsa.gov/odi/ffdd/cmpl/FLAT_CMPL.zip. The file layout and field definitions are documented in NHTSA's flat file specification. Anyone can download it and reproduce every number below.

  • Scale: 2,228,766 rows scanned, 1,052,748 retained after dropping test records (14,750) and

duplicate submissions (13,317). Complaints from filing year 2015 onward.

  • Cohort: 2015 to 2023 model years, 917 model-years, 408,941 complaints. Model-years before

2015 are excluded deliberately. Their complaints arrive years after sale and skew hard toward dramatic events: the 2002 Pontiac Grand Prix computes to a 49% fire share on 116 complaints, which is a selection effect, not a finding.

  • Threshold: a model-year needs at least 100 complaints to be ranked.
  • Casualties: death and injury figures are collected but never headlined. They are

user-entered and unreliable in aggregate. Values at or above 99 are sentinels and single complaints reporting more than 8 casualties are not credible; both are zeroed while the complaint itself is retained. Treat any casualty total as a floor.

Get the data

The full dataset is free to download, use and republish with attribution and a link to this page. No email required:

  • All 917 model-years (CSV) — make, model, model year, complaint count, fire- and crash-flagged counts, injury and death floors, fire share and crash share.
  • Brand rollup, 30 makes (CSV) — with the fire index against the 1.41% baseline and a thin_sample flag on brands below the reliable-sample threshold.

Both files are the exact frozen snapshot these figures were computed from, so every percentage on this page can be recomputed from them: fire share is fire_flagged / complaints, and the fire index is that share divided by the 1.41% cohort baseline.
