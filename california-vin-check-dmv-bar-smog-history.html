California VIN Check: The Free DMV & Smog-History Lookups (and What They Miss)

Direct answer: There is no single free, official, full-history California VIN check. A real one stacks three free government lookups — NHTSA recalls-by-VIN for open safety recalls, the Bureau of Automotive Repair (BAR) smog-check history for a dated odometer log, and a DMV REG 31 verification that the car matches its paperwork — then a paid NMVTIS report for the legal title brand and accident history. Each answers one narrow question.

California VIN check: the key facts

  • No single free full report. California has no official free lookup that returns title, accidents, recalls and mileage together. "California VIN check" means combining free government tools plus a paid report.
  • NHTSA recalls-by-VIN (nhtsa.gov/recalls): free; shows open/unrepaired safety recalls for that VIN and whether a remedy is available; does not list already-repaired recalls.
  • BAR smog-check history (bar.ca.gov/inspection): free, by VIN or plate; returns each test's date, pass/fail, test type, station, and the odometer reading taken at that test — a free, dated mileage log. Shows nothing about accidents, title or ownership. (smogcheck.ca.gov now redirects to bar.ca.gov.)
  • DMV REG 31 VIN verification: a physical inspection confirming VIN, engine number, make, model and odometer match the paperwork; required to title an out-of-state car in California. Not a history report.
  • REG 31 verifiers: DMV staff; CHP and other peace officers; auto-club (e.g. AAA) registration staff; DMV-licensed vehicle verifiers. Licensed verifiers cannot do revived salvage, revived junk, SPCNS or motorcycles lacking a California record — those go to DMV/CHP.
  • NMVTIS (vehiclehistory.gov to vehiclehistory.bja.ojp.gov): the only congressionally mandated national title-brand system. Consumer reports are paid, only through DOJ-approved providers. Carfax, Experian and DMVDesk are not available as NMVTIS consumer sources (dealer/commercial only).

The single most useful thing to understand is that a "California VIN check" is not one lookup but four layers, each authoritative for one thing and blind to the rest. Three are free government tools; the fourth is a paid report that fills the accident, salvage and ownership gap. The table below is the page's reason to exist (all verified 2026-10-02).

How do I check a California car for open safety recalls by VIN?

Start with the free federal tool, because it is the authority for per-VIN recall status. Open nhtsa.gov/recalls, enter the 17-character VIN, and NHTSA pulls that specific vehicle's manufacturer data. It shows any open, unrepaired safety recall and whether the remedy is available — flagging "Recall incomplete, Remedy not yet available" in red — or "0 Unrepaired recalls associated with this VIN" when the car is clean. One scope limit worth knowing before you trust a clean result on an older car: NHTSA's VIN search covers safety recalls conducted in the last 15 calendar years, not the whole life of the vehicle, so a recall campaign older than that will not appear even if it was never repaired.

The catch to read carefully: the tool shows what is still open, not what was already fixed. It does not list recalls that have been completed, and it does not flag NHTSA investigations (the preliminary and engineering analyses that precede a recall). So an open recall here is a hard signal to act on; a clean result means nothing is currently outstanding, not that the car was never recalled. This is the same per-VIN recall logic covered in our guide to how to check a used car for open recalls before buying.

How do I look up a California smog check history for free (and read the odometer log)?

Use the Bureau of Automotive Repair's free public lookup at bar.ca.gov/inspection, searching by VIN (recommended) or license plate. (The older smogcheck.ca.gov and bar.ca.gov/pubwebquery addresses are retired; both now dead-end, so use bar.ca.gov/inspection. Records are updated daily.) The returned Vehicle Test History report lists, for each inspection: the test date, pass/fail result, the test type (OIS or BAR-97), the testing station's name and location, and the odometer reading taken at that test.

That odometer field is the quietly powerful part. BAR's Smog Check Manual requires inspectors to enter the odometer "as displayed" at every test — no estimating or converting — so the reading is captured and dated at each inspection. A car tested every year or two for a decade therefore leaves a free, dated sequence of mileage readings. That is the closest thing California offers to a free odometer log, and it is the one free public California record carrying dated mileage — which makes it a direct cross-check for how to check if a used car odometer was rolled back: if a paid report or the seller's claimed mileage runs backward against the BAR sequence, you have caught a rollback. BAR now hides the VIN and plate on the returned report for privacy, but the test rows remain. The hard limit: the smog history is emissions results and mileage only. It says nothing about accidents, title, liens or ownership.

What does a California DMV VIN verification (REG 31) actually check, and do I need one?

A DMV VIN verification uses Form REG 31, "Verification of Vehicle," and it is a physical inspection, not a data report. A verifier looks at the car and records the VIN, license plate, engine number, make/model and odometer reading, confirming the vehicle in front of you matches its paperwork. It is required when you title and register an out-of-state (nonresident) vehicle in California, and in other cases where the DMV has no existing California record of the car.

Understand what it does and does not prove. REG 31 proves identity — that this is the car the documents describe and that the VIN has not been swapped or cloned. It tells you nothing about the car's accident history, open recalls or prior title brands; those live in the other three layers. If you are bringing a car in from another state, you will need this verification to register, and you should still pull NHTSA, BAR and an NMVTIS report separately.

Who can perform a REG 31 VIN verification in California?

Four categories of people are authorized to complete a REG 31: DMV employees; CHP and other peace officers (including military police); employees of auto clubs such as AAA that provide registration services; and persons licensed by the DMV as vehicle verifiers. For a straightforward out-of-state car, a DMV field office (as part of the registration transaction) or a AAA branch that offers registration services is the usual route.

There is an important exception. A licensed vehicle verifier cannot verify a revived salvage vehicle, a revived junk vehicle, a specially constructed (SPCNS) vehicle, or a motorcycle whose California record is unavailable. Those must be verified by the DMV or CHP. On the fee: no current primary DMV page quotes a standalone dollar figure for the verification itself — at a field office it is part of the registration transaction, while a private licensed verifier sets their own fee. Don't assume a published price; confirm it with whoever performs the check.

How do I check if a California car has a salvage, junk or flood title?

The legal title brand does not live in any free California emissions or verification tool — it lives in NMVTIS, the National Motor Vehicle Title Information System run under the U.S. DOJ Bureau of Justice Assistance. NMVTIS is the only congressionally mandated national system that consolidates title brands (salvage, junk, flood, rebuilt), total-loss and junk/salvage records, odometer readings and the states a car has been titled in. Access the consumer portal at vehiclehistory.gov (it redirects to vehiclehistory.bja.ojp.gov).

Two things to know before you buy. First, NMVTIS consumer reports are paid, sold only through DOJ-approved providers, and the portal splits its list into providers serving both commercial and public customers and providers serving commercial customers only — buy from the first group. Second, and counterintuitively, you cannot buy an NMVTIS consumer report from Carfax or Experian; those are approved for dealer/commercial use only, not as consumer NMVTIS sources. If you want to understand what each brand means for price, insurance and safety before you spend, our explainers on what is a salvage title and rebuilt vs salvage title break down the distinctions, and how to check if a used car has flood or salvage history covers the cross-state title-washing gap that NMVTIS exists to close.

Who should run which lookup

Buying any used California car: run the three free tools first — NHTSA recalls-by-VIN, the BAR smog history (for the odometer log), and, if the car is coming from out of state, line up a REG 31 verification for registration. They cost nothing and cover recalls, mileage and identity.

Confirming the legal title: you need NMVTIS through an approved provider. No free California lookup and no Carfax/Experian consumer report gives you the legal brand.

Wanting accident, salvage-auction, ownership and listing history: you need a paid commercial VIN report. The free California tools show none of it.

What to verify by VIN before you pay

  1. Open safety recalls — run nhtsa.gov/recalls; an open, unrepaired recall is stop-and-confirm, and a clean result only means nothing is currently outstanding.
  2. Smog + odometer trail — pull the BAR Vehicle Test History and read the odometer readings in date order; a reading that runs backward is a rollback flag.
  3. Legal title brand — check NMVTIS through an approved provider for salvage, junk, flood or rebuilt; don't rely on the seller's state title alone.
  4. Accident, salvage-auction and ownership history — pull a paid report; the free California tools show none of this.
  5. VIN identity — for an out-of-state car, get the REG 31 verification; it confirms the VIN/engine/odometer match the paperwork and is required to register.
  6. Theft / total-loss flags — run the free NICB VINCheck at vinCheck.nicb.org as a second, independent screen.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a free California VIN check that shows the full history? No. California has no single free official full-history VIN report. A real California VIN check stacks three free government lookups — NHTSA recalls-by-VIN, the BAR smog-check history, and a DMV REG 31 VIN verification — then a paid NMVTIS-based report for title brands and accident/odometer history. Each free source answers one narrow question.

How do I look up a car's smog check history in California for free? Use the Bureau of Automotive Repair's public Vehicle Test History lookup at bar.ca.gov/inspection. It is free and searches by VIN or license plate. It returns each smog test's date, pass/fail result, test type, station, and the odometer reading taken at that test — but nothing about accidents, title or ownership.

Does the California smog check history show the odometer at each test? Yes. BAR's Smog Check Manual requires inspectors to enter the odometer reading at every test, as displayed. The public Vehicle Test History report lists that reading per test, so a car tested every year or two yields a free, dated sequence of mileage readings — the closest thing California has to a free odometer log.

What does a California DMV VIN verification (REG 31) check, and do I need one? Form REG 31 is a physical inspection that confirms the car's VIN, engine number, make, model and odometer match its paperwork. It is required to title an out-of-state vehicle in California. It is not a history report — it proves the car in front of you matches its documents, nothing about accidents, recalls or prior titles.

Who can do a REG 31 VIN verification in California? DMV employees; CHP and other peace officers; employees of auto clubs such as AAA that offer registration services; and DMV-licensed vehicle verifiers. Licensed verifiers cannot verify revived salvage, revived junk, specially constructed (SPCNS) vehicles, or motorcycles with no California record — those must go to DMV or CHP.

Does a smog check or DMV lookup prove a California car has a clean title? No. The BAR smog history shows emissions results and mileage only; the DMV REG 31 confirms the car matches its paperwork. Neither shows title brands, liens or accidents. The legal title brand lives in NMVTIS and on the state DMV title — not in any free California emissions or verification record.

Does the NHTSA recall tool tell me if the recall was actually fixed? The free NHTSA recalls-by-VIN tool shows open, unrepaired safety recalls for that VIN and whether a remedy is available. It does not list recalls already repaired. A paid VIN report shows recall presence and count but not remedy status, so confirm an open recall was completed with NHTSA's tool and the franchise dealer.

Why can't I buy a Carfax report through the official NMVTIS site? Because the NMVTIS consumer portal only sells reports through DOJ-approved consumer providers, and NMVTIS states plainly that consumers cannot receive NMVTIS reports from Carfax, DMVDesk or Experian — those are commercial-only sources. Use one of the providers listed under the portal's “both Commercial & Public Customers” heading — those are the ones an individual can buy from.
