What NHTSA Complaints Reveal About the 2021-2024 Ford Bronco Before the 565K Fire Recall

Ford recalled 565,691 body-on-frame 2021-2026 Bronco and Bronco Raptor SUVs on July 24, 2026 (NHTSA campaign 26V468000) for an engine-compartment wiring harness that can chafe, short, and ignite. In NHTSA's complaint database for 2021-2024 Broncos, 33 of 850 complaints described a fire or thermal event — all filed before the recall. A VIN check surfaces recall presence plus fire, salvage, and odometer history.

Key facts

  • Recall: NHTSA campaign 26V468000 / Ford recall 26S55, made public around July 24, 2026; VINs searchable on NHTSA.gov since July 23, 2026.
  • Population: 565,691 vehicles — 2021-2026 Ford Bronco and Bronco Raptor, body-on-frame only. Production Sep 23, 2020 to Jun 28, 2026. Ford estimates 1% carry the defect.
  • Defect: Relative movement plus insufficient abrasion protection lets the engine-compartment wiring harness chafe through; exposed circuits short to ground and arc, and heat or spark can ignite flammable components, with "flames coming from the passenger side engine compartment area."
  • Warning signs: Smoke through the air vents or an instrument-cluster warning message before flames.
  • Documented incidents: 15 fires between Oct 15, 2024 and Jun 20, 2026 — 7 Bronco Raptor, 8 base Bronco. Ford reports no accidents or injuries tied to the defect.
  • Remedy: Dealer installs convolute sheathing/abrasion tape over the harness, free. Not an over-the-air update. Owner letters mailed beginning Aug 24, 2026.
  • Supplier: Versigent (Guadalupe, Mexico).

How many fire complaints did owners file before the recall?

In NHTSA's ODI complaints database for the 2021-2024 Ford Bronco, 33 of 850 complaints described a fire, flame, smoke, burn, or melt event, and 11 carried NHTSA's binary fire flag. All 33 were filed before recall 26V468 was received by NHTSA on July 20, 2026. By model year the fire/thermal complaints break out as 2021: 13, 2022: 8, 2023: 7, 2024: 5.

The count is a superset, not a harness-confirmed tally. Some 2026 fire complaints reference other Bronco recalls (such as 26V236, a driveshaft/transfer-case campaign), so not every one stems from the harness. Ford's own harness-attributable figure is 15 incidents across the full 2021-2026 population. Read the 33 as the broad owner-reported fire signal that accumulated ahead of the recall, and the 15 as Ford's confirmed harness subset.

Which model years and trims cluster hottest?

The 2021 Bronco is the hottest cluster in the complaint data: 13 of the 33 fire/thermal complaints, and the highest engine-component share at 67 of its 218 complaints (31%). That is consistent with it being the earliest-built, highest-mileage, longest-exposed cohort. Electrical-system complaints run across all four years (28 in 2021, 35 in 2022, 22 in 2023, 16 in 2024; 101 total).

Ford's filing tells a different-looking story on trim. Of the 15 documented harness fires, 7 were Bronco Raptors and 8 were base Broncos — meaning the Raptor is massively over-represented, because it is only a small slice of production. Ford attributes this to greater harness chafing from the Raptor's suspension travel and underhood vibration. Note the data limits: NHTSA's complaint API does not tag Raptor separately from base Bronco, so the 7/8 split comes only from Ford's Part 573, not the complaint set.

Is this the same as the Bronco Sport / Escape recall?

No. Recall 26V468 covers 565,691 body-on-frame Ford Bronco and Bronco Raptor SUVs for the wiring-harness fire. The Bronco Sport and Escape recall — roughly 694,000 unibody vehicles — is a separate campaign for a cracked fuel injector that can leak onto an ignition source. Different vehicles, different mechanism, different campaign. It is also distinct from the 2024 recall 24V635000 (2021-2022 Bronco and F-150 2.7L EcoBoost intake-valve engine failure), which is mechanical rather than a fire risk.

How to check a specific Bronco by VIN

Start with the authoritative source: enter the VIN at NHTSA.gov/recalls, the free federal tool, which now flags 26V468 for in-scope trucks and shows any open recall's presence. To confirm the repair itself — whether a dealer actually installed the harness sheathing — contact a Ford dealer, who can check remedy status through Ford's OASIS system. Recall lookups show whether a fix is available, not whether it was performed.

A Zilocar VIN check is a useful second screen alongside NHTSA. It surfaces the same open-recall presence signal and, more usefully for a used buyer, pulls the vehicle's history: accident and damage records with location, severity, and airbag-deployment status; junk and salvage auction records; an odometer/rollback check; theft records (NICB); ownership history; and past and current sales-listing history with prices, mileage, and days on market. That combination is what catches a Bronco that burned to a total loss, was rebuilt, and is being flipped.

What a VIN check can and can't tell you here

A VIN check is strong on history and recall presence, and deliberately silent on remedy and investigation status. Use it to screen, then cede confirmation to NHTSA and the dealer.

Frequently asked questions

How many fire complaints did owners file on the 2021-2024 Ford Bronco before the recall? In NHTSA's ODI database (2021-2024 model years, pulled July 26, 2026), 33 of 850 complaints described a fire, flame, smoke, burn, or melt event, and 11 carried NHTSA's binary fire flag. All 33 were filed before recall 26V468 landed on July 20, 2026.

Which Bronco model year is most at risk for an engine-bay fire? By complaint volume, the 2021 Bronco is the hottest cluster: 13 of the 33 fire/thermal complaints, plus the highest engine-component share (67 of 218 complaints, 31%). It is the earliest-built, highest-mileage cohort. Ford's own 15-incident tally, however, shows the Bronco Raptor over-represented.

Is the Bronco Raptor worse than the base Bronco for the fire risk? Is this the same as the Bronco Sport and Escape fire recall? No. Recall 26V468 covers 565,691 body-on-frame Ford Bronco and Bronco Raptor SUVs for a wiring-harness fire. What are the early warning signs of the wiring-harness fire? Ford's filing describes smoke coming through the air vents or a warning message in the instrument cluster before flames appear from the passenger-side engine compartment. On a test drive, note any burning smell, smoke, or cluster warning lights and have a mechanic inspect the engine-bay harness.

Does a VIN check show whether the Bronco recall was already fixed? No. A VIN or recall check shows whether an open recall like 26V468 is present, not whether the sheathing repair was performed. Open-versus-closed remedy status stays with NHTSA's VIN tool and a Ford dealer via OASIS. Confirm the repair there before buying.

Could a used Bronco that already caught fire be resold? Yes. The recall documents 15 fires, several total losses. A burned Bronco can be bought at salvage auction, rebuilt, and resold. A VIN history can surface junk and salvage auction records, fire/burn-type damage, airbag deployment, and odometer rollbacks that a title wash may hide.

What should I check on a used Ford Bronco before buying in 2026? Screen the VIN for open recalls (26V468 and others like the 26V236 driveshaft campaign), then review accident and fire/burn damage, salvage-auction records, odometer consistency, theft records, ownership count, and sales-listing history. Confirm any recall repair at a Ford dealer or NHTSA.gov.

Before you buy a used Bronco

If you are shopping a 2021-2024 Bronco, run the VIN through NHTSA.gov first for open-recall presence, and pair it with a Zilocar VIN check to see accident and airbag-deployment records, salvage and junk-auction history, odometer and theft records, ownership, and sales-listing history in one place. Zilocar screens for recall presence and surfaces history; it does not confirm a recall was remedied, track NHTSA investigations, or show the legal title brand — take the repair confirmation to a Ford dealer or NHTSA.
