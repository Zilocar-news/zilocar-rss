2016-2019 Ford Explorer Roof-Rail Cover Recall (26V448): What Used Buyers Should Check by VIN

On Jul 14, 2026, Ford filed NHTSA recall 26V448 (Ford 26S54) covering 288,314 model-year 2016-2019 Explorers whose roof-rail covers can loosen and detach in motion, creating a road hazard for following drivers. The remedy is a free dealer repair adding four push-pins per side; interim owner letters mail Aug 24-28, 2026. A VIN check confirms whether the recall is present, but only a Ford dealer can confirm the repair was completed.

Key facts

  • NHTSA campaign: 26V448. Ford recall number: 26S54. Filed by Ford Motor Company on Jul 14, 2026.
  • Affected population: 288,314 vehicles, all 2016-2019 Ford Explorer, built Sep 19, 2014 to Mar 3, 2019. Ford estimates 1% contain the defect.
  • Defect: Roof-rail covers (the painted or plated trim caps over the roof rails) can loosen, rattle, show a visible gap, and detach at speed, becoming a road hazard that raises crash risk for trailing vehicles.
  • Trims/scope: Explorers with painted or plated (satin/chrome) covers not already fixed by the four-push-pin method under recall 21V316. Explicitly includes vehicles previously "repaired" under epoxy program 20N11.
  • Remedy (free): Dealers inspect, repair or replace, and add four additional push-pins per side. Ford applies its general reimbursement plan to owners who already paid.
  • Warning signs: increased wind noise, squeaks or rattles, and a visible gap or misalignment between the cover and the roofline.
  • Key dates: VINs searchable Jul 16, 2026; interim owner letters Aug 24-28, 2026; remedy letters Sep 21-25, 2026 (phased as parts become available).
  • Harm reported: Ford is aware of no injuries and one alleged accident, on a vehicle previously repaired with epoxy under 20N11.
  • Supplier: JAC Products, Saline, MI. Ford hotline: 1-866-436-7332. Self-check: nhtsa.gov/recalls.

Is my 2016-2019 Ford Explorer part of the roof-rail recall?

If you own or are buying a 2016-2019 Ford Explorer built between Sep 19, 2014 and Mar 3, 2019, it may be in recall 26V448. The recall covers 288,314 vehicles with painted or plated roof-rail covers that were not already secured by the four-push-pin method under the earlier 21V316 recall. The only reliable way to know is to enter the specific VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls, which became searchable for this campaign on Jul 16, 2026.

Because the population is defined by build date and prior repair history rather than a simple model-year cutoff, two identical-looking Explorers can have different recall status. That makes a per-VIN check, rather than a model-year assumption, the right move.

What exactly is the defect, and how dangerous is it?

The roof-rail covers are the trim caps that run along the top of each roof rail. Over time, with heat cycles, wear, and sun exposure, their retention clips can loosen. The cover may first rattle or show a gap, then separate from the vehicle at speed. A detached cover can land in the roadway and become debris that increases crash risk for drivers behind.

Ford estimates 1% of the recalled vehicles contain the defect, which is an estimate on the Part 573 form rather than a measured failure rate. Ford reports no injuries and one alleged, unconfirmed accident tied to a detaching cover on a vehicle that had earlier received the epoxy repair. The available reporting does not describe a Do Not Drive or Park Outside order for this recall.

How is this different from the older 2021 roof-rail recall (21V316)?

This is a distinct, net-new recall, not a re-issue. The 2016-2019 Explorer has a layered roof-rail history, and 26V448 exists because two earlier fixes did not fully hold. Vehicles corrected under the 2021 four-push-pin recall (21V316) were found to be performing as intended and are not in scope. Vehicles fixed with the earlier epoxy method, or never fixed at all, are the ones being recalled now.

The chronology: Ford launched epoxy program 20N11 in November 2020, then moved to a four-push-pin mechanical fix under 21V316 in April 2021 after field data showed rising detachments. In March 2026, NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation flagged 46 owner questionnaires alleging looseness, including on vehicles already repaired under both prior programs. Ford's analysis found that plated covers in original condition and epoxy-repaired covers kept degrading, while push-pin-fixed covers held. Ford approved the new field action on Jul 7, 2026.

My used Explorer's records say the roof rail was already repaired. Could it still be recalled?

Yes. This is the single most important caveat for used buyers. Recall 26V448 deliberately re-captures Explorers that were previously "repaired" under epoxy program 20N11, because that adhesive fix proved technician-sensitive and unreliable. So a history report or service record showing a prior roof-rail repair does not mean the vehicle is out of scope.

Only a repair done with the four-push-pin method under 21V316 removes a vehicle from this recall. Since you usually cannot tell which method was used from a history report alone, confirm the current open-recall status by VIN with NHTSA or a Ford dealer.

Is the roof-rail recall the same as Ford's roll-away recall?

No. These are two separate July 2026 recalls affecting different model years, and confusing them is easy because both involve the Explorer name. The roof-rail recall (26V448) covers 2016-2019 Explorers. The roll-away park-pawl recall (NHTSA 26V402 / Ford 26S48) is a 741,195-vehicle action covering 2020-2021 Explorers and other models, where the transmission park pawl may engage in motion or fail to hold in Park.

A 2016-2019 Explorer is in the roof-rail recall and is not in the park-pawl recall. The roughly 560,000 Explorer/Expedition figure cited in some July 2026 news wrap-ups refers to the 2020-2021 Explorer (313,147) plus 2018-2021 Expedition (246,202) subset of 26V402, not to the 2016-2019 roof-rail vehicles.

How much does the repair cost, and when can I get it done?

The repair is free. Dealers inspect, repair or replace the roof-rail cover, and add four additional push-pins per side to secure it. Owners who already paid out of pocket for a related repair can seek reimbursement under Ford's general reimbursement plan.

On timing, Ford notified dealers and made VINs searchable on Jul 16, 2026. Interim owner letters mail Aug 24-28, 2026, and remedy letters follow Sep 21-25, 2026 on a phased basis as parts become available. Owners can call Ford at 1-866-436-7332 in the meantime.

What other problems should I check on a used 2016-2019 Explorer?

Beyond this roof-rail recall, a used 2016-2019 Explorer warrants a full per-VIN recall screen, since this generation has been subject to multiple separate actions over the years. Always run the exact VIN rather than relying on the model year, because open-recall status varies vehicle by vehicle.

Just as important is the vehicle's condition history. This is a high-volume SUV, so examples range from well-kept to hard-used. Prioritize accident and damage records (roof or hail damage and a torn-off cover can surface here), salvage or junk auction records, an odometer/rollback check, ownership history, and past sales-listing history to gauge how the vehicle has been priced and turned over.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to drive or buy a 2016-2019 Explorer with this open recall? Ford reports no injuries and one alleged, unconfirmed accident, and no Do Not Drive order was reported for this recall. The repair is free and available at dealers. Buying one is reasonable if you plan to have the recall completed; confirm open status by VIN first.

Does it matter whether my Explorer has painted or chrome roof rails? Both are in scope. The recall covers painted covers (Black Painted, Silver Painted, Absolute Black) and plated covers (Satin Chrome, Chrome) that were not already fixed with the four-push-pin method. Plated covers were specifically added this time after degrading in original condition.

How do I check my exact VIN? Enter the 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls or Ford Owner Support, or call Ford at 1-866-436-7332. VINs for 26V448 became searchable Jul 16, 2026, so very recent data may still be propagating.

Can a history report prove the recall was fixed? No. History and VIN tools show that a recall exists, not that a particular remedy was performed. Only a Ford dealer can confirm, by VIN, whether the four-push-pin roof-rail repair is complete.

Should I negotiate the price on an Explorer with an open recall? The repair itself is free, so the recall is not a permanent inherited defect. The stronger levers on price are the vehicle's accident history, any salvage-auction records, odometer accuracy, and how it compares to past listing prices for similar mileage.

What history should I pull on a high-mileage used Explorer? Accident and damage records, salvage or junk auction records, an odometer/rollback check, ownership history, and past and current sales-listing history (price, mileage, days on market). Together these tell you far more about a specific Explorer than the model year alone.
