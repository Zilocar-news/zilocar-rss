Window Sticker by VIN: What the Monroney Label Proves About a Used Car (and the Free Way to Pull One)

You can often get a car's original window sticker by VIN for free, but the honest route depends on the brand, not a paid report. The Monroney label is the factory price sheet required by the Automobile Information Disclosure Act (15 U.S.C. 1232): base MSRP, every attached option and its price, the destination charge, the total, the final assembly point, and NCAP safety stars. Ford returns the original PDF free by VIN; NHTSA's free vPIC decoder returns the factory build and often the base MSRP; EPA's fueleconomy.gov returns the mpg figures. None of these, and no history report, is the other half of the question: what happened to the car after it left the lot.

Key facts

  • What it is: The Monroney label is the price-disclosure label the manufacturer must affix to every new passenger car, required by the Automobile Information Disclosure Act of 1958 (15 U.S.C. 1231-1233; penalties up to $1,000 per vehicle under 1233).
  • Legally required fields (15 U.S.C. 1232): make, model and VIN; final assembly point; dealer name and delivery city; base MSRP, each attached option's price, the destination charge, and the total; and NCAP safety stars when assigned.
  • EPA mpg is required separately, by EPA/DOT rules (49 CFR 575.401 and 40 CFR Part 600), not the Monroney Act - same sticker, different legal basis.
  • "Port of entry" is not a required Monroney field; the statute requires the final assembly point and dealer delivery city. Parts origin lives on a separate AALA label (49 CFR Part 583).
  • Free factory routes: Ford/Lincoln original PDF by VIN (portal documented but we could not load it on 2026-10-22); NHTSA vPIC build decode, which carried a "Base Price ($)" on only 3 of 15 VIN patterns we tested; EPA fueleconomy.gov for the mpg numbers.
  • What the sticker cannot tell you: accidents, airbag deployment, odometer rollback, theft, salvage/junk auction records, ownership and listing history - that is the paid-report side.

What is a window sticker (Monroney label) and what does it prove?

A window sticker is the Monroney label - the factory price-disclosure sheet a manufacturer must affix to every new passenger car before it is delivered to a dealer. It proves one thing: how the car was configured and priced on day one. Under 15 U.S.C. 1232 it must list the make, model and VIN; the final assembly point; the dealer and city of delivery; the base MSRP, the price of each attached option not already in the base price, the destination charge, and the total; and NCAP safety star ratings when NHTSA has assigned them.

Named for Senator Mike Monroney, who sponsored the 1958 Act, the label proves the factory build and original pricing - not condition, history, or whether the car still matches that build today. For a used car, the itemized options list is the record you check a seller's claims against. Here is exactly what the statute requires, and where a common assumption goes wrong:

Can I get the original window sticker by VIN, and is it free?

Often, yes, and free - but the route depends on the brand, and no single site serves every manufacturer's original Monroney for free. The cleanest case is Ford and Lincoln: the manufacturer's portal at windowsticker.ford.com is VIN-gated and historically returns the original Monroney PDF free with no login, with coverage skewed to later model years. (We could not load the Ford portal on 2026-10-22 - a proxy/DNS failure on our end, not a confirmed outage - so its exact current behavior is cited from established documentation; mid-1990s and older Fords likely need a dealer.)

Other brands differ. GM (Chevrolet, GMC, Buick, Cadillac) has no public self-serve tool we could confirm; the standard route is a free dealer request by VIN. Toyota/Lexus and Honda/Acura route through owner accounts that generally require sign-up, so they are not a clean anonymous free lookup. Do not assume your brand offers a free self-serve sticker because Ford does. And beware the biggest trap in this search: most third-party "free window sticker by VIN" tools do not serve the original Monroney at all - they serve a reconstruction assembled from the decoded VIN plus EPA fuel-economy data. A rebuilt sticker can only restate what the VIN already said; it cannot confirm an option was actually installed. Treat any "directly from the OEM" claim as a vendor assertion, not a primary source.

Does NHTSA's free vPIC decoder show the same thing as the sticker?

No, but it covers part of it for free. NHTSA's vPIC decoder at vpic.nhtsa.dot.gov is free, needs no login, and returns the manufacturer-reported build from the VIN: make, model, model year, body class, trim, drivetrain, engine, assembly plant, and roughly 141 variables. It sometimes returns a field literally named "Base Price ($)" carrying the base MSRP as the manufacturer reported it. Measure before you rely on it: decoding 15 VIN patterns across 10 makes and model years 2013-2024 on 2026-10-22, the field was populated on 3 of 15 - a 2021 Toyota RAV4 pattern at $26,250, a 2018 Honda Civic at $18,940 and a 2021 Jeep Wrangler at $32,870 - and empty on all five Ford patterns we tried. So vPIC is not priceless, but it is not a price route you can count on either, and least of all on the brand whose own portal is the easiest free option.

What vPIC does not do: reproduce the Monroney PDF, itemize option prices, show the destination charge, show the as-configured total, or print EPA mpg. The "Base Price ($)" field was populated on only 3 of the 15 VIN patterns we decoded, so treat it as a bonus rather than a feature. Treat vPIC as the free factory build decode that sometimes carries the base price, not as the sticker. For the mpg figures, use the EPA/DOE tool at fueleconomy.gov, which reproduces the city/highway/combined numbers on the label for any model year 1984 or newer, searchable by year, make, model and engine (not VIN-gated).

Is "port of entry" on the window sticker?

No. This is the most common Monroney error. 15 U.S.C. 1232 requires the final assembly point (where the car was built) and the city of delivery to the dealer - not a "port of entry." For imports, parts country-of-origin content is disclosed on a separate American Automobile Labeling Act label (49 CFR Part 583), not the Monroney. So the sticker proves where the car was assembled, and a companion AALA label shows parts-origin content.

How do I confirm a used car actually has the options the seller claims?

Pull the factory options record and match it against the car and the listing. The Monroney's itemized option list is the authoritative day-one record of packages and equipment, so if a seller advertises a tow package, a driver-assist bundle or a specific trim, the original sticker (or vPIC's decoded equipment) is where you confirm it. It is the same verification problem as checking any feature set - our guide on how to check what features and options a used car has by VIN covers decoding equipment when the original PDF is not available.

The original MSRP also anchors value, which matters most when a model sells above its original sticker - as we saw in our look at what to check before buying a used hybrid. But the sticker is a starting price, not a current value, and says nothing about what the car has been through since.

What does the window sticker NOT tell you about a used car?

Everything that happened after the car left the dealership. The Monroney is a factory snapshot: it cannot show accidents or airbag deployment, odometer rollback, theft, salvage or junk auction history, how many owners it has had, or its listing and asking-price history. A pristine sticker and a flood-totaled wreck can describe the same VIN years apart. For the post-sale side you need a vehicle history report; for the legal title brand you need the federal NMVTIS system at vehiclehistory.gov and the state DMV - the authorities for salvage, rebuilt, flood and junk classifications. Separately, how to check a used car's price history before buying is an exercise the factory sticker cannot help with.

Who should use which

  • Original sticker, options and MSRP: the manufacturer - Ford/Lincoln self-serve at windowsticker.ford.com, GM via a dealer, Toyota/Lexus and Honda/Acura via an owner account. All free.
  • Factory build and often the base price, instantly and anonymously: NHTSA vPIC. Free, but not the full sticker.
  • Just the EPA mpg numbers: fueleconomy.gov. Free, no VIN required.
  • What happened after sale: a vehicle history report (Carfax, AutoCheck, Bumper, Zilocar, or others) - none of which is the sticker.
  • The legal title brand: the state DMV and NMVTIS (vehiclehistory.gov), not a commercial report, which shows auction records rather than the DMV classification.

What to verify by VIN before you pay

  1. Options match the claim: pull the original sticker or vPIC's decoded equipment and confirm the trim, packages and features advertised.
  2. Original MSRP vs asking price: use the base MSRP as a sanity check against current value and listing history.
  3. EPA mpg: confirm the real figures at fueleconomy.gov, not a seller's number.
  4. Accident/airbag and odometer: run a history check for crash, airbag-deployment and rollback flags the sticker can never show.
  5. Title brand: confirm the legal classification at the state DMV / NMVTIS, not from auction records alone.
  6. Open recalls: check the VIN at NHTSA's free tool and with the dealer for remedy status.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get the original window sticker just from the VIN, and is it free? Sometimes, free, depending on the brand. Ford/Lincoln return the original Monroney PDF free by VIN at windowsticker.ford.com; GM is a free dealer request; Toyota/Lexus and Honda/Acura route through owner accounts that generally require sign-up. No single site serves every brand's original sticker free.

How do I pull a Ford window sticker by VIN? Go to windowsticker.ford.com and enter the 17-character VIN. It is documented to return the original Monroney PDF free with no login, coverage skewed to later model years; we could not load the portal ourselves on 2026-10-22, so that behaviour is documented rather than confirmed on the day. Mid-1990s and older Fords may need a dealer.

Does NHTSA's free vPIC decoder show the same thing as the window sticker? No. vPIC decodes the factory build free and sometimes carries a "Base Price ($)" field with the base MSRP - on 3 of the 15 VIN patterns we tested on 2026-10-22, and on none of five Ford patterns. It does not reproduce the Monroney PDF, itemize option prices, show the destination charge, give the as-equipped total, or print EPA mpg.

Is "port of entry" on the window sticker? No. 15 U.S.C. 1232 requires the final assembly point and the city of delivery to the dealer, not a "port of entry." Imported-parts origin content is a separate American Automobile Labeling Act label (49 CFR Part 583).

Does the window sticker show the original MSRP and every factory option? Yes. 15 U.S.C. 1232(f) requires the base MSRP, the price of each attached option not in the base price, the destination charge, and the total - the itemized list you use to confirm a car has the packages a seller claims.

What does the window sticker NOT tell me about a used car? Everything after it left the lot: accidents and airbag deployment, odometer rollback, theft, salvage/junk auction history, ownership changes, and listing/price history. It is a factory snapshot, not a history.

Can a VIN report tell me if a recall was actually fixed? No. A paid history report, like NHTSA's free tool, shows open recall presence only, not whether the remedy was performed. Confirm remedy status with the dealer using the VIN.
