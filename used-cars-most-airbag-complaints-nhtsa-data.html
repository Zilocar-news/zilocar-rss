Which Used Cars Draw the Most Airbag Complaints — and How Many Say the Bag Didn't Deploy? We Counted the NHTSA Data

TL;DR: We counted NHTSA's ODI airbag complaints (FLAT_CMPL, file dated 2026-09-06, processed 2026-09-08): 156,619 records across 1,873 used make/model combinations. By raw volume the Toyota Corolla (4,713), Honda Accord (4,120) and Ford Fusion (3,537) lead — but those are best-sellers. The finding nobody aggregates: 22.1% of all airbag complaints (34,664) describe a bag that did NOT deploy, and that share swings from 6.6% (Ford Fusion) to 71.8% (Chevrolet S10).

Update — 2026-09-11: NHTSA's counterfeit-inflator toll has climbed: the agency now links the DTN60DB replacement inflators to 11 deaths and 3 severe injuries (up from 8 deaths, 2 injuries and 12 ruptures). The 11th death — on 2026-08-27 in a 2018 Chevrolet Equinox near Dallas — is the first outside the Malibu/Sonata models and the first since NHTSA's April import ban. The Kia EV9 recall (26V545000) is unchanged: 21,290 units, owner letters still mailing from 2026-10-20.

  • Dataset: NHTSA ODI Consumer Complaints flat file (FLAT_CMPL.txt), NHTSA file date 2026-09-06, retrieved and processed 2026-09-08; 2,243,124 total records.
  • Airbag complaints analyzed: 156,619 (COMPDESC contains "AIR BAGS"), across 1,873 make/model combinations.
  • Failure-type split: recall/Takata-process 54,659 (34.9%); did-NOT-deploy 34,664 (22.1%); warning light 33,359 (21.3%); other 27,353 (17.5%); inadvertent/unexpected deployment 6,584 (4.2%).

Which used cars get the most airbag complaints?

By raw complaint volume, the leaders are the Toyota Corolla (4,713), Honda Accord (4,120), Ford Fusion (3,537), Honda Civic (2,933) and Chevrolet Silverado 1500 (2,407). Every model at the top of the raw list is a long-running best-seller, so the count reflects how many cars are on the road as much as any defect. Raw volume is fleet-size biased and should not be read as a per-car risk rate.

Which models have the highest share of 'airbag did not deploy' complaints?

Ranking by the within-model share of non-deployment complaints reverses the board entirely, because the denominator is each model's own airbag-complaint total rather than its fleet size. Among models with at least 300 airbag complaints, the highest non-deployment shares are Chevrolet S10 (71.8%), Chevrolet Blazer (65.8%), Dodge Ram (64.5%), Chevrolet TrailBlazer (58.8%) and Toyota Prius (56.4%) — older or smaller-fleet vehicles that never appear on the raw list. Among high-volume models, the Toyota Camry stands out at 53.1% non-deployment.

What is the news peg — the Kia EV9 recall and counterfeit airbags?

Two events keep airbag safety live for used-car buyers. First, campaign 26V545000: Kia is recalling 21,290 model-year 2025-2026 EV9s because an improperly routed occupant-detection (ODS) bladder tube can be pinched after seat adjustment, so the system may fail to suppress the front-passenger airbag when a child or child seat is present — an FMVSS 208 noncompliance that leaves the bag wrongly armed for a child. Dealers inspect and replace the ODS mat and bladder tube free; owner letters mail from 2026-10-20.

Second, and more relevant to used cars: NHTSA's ongoing warning about dangerous, likely illegally imported replacement inflators made by DTN (marked DTN60DB). NHTSA issued its first compulsory defect recall order in about 20 years on 2026-04-29; as of September 2026 it is aware of 11 drivers killed and 3 severely injured. Most occurred in 2017-2022 Chevrolet Malibu or Hyundai Sonata, but the 11th death — on 2026-08-27, in a 2018 Chevrolet Equinox near Dallas — is the first outside those two nameplates and the first since NHTSA's April import ban, bearing out the agency's warning that the risk is not limited to those makes. The critical caveat: these are aftermarket parts installed after prior crashes, tied to no OEM campaign and no affected-VIN list — so a standard VIN recall lookup will not surface them. (We could not confirm a discrete NHTSA press release dated 2026-09-06; the documented counterfeit-airbag warnings run April through July 2026, and the 2026-09-06 date belongs to the complaint data file itself.)

What a VIN check can and can't tell you here

A VIN check is a genuine but bounded complement to this complaint data. It can surface a car's crash and airbag-deployment history; it cannot certify that the airbags will fire correctly today. Use it to learn history and screen for recalls, then confirm remedy status with a dealer and have any prior-crash airbag physically inspected.

Frequently asked questions

Which used cars get the most airbag complaints filed with NHTSA? By raw volume: Toyota Corolla (4,713), Honda Accord (4,120), Ford Fusion (3,537), Honda Civic (2,933) and Chevrolet Silverado 1500 (2,407). These are all best-sellers, so high counts partly reflect how many were sold, not a per-car defect rate.

Which used cars have the highest share of 'airbag did not deploy' complaints? Among models with at least 300 airbag complaints, the highest non-deployment shares are Chevrolet S10 (71.8%), Chevrolet Blazer (65.8%), Dodge Ram (64.5%), Chevrolet TrailBlazer (58.8%) and Toyota Prius (56.4%). Toyota Camry leads high-volume models at 53.1%.

Is a high raw airbag-complaint count bad, or does it just mean the car sold a lot? Largely the latter. Raw counts track fleet size, so best-sellers dominate. We normalize by each model's own non-deployment share, which is fleet-neutral and reverses the leaderboard — Ford Fusion is 3rd by volume but only 6.6% non-deployment, because its file is mostly Takata recall-process complaints.

Will a free VIN recall check tell me whether a used car's airbags will actually work? No. A VIN recall lookup shows open recall campaigns, not whether the airbags currently function. It also cannot flag a counterfeit or aftermarket inflator installed after a prior crash — those are tied to no VIN and no OEM campaign, so no VIN tool can see them.

Can a VIN check show whether a used car's airbags already deployed in a past crash? Yes — within accident records. A VIN check can surface airbag-deployment status by VIN, i.e. whether a bag has already fired in a prior wreck. That is the prior-crash history NHTSA says to investigate, but it does not prove the current airbags work or that any recall was remedied.

Is the Kia EV9 airbag recall (26V545000) fixed, and how do I check my VIN? Recall 26V545000 covers 21,290 model-year 2025-2026 EV9s; dealers inspect and replace the occupant-detection mat and bladder tube free, with owner letters from 2026-10-20. Check remedy status at nhtsa.gov/recalls by VIN or with a Kia dealer — a history report shows a recall exists, not that it was performed.

Why doesn't an SRS/airbag warning light show up as an open recall? A warning light is a diagnostic symptom, not a recall. In our data, 33,359 airbag complaints (21.3%) describe a warning light with no defect campaign attached. It signals the SRS system has logged a fault that needs a scan-tool diagnosis, independent of any recall status.

Method and its limits

Failure types are classified by regex on free-text narratives, so they are approximate; non-deployment counts are conservative (only explicit negation-plus-deploy phrasing is counted), so true shares may be modestly higher. Death and injury figures are complainant self-reported and unverified by NHTSA, and include duplicate filings; treat per-model death tallies as directional. Models are aggregated across all model years exactly as NHTSA records them (name variants like "RAM 1500" and "RAM" are not merged). We did not compute a literal complaints-per-100,000-registered-vehicles rate — that requires proprietary registration data — so the within-model non-deployment share is our fleet-neutral substitute, stated plainly here rather than presented as a per-capita rate.
