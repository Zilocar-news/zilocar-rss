Used Cars With the Most Suspension Complaints: Ford's Super Duty Leads the NHTSA Data on Volume and Share

TL;DR: We counted every suspension complaint in NHTSA's consumer-complaint files filed since 2015 — 31,090 of 1,099,513 reports — and ranked used models two ways. The Ford F-250 Super Duty leads on raw volume with 1,806 complaints and ranks second on share at 27.4%, so the same truck tops one ranking and nearly tops the other. These are consumer-filed reports, not verified defects.

Key facts

  • Data analyzed: 1,099,513 NHTSA ODI consumer complaints filed 2015-01-01 through 2026-09-03; 31,090 of them (2.83%) coded as SUSPENSION across 6,593 make/model groups.
  • Raw-volume leader: Ford F-250 Super Duty, 1,806 suspension complaints — the highest of any model, and nearly double the next nameplate.
  • Share leader (500+ total complaints): Ford Windstar, 37.2% (474 of 1,273) — a discontinued 1995-2003 minivan, not a car most people are shopping.
  • The two rankings mostly agree. Unlike our airbag analysis, there is no clean flip here: the F-250 Super Duty is 1st on volume and 2nd on share, and four of the top six share positions are heavy-duty Ford pickups.
  • Reversal check run: NHTSA files the same truck under both F-250 and F-250 SD. Merging them raises the family to 2,238 complaints (26.6% share) and the leader does not change.
  • Caveat: NHTSA complaints are unverified consumer reports with no sales denominator, so this is a count, never a failure rate.

Which used cars have the most suspension complaints?

By raw volume, the Ford F-250 Super Duty tops the list with 1,806 suspension complaints, followed by the Jeep Wrangler (1,105), Toyota Tacoma (979), Tesla Model S (730), and Ford F-350 Super Duty (724). These are the models that generated the largest absolute number of suspension reports filed with NHTSA between 2015 and September 2026.

High-volume nameplates have a structural advantage in a raw count: a model that sells in the millions and draws many complaints of every kind will accumulate more suspension reports too. The Ford Escape is the clearest example — 25,209 total complaints, the most of any model on this list, but only 2.6% of them suspension. Does the ranking change when you correct for complaint volume?

Less than you might expect, and that is the finding. When we rank by suspension complaints as a share of each model's total complaints — limited to models with at least 500 complaints, so small-sample models cannot dominate — the discontinued Ford Windstar comes first at 37.2%. But the F-250 Super Duty is second at 27.4%, and heavy-duty Ford pickups take four of the top six places. The truck that leads the raw count is also near the top of the corrected one.

Method

Source files. NHTSA publishes its ODI consumer-complaint data as period files at https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/ffdd/cmpl/. We concatenated the three that cover filings from 2015 onward: COMPLAINTS_RECEIVED_2015-2019.zip, COMPLAINTS_RECEIVED_2020-2024.zip and COMPLAINTS_RECEIVED_2025-2026.zip, all retrieved 2026-09-09. Each is tab-delimited with 51 fields and no header row.

A note on which file we used, because it matters. NHTSA also publishes a single combined download, FLAT_CMPL.zip. On the day we ran this analysis that combined file was serving a regressed extract: 1,482,744 rows whose filing dates stop at 2018-07-14, despite a 2026-09-08 timestamp, and despite the same download having carried 2,243,124 rows through 2026 the day before. We verified the archive was intact (CRC check passed), so this is what NHTSA was serving, not a truncated download on our side. Anyone reproducing a "we counted the NHTSA complaints" study from FLAT_CMPL.zip around this date will get a ranking that silently omits eight years of filings. The period files are complete and are what we used.

Universe. 1,099,513 complaint records with a filing date (LDATE) of 2015-01-01 or later, running through 2026-09-03. No duplicate complaint IDs were found across the three files.

Filter. A complaint counts as suspension when its component description (COMPDESC) begins with SUSPENSION. That yields 31,090 records, 2.83% of the universe. NHTSA's coding is coarse here: 96.4% of those rows (29,957) carry the bare label SUSPENSION with no sub-part, so this file cannot reliably separate coil springs from control arms, ball joints or struts. The remaining 3.6% break down into SUSPENSION:REAR (331), SUSPENSION:FRONT:WHEEL BEARING (126), SUSPENSION:FRONT (97), SUSPENSION:FRONT:CONTROL ARM (75) and a long tail.

Aggregation. Grouped by MAKETXT and MODELTXT across all model years — 6,593 groups, of which 1,485 have at least one suspension complaint. Ranked twice: by raw suspension count, and by suspension share of that model's total complaints with a 500-complaint inclusion floor (290 models clear it).

Reversal check, and its result. NHTSA's MODELTXT is free text, so one nameplate can occupy several strings and a naive model ranking can be wrong. We swept the top 40 models for same-make prefix pairs. The material one is Ford's heavy-duty line, filed under both F-250 (432 suspension of 1,837) and F-250 SD (1,806 of 6,581), with nine overlapping model years. Merging the family gives 2,238 suspension complaints of 8,418 (26.6%), which increases the raw-volume lead and moves the share from 2nd to 2nd. The same merge for the F-350 line gives 950 of 4,509 (21.1%). The headline survives the check either way, which is why we report the as-filed strings in the tables and the merged family here. Both are in the frozen dataset.

Known limits. Complaints are unverified consumer reports. The file has no denominator — no sales or registration counts — so nothing here can be read as a failure rate, only as a count and a composition. Nameplates span different model-year ranges, and older or recall-publicized models attract disproportionate filings. Model-year coverage per nameplate is in the CSV.

What is the Jeep Grand Cherokee rear coil spring recall (26V562)?

NHTSA campaign 26V562000 (Chrysler/FCA US) covers 328,381 US Jeep Grand Cherokees in which a rear coil spring may have been incorrectly installed and can detach from the vehicle while driving, creating a road hazard and raising crash risk. The Part 573 Safety Recall Report reached NHTSA on 2026-09-01, and interim owner-notification letters are expected around 2026-09-29. The remedy is a free dealer replacement of the rear coil spring lower isolators.

Two points matter for owners. First, campaign 26V562 supersedes earlier recalls 23V413 (2023) and 26V051 (2026); per NHTSA, vehicles already repaired under those must still get the new remedy. Second, the recalled WL-generation Grand Cherokee barely registers in our complaint ranking — the Grand Cherokee nameplate logged 349 suspension complaints across all model years in this window, and the Grand Cherokee L a further 48 — so the recall and the ranking describe different things. A recall is a manufacturer-confirmed defect in specific build dates; a complaint count is what owners chose to report. (The per-model unit split above is from news reporting cross-checked against the 328,381 total we verified in NHTSA's recalls API; the Part 573 PDF is image-only and was not machine-parsed.) What a VIN check can and can't tell you here

A VIN check is a screening step, not proof that a suspension recall was fixed. Entered on a used Grand Cherokee or any model on these lists, NHTSA's free VIN tool and a paid history report both surface whether the vehicle has an open or associated recall and a count of them. What they do not do is equally important, so read the scope plainly.

To confirm the rear-coil-spring fix was performed, check NHTSA's VIN tool for remedy status or ask a Jeep dealer — neither a complaint count nor a history report proves the repair happened. And no VIN report tells you the condition of the suspension on the specific truck in front of you; on a heavy-duty pickup with a towing history, that is what a pre-purchase inspection is for.

Frequently asked questions

Which used cars get the most suspension complaints filed with NHTSA? By raw volume in complaints filed from 2015 to September 2026, the Ford F-250 Super Duty leads with 1,806, followed by the Jeep Wrangler (1,105), Toyota Tacoma (979), Tesla Model S (730), and Ford F-350 Super Duty (724). These are consumer-filed reports, not verified defects.

Does the ranking change when you correct for how many complaints a model gets overall? Less than you would expect. Ranked by suspension share of total complaints (models with 500+ complaints), the discontinued Ford Windstar leads at 37.2%, but the F-250 Super Duty is second at 27.4%, so the same truck tops one ranking and nearly tops the other. The Jeep Wrangler, third on volume, falls to 7.1% share.

Is the Ford Super Duty's suspension really that bad, or does it just sell in huge numbers? Volume alone does not explain it. Suspension is 27.4% of everything F-250 Super Duty owners complained about, against 2.83% across the whole file, and the Ford Escape drew 25,209 complaints in total yet only 2.6% were suspension. But complaint counts have no sales denominator, so they measure reporting, not a failure rate.

Why does a discontinued Ford Windstar top the share ranking? The Windstar drew 474 suspension complaints out of 1,273 total, or 37.2%. It is a 1995-2003 minivan subject to a long-running rear-axle corrosion recall history, so suspension dominates what owners still file about. It is not a used car most buyers are shopping in 2026.

Which Jeep Grand Cherokee years are affected by the 2026 rear coil spring recall? NHTSA campaign 26V562000 covers 328,381 US vehicles: 2021-2023 Grand Cherokee L, 2022-2023 Grand Cherokee, and 2022-2023 Grand Cherokee 4xe. A rear coil spring can detach while driving. Check your VIN at NHTSA.gov.

My Grand Cherokee was already repaired under an older recall. Do I still need the new fix? Yes. Campaign 26V562 supersedes recalls 23V413 (2023) and 26V051 (2026). Per NHTSA, vehicles repaired under those earlier recalls must still receive the new remedy. Confirm with a Jeep dealer.

Are NHTSA complaints the same as proven defects or recalls? No. Complaints are unverified reports filed by consumers, per NHTSA's own documentation. They can signal a real problem or simply reflect a popular model, and they are separate from official recalls and investigations.

How can I check if a specific used car has an open recall or suspension history? Use NHTSA's free VIN tool at nhtsa.gov/recalls for recall status. A paid VIN history report adds accident, salvage, odometer, and listing history. Neither confirms a recall was actually repaired; a dealer does that.
