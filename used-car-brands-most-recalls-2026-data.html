The Used-Car Brands With the Most Recalls in 2026 (NHTSA Data Study)

In the first half of 2026, US automakers recalled 23,838,051 vehicles across 412 NHTSA campaigns, about 2.09 times the H1 2025 total and a first-half record as reported by industry trackers. Ford led by a wide margin with 12,194,548 vehicles across 55 campaigns, about 52% of light-vehicle recall volume, headlined by the 4,381,878-unit trailer-brake recall (26V104000). A VIN check shows whether an open recall is present and how many, not whether it was already repaired.

Key facts

  • Dataset: NHTSA/ODI "Recalls Data" (recall_type = Vehicle), report_received_date Jan 1 to Jun 30, 2026; pulled Aug 5, 2026 and current through Jul 30, 2026.
  • H1 2026 vehicle recalls: 412 campaigns, 23,838,051 vehicles, equal to 2.09x H1 2025 (11,397,868) and 1.58x H1 2024 (15,059,107).
  • The twist: campaign count actually fell (412 vs 429 in 2025 and 487 in 2024) while units doubled, so the surge is concentration in a few mega-campaigns, not more recalls.
  • Leader: Ford (with Lincoln), 12,194,548 vehicles across 55 campaigns, about 52% of light-vehicle recall volume (51% of all vehicle units); its two largest campaigns alone are 47% of its units.
  • Largest campaign: 26V104000 (Ford), 4,381,878 units, MY 2021-2026 F-150, Super Duty, Ranger, Maverick, Expedition and Navigator; trailer-brake and lighting failure (FMVSS 108); free dealer or over-the-air software; owner letters Mar 25, 2026. F-150 alone exceeds 2.3 million units.
  • Top 3 brands (Ford, Honda, Stellantis/Chrysler) hold 17,342,541 vehicles, 72.8% of all H1 vehicle-recall volume.
  • Highest-urgency mainstream recall: 26V363000 (Jeep Wrangler/Gladiator, MY 2021-2025), 1,076,999 units, power-steering fire, park outside.
  • Excluded (investigations, not recalls): the Tesla suspension probe PE26006 and Ford EcoBoost EA26004; neither appears as a VIN recall flag.

Does Ford really account for more than half of all 2026 vehicle recalls?

Yes by volume, with an important caveat. Ford's 55 H1 2026 campaigns cover 12,194,548 vehicles, equal to 51% of all vehicle-recall units and 52% of the consumer light-vehicle cut, against a US new-sales share of roughly 13-14%. That over-concentration is real and is corroborated by Forbes and BizzyCar. But the volume is not spread evenly across the fleet: Ford's two largest campaigns alone are 47.4% of its units, and its top seven (each above 500,000) account for about 9.4 million of the 12.19 million. Exposure clusters in specific nameplates (F-Series, Explorer, Escape, Bronco, Edge, Expedition and the Lincoln Aviator, Corsair and Navigator), so "Ford leads" does not mean every Ford VIN is recalled.

How big is the 2026 surge, and is it really a record?

H1 2026's 23,838,051 recalled vehicles is 2.09 times the same period in 2025 and the highest first-half total in the 2024-2026 window, which is why industry trackers describe it as record-setting. Treat "record" as a first-half or period record as reported; the all-time annual high remains the 2016 Takata-driven year (about 53 million full-year). The counterintuitive part is that the number of campaigns fell (412 in 2026 versus 429 in 2025 and 487 in 2024). Volume more than doubled while recall events declined, meaning the surge is driven by a handful of very large campaigns rather than a broad rise in defects. The late-July and early-August 2026 headlines that put recalls back in the news (Forbes' July 31 "skyrocketing" report and multi-automaker roundups on Aug 2-4) are Q3 events that reinforce the trend but are not included in the H1 figures here.

What is driving the volume, and which defects?

Software, electronics and cameras, not mechanical failures, account for the majority of 2026's recalled units. In our by-units breakdown, electrical-system defects total 6,296,560 vehicles (26.4%, 68 campaigns), rear or back-over camera recalls add 3,080,778 (12.9%, 21 campaigns) and visibility displays another 1,197,177 (5.0%). Powertrain (2,361,423; 9.9%), air bags (2,201,798; 9.2%), structure (1,615,947; 6.8%), steering (1,112,290; 4.7%, driven by the Jeep power-steering fire recall) and tires (1,050,561; 4.4%, driven by Honda's tire-repair-kit recall) follow. Forbes grouped its data differently (powertrain 20.6%, structural 14.26%, air bags 11.38%), so the two cuts are not directly comparable and are reported separately.

Which 2026 recalls are "do not drive" or "park outside"?

Nineteen H1 2026 vehicle campaigns carry a Do-Not-Drive, Park-Outside or park-away-from-structures fire warning. The highest-urgency mainstream example is Stellantis campaign 26V363000, covering 1,076,999 Jeep Wrangler, Wrangler 4xe and Gladiator vehicles (MY 2021-2025): power-steering wiring can overheat and ignite, so NHTSA advises parking outdoors and away from buildings until a dealer completes the wiring repair. By contrast, most of the largest Ford campaigns, including the 4.38 million-unit trailer-brake recall, are software fixes and are not park-outside actions.

How do I check a used car for open recalls before buying?

Enter the 17-digit VIN in a lookup that returns results by VIN. NHTSA's free tool at nhtsa.gov/recalls and each manufacturer's owner site both show open recalls tied to that specific VIN and are the authoritative sources. A Zilocar VIN report runs the same recall-presence screen and pairs it with the vehicle's history: accident and airbag-deployment records, salvage- and junk-auction records, odometer and rollback checks, ownership history, and past and current sales listings, in one report. For whether a specific recall was already repaired or flashed, call a franchised dealer with the VIN, because that "remedied" status is not shown by a presence check.

What a VIN check can and can't tell you here

A VIN recall check confirms presence and count, meaning whether that VIN currently has an open safety recall and how many, the same signal NHTSA's free tool provides. It does not prove a recall was remedied, and it does not track investigations. Because many of 2026's largest campaigns (the Ford trailer-brake recall 26V104000, the rear-camera recalls, and the Tesla camera recall 26V283000) are over-the-air or dealer software updates, a flagged VIN may already be fixed; only a dealer or nhtsa.gov/recalls can confirm.

Honda (2,613,738) and Stellantis/Chrysler (2,534,255) are second and third. The ranking is by vehicles recalled, not the odds a given VIN flags.
