Which Used Cars Have the Most Backup-Camera Failure Complaints? We Counted the NHTSA Data

TL;DR: Across NHTSA's full owner-complaint file, the Polestar 2 has by far the most backup-camera failure complaints of any model — 393 distinct complaints, about 3.8x the No. 2 Ford Explorer (103) — out of 1,714 camera-coded complaints we isolated from 2,235,299 records retrieved 2026-08-18. Ford is the top brand overall at 482. A VIN check can confirm whether a used car is named in a backup-camera recall, but it cannot prove the fix was applied; camera-test on the drive and confirm remedy with a dealer.

This piece is published the day after Stellantis announced a recall of an estimated 848,511 US vehicles (955,085 globally) over radio software that can stop the rearview camera image from appearing on the media screen. That recall is the news hook. The question it does not answer, and this analysis does, is: across the whole used market, which models do owners actually report a black or frozen backup camera on?

Key facts

  • Load-bearing finding: Polestar 2 leads all models with 393 distinct backup-camera complaints in NHTSA's ODI complaint file (retrieved 2026-08-18).
  • Dataset: NHTSA ODI Complaints flat file (FLAT_CMPL.txt), file dated 2026-08-15, N = 2,235,299 records; 1,714 distinct complaints after filtering to the rear-camera image/display components.
  • Top 5 models: Polestar 2 (393), Ford Explorer (103), Ford F-150 (67), Tesla Model Y (66), Ford Mustang (58).
  • Top brand: Ford, 482 complaints, spread across many nameplates and a long run of camera recalls.
  • Why Polestar 2 leads: two of its own software recalls, 25V280000 and 25V615000 (2021-2025 Polestar 2, "the rearview camera image may not display"); 340 of its 393 complaints were filed in calendar 2025, tracking those recalls.
  • News peg: Stellantis (FCA US LLC), 848,511 US vehicles, 2026-27 Chrysler/Dodge/Jeep/Ram models, radio-software camera fault, over-the-air remedy, owner letters begin September 2026. No related crashes or injuries reported. NHTSA campaign number pending publication.
  • Legal context: backup cameras are federally mandated on new light vehicles built since May 2018 under FMVSS No. 111.

Which used cars have the most backup-camera failure complaints?

The Polestar 2 has the most backup-camera failure complaints of any model in NHTSA's database — 393 distinct complaints, versus 103 for the second-place Ford Explorer. That gap holds up when we sort the entire filtered dataset, not a subset: the Polestar 2 is the single highest make-and-model, and it leads the field by roughly 3.8-to-1. Ford is the busiest brand overall, with 482 complaints spread across the Explorer, F-150, Mustang, Escape, Edge and more.

Is the Polestar 2 leader a sales-volume artifact?

No — and this is the interesting part. Raw complaint counts normally reward high-volume vehicles, so the honest check is to normalize per sales and see whether the leader survives. Here the normalization does not reverse the ranking; it makes the Polestar 2 look worse. The Polestar 2 is a niche EV, with roughly 6,960 US units sold in 2022 and 10,432 in 2023, for a cumulative US fleet somewhere in the range of 35,000-50,000. At that size, 393 complaints works out to roughly 786-980 per 100,000 vehicles.

The Ford Explorer, by contrast, has well over 1.5 million cumulative US units across its 2014-2023 model years, so its ~103 complaints amount to roughly 7 per 100,000 — on the order of 100 times lower. High-volume trucks such as the F-150 do not lead on raw counts and fall further per-capita. The volume artifact is refuted: the model on top is a low-volume vehicle with a genuine, concentrated defect cluster, not a big seller collecting proportional noise.

Per-100k figures use approximate cumulative US sales (GoodCarBadCar/press) and are directional; exact registered-fleet denominators are not published, but the gap is far too wide to be a rounding effect.

Two further checks that could have overturned the No. 1, and did not. First, model-string consolidation: NHTSA often files one vehicle under several model strings, which can hide the true leader (the Chevrolet Silverado does exactly this in our instrument-cluster study). Consolidating every Ford string still tops out at the Explorer's 103, and consolidating all four Tesla strings gives 94 — neither approaches 393, so no merge changes the ranking. Second, repeat filings: NHTSA publishes only the first 11 VIN characters, and the Polestar 2's 393 complaints carry 72 distinct prefixes. Because an 11-character prefix identifies a model/year/plant rather than an individual car, that number neither proves nor disproves repeat filers — so we state plainly what the data supports: these are 393 distinct complaint submissions, not 393 verified separate vehicles. The concentration of 340 filings into calendar 2025 also means recall publicity almost certainly pulled complaints forward, which inflates a raw count relative to a model whose owners had no recall notice prompting them to file.

Why does the Polestar 2 have so many backup-camera complaints?

The Polestar 2's complaint surge is tied to its own recalls, and the timing proves it. NHTSA lists two software recalls, 25V280000 and 25V615000, both covering 2021-2025 Polestar 2 vehicles under the "back over prevention: software" component, both stating that "the rearview camera image may not display." Of the model's 393 complaints, 340 were added to NHTSA's file during calendar 2025 — the year those recalls landed — with 24 in 2024 and 29 in 2026.

The narratives match the failure mode precisely: owners describe the "rear camera freezes up for a few seconds and then blacks out," a picture that "flickers or does not display an image," and a "Camera is temporarily unavailable" error on the screen. That is the same failure class as the Stellantis news peg — software preventing the camera image from rendering — which is why the two stories rhyme even though the vehicles could not be more different.

What the fresh Stellantis recall does and doesn't tell used buyers

The August 17, 2026 Stellantis recall is a forward-looking, preventive fix on brand-new vehicles, not evidence that these models are failing in the field. FCA US LLC recalled an estimated 848,511 US-market vehicles (955,085 globally, once Canada, Mexico and other markets are added) because radio software may prevent the rearview camera image from appearing on the media screen. The affected models are 2026-27 Chrysler Pacifica, Voyager and Pacifica Plug-in Hybrid, Dodge Charger, Jeep Cherokee, Compass, Gladiator, Grand Cherokee and variants, Wrangler, and Ram 1500, 2500, ProMaster and ProMaster EV. The remedy is an over-the-air software update prompted on the media screen, with no dealer visit required; owner letters begin in September 2026. Stellantis says it is unaware of any related accidents or injuries.

Crucially, those recalled 2026-27 vehicles show essentially zero owner complaints in NHTSA's file today (the 2026-27 Pacifica, Wrangler and Grand Cherokee each register 0; the Ram 1500, 1). The recall is precautionary. Our used-market ranking, by contrast, captures where owners have actually reported the camera going black over years of ownership — a different and, for a used buyer, more useful question. What a VIN check can and can't tell you here

A VIN check answers one question about backup cameras cleanly: does an open recall name this specific vehicle? It does not tell you whether that recall was fixed. Both a VIN check and NHTSA's free tool report recall presence by VIN; neither confirms that a software flash or camera replacement was actually completed on the car in front of you. Treat the two questions separately, and never assume a flagged recall was quietly handled by a prior owner.

Because a VIN check cannot prove remedy, do two things on any car with a camera recall: shift into reverse on the test drive and confirm a clear, non-frozen image (test again after a cold start, when software faults often surface), and ask a franchised dealer or check the manufacturer portal to confirm the update was completed. For the difference between a fixed recall and an open probe, see what "recall closed" means versus an open investigation on a VIN check.

It does not confirm a recall was remedied or track investigations; leave those to NHTSA and the dealer.

Frequently asked questions

Which used cars have the most backup-camera failure complaints? In NHTSA's complaint database, the Polestar 2 leads with 393 distinct backup-camera complaints, followed by the Ford Explorer (103), Ford F-150 (67), Tesla Model Y (66) and Ford Mustang (58). By make, Ford has the most overall at 482.

Was the Polestar 2 backup camera recalled? Yes. NHTSA lists two Polestar 2 software recalls, 25V280000 and 25V615000, covering 2021-2025 model years, where "the rearview camera image may not display." The remedy is a software update; confirm it was completed with a Polestar dealer before buying.

Why does a backup camera go black or freeze when I shift into reverse? The most common owner-reported cause is a software or radio-module fault that stops the rear image from rendering on the screen, often shown as a frozen picture or a "camera temporarily unavailable" message. It is usually fixed by a software update rather than a new camera.

Is it illegal to drive a used car with a broken backup camera? Backup cameras have been federally required on new light vehicles built since May 2018 under FMVSS No. 111. A non-working camera can fail a state safety inspection where one applies and removes a mandated safety feature, though rules vary by state.

Can a VIN check tell me if a used car's backup-camera recall was fixed? No. A VIN check shows whether a recall applies to that VIN, but not whether the software update or repair was completed. Confirm remedy status with a franchised dealer or at the manufacturer's recall portal.

Are the 2026-2027 Jeep, Ram, Dodge and Chrysler backup cameras safe after the Stellantis recall? The August 2026 Stellantis recall is a preventive software fix on new 2026-27 vehicles, delivered over the air. Those model years currently show essentially zero owner complaints in NHTSA's file, so the recall is precautionary rather than a response to widespread failures.

What should I test on a used car's backup camera during a test drive? Shift into reverse and confirm the image appears within a second or two, is sharp and not frozen, updates as you move, and shows no "camera unavailable" error. Test it again after a cold start, since some faults appear only on the first drive of the day.
