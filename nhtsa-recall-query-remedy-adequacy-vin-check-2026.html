NHTSA opened a formal investigation on 6 November into whether a recall fix actually worked. The car it concerns is a robotaxi, but the instrument it used is the same one the agency has pointed at mainstream car recalls 163 times since 1997 - and the record of what happens next has not been published before.

TL;DR

NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation opens a Recall Query when an existing recall's own remedy looks inadequate. Isolating that prefix in the agency's investigations file gives 163 car and light-truck queries since 1997. 140 are closed, and NHTSA links 94 of them - 67.1% - to a recall campaign. A completed recall is a prescribed repair, not a proven one.

What is an NHTSA recall query?

It is the probe NHTSA opens when a recall's own fix looks inadequate, or when the recall looks too narrow. It is filed under an action number beginning RQ, and it is a different instrument from the PE (Preliminary Evaluation) and EA (Engineering Analysis) probes that investigate a suspected defect in the first place. A PE or EA asks "is there a defect here?" A recall query asks "the manufacturer already said yes and fixed it - did the fix hold, and did it reach enough cars?"

NHTSA's own wording is consistent across cases. For the Ford Maverick frozen-camera query RQ26004, ODI opened it "to evaluate the remedy effectiveness and scope of recall 24V684." For the GMC Sierra grille-deflector query RQ26003, "to assess the adequacy of the scope and remedy for Recall 25V-060." For the Jeep Grand Cherokee coil-spring query RQ25005, "to assess the adequacy of FCA's remedy procedure under Recall 23V-413."

This matters to a used-car buyer for a specific reason: all of those recalls read as closed business on a vehicle history report. The query is the only federal record that the business may not be closed.

How often does a recall query end in another recall?

About two thirds of the time. Of the 163 car and light-truck queries, 140 are closed, and NHTSA's CAMPNO field links 94 of them to a Part 573 recall campaign - 67.1%.

The unrestricted 248 includes 34 undated pre-1997 legacy stubs described in the Method; all 34 are uncloseable and outside the car basis, so they sit in that row's first column only and change neither rate.

The last row is the reason the rate is the headline and the count is not. Restricting the file to cars and light trucks removes a third of the queries and barely moves the rate. We have twice found the opposite shape in this file - a scope restriction that nearly halved a campaign count, and one that nearly doubled a share - so which of a filter's statistics survives is not predictable, and both bases are printed beside every figure on this page.

What the rate does not say. It is not "two thirds of recall repairs fail." A recall query is rare - 163 of them against many thousands of car and light-truck recall campaigns since 1997. The rate describes what happens after NHTSA has already decided a remedy is worth questioning, which is a selected set by construction. Read it as: when the agency takes that step, it usually turns out to have been warranted.

What is the Waymo recall query, and why is it not in the headline number?

It is RQ26006, opened 6 November 2026, still open - and Waymo LLC is not a passenger-car filer, so it is counted only in the unrestricted 248. Saying so plainly matters more than borrowing the news peg: the honest version is that the live story sits outside the basis this page leads with.

NHTSA's own summary of RQ26006 is unusually direct. Waymo filed recall 25E084 on 10 December 2025 to stop its vehicles driving past stopped school buses with red lights flashing, and the recall report said the software remedy "was completed for all affected vehicles on November 17, 2025." ODI then found that "of the total reported incidents identified during PE25013, a majority of the incidents occurred after the November 17, 2025 remedy date," and opened the query "to assess the adequacy of the remedy for Recall 25E084."

That is the whole mechanism of a recall query in one case: a remedy reported complete, incidents continuing anyway, and a federal record of the doubt that no vehicle history report carries.

What does a recall query look like on an ordinary used car?

Like a recall that was already repaired. Three current examples, all cars or light trucks, all taken from NHTSA's own summaries:

  • RQ26004, Ford Maverick (opened 23 October 2026, open). Recall 24V684 covered 2022-2024 Mavericks whose rear-view camera could freeze in reverse; the remedy was a software update. ODI has since received five owner reports of frozen camera images following that remedy, plus a redacted number reported by Ford.
  • RQ26003, GMC Sierra (opened 9 October 2026, open). Of 118 complaints alleging the upper grille deflector detached on 2022-2024 Sierras, 21 allege it detached after recall 25V-060 was performed - and 2023-2024 model years were never in that recall at all, which is the "scope" half of a query.
  • RQ25003, Ram 1500-5500 (opened 3 July 2025, open). Fourteen owner reports and six death-and-injury reports of brake-transmission-shift-interlock failures on 2013-2018 Ram trucks that had all previously been repaired under recall 17V-821 or 18V-100.

The pattern across all three is the one a buyer should take away: the recall shows as done, and the federal doubt about it is recorded somewhere else entirely.

For the single most documented case, GM's L87 V8, the query has already escalated. RQ26001 was opened in January 2026 to assess the remedy effectiveness of recall 25V-274; NHTSA recorded 499 complaints of post-remedy engine failure and noted GM had received 6,953, then closed the query in August 2026 and continued the work as Engineering Analysis EA26005. We have covered that engine separately, both as a history check on the L87 and as failures after the recall fix; it appears here as one case among 163, not as the finding.

What a VIN check can and cannot tell you here

This page exists because of a gap in the public data, and the gap includes our own report. Being exact about it is the point.

QuestionIn a Zilocar VIN check?Where it actually lives

Did the remedy work on this specific car?NoNothing publishes this; the query is the nearest thing

A VIN check is the right tool for finding out that a recall exists on the car and for seeing the history the seller did not mention. It is the wrong tool for remedy status, and no vehicle history report on the market carries the recall-query record - that is a federal investigations file, not a vehicle file. Our companion analysis from the complaints side, defects that returned after a completed recall repair, measures the same problem from the owner's end rather than the regulator's.

The practical check, in order: run the VIN for recall presence and history; ask the dealer to confirm by VIN which recall repairs were actually performed; then search NHTSA's investigations record for the recall numbers you were given, to see whether any of them carries an RQ.

Method

Source file. NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation defect-investigations flat file, FLAT_INV.txt, inside FLAT_INV.zip (public domain). Retrieved 2026-11-11 at 08:22 UTC: 4,303,154 bytes, server Last-Modified: Tue, 10 Nov 2026 20:49:21 GMT, expanding to 390,634,982 bytes with a member timestamp of 2026-11-10 06:06:28. zipfile.testzip() returned None, so the archive is intact. The file holds 154,460 rows resolving to 5,357 distinct NHTSA action numbers.

Universe and filter. The file stores one row per action-number-and-model-year, while open date, close date and CAMPNO are action-level fields repeated on every row, so it is collapsed to one record per action number before anything is counted. Of the 5,357 action numbers, 248 carry the RQ prefix. For reference, the rest of the file is PE 2,331, EA 1,933, DP 510, SQ 159, EQ 85, AQ 43, ID 24, TA 7, RP 6 and 11 unprefixed. RQ is the only prefix NHTSA uses for a query into an existing recall's remedy or scope, and it was explicitly excluded from our 2026-10-09 investigation-outcome analysis, which kept only PE and EA.

Reconciling 248. Of the 248 RQ records, 214 carry a parseable open date (1997 to 2026) and 34 do not - records numbered RQ89001 through RQ96xxx with an empty open date, an empty close date and an empty manufacturer field. They are legacy stubs predating the file's usable history. All 34 fall outside the car and light-truck basis, so the 163-query headline universe contains none of them, and because every rate here is computed on closed queries and these carry no close date, they affect no rate on this page. They are included in the raw 248 and excluded from the year table, which is why that table's unrestricted column sums to 214.

The car and light-truck restriction. The file covers motorcycles, heavy trucks, buses, motorhomes, trailers, child seats, standalone equipment and automated-driving companies alongside cars. The headline basis keeps only queries whose MFGTXT filer appears on the 42-name allow-list of US passenger-car and light-truck Part 573 filers published in full in the frozen CSV of our 2026-11-08 software-remedy analysis. That list is reused rather than rebuilt, and it is an explicit allow-list rather than a name pattern on purpose: a name regex over this field leaks in both directions, keeping fire-apparatus and bus builders while dropping car filers. The restriction takes 248 queries to 163. Every figure is also given on the unrestricted basis.

What counts as ending in a recall. NHTSA's own CAMPNO field, non-empty. Our 2026-10-09 analysis established this as the agency's link from an investigation to the Part 573 campaign it produced, and no consumer-facing NHTSA tool and no commercial vehicle-history aggregator exposes it.

The rate is computed on closed queries only - 140 of the 163 car-basis queries, and 183 of the 248 unrestricted. An open query has no outcome yet, so including the 23 open ones in the denominator would understate the rate by construction.

Cleanup and dedup. Deduplication is on the action number. Makes, models, model years and component labels are unioned across a query's rows. Filer attribution uses MFGTXT, the Part 573 filer, not the make label: one query routinely spans several makes (RQ26001 covers Cadillac, Chevrolet and GMC) and make spellings split one manufacturer across rows.

Reversal checks, reported either way.

  1. Does CAMPNO point forward to a new recall, or back at the recall being queried? This is the check most likely to break the finding, and it only partly survives. Linked campaign numbers were resolved against the Part 573 file (FLAT_RCL_POST_2010.zip, retrieved 2026-11-11, 14,986,264 bytes, 247,848 rows, 15,393 campaigns, newest RCDATE 2026-11-06) and their filing dates compared with the query's open date. Only 31 of the 94 car-basis links resolve at all, because 63 of the linked campaigns predate 2010 and are absent from that file. Of the 31 that do resolve, 28 (90.3%) were filed after the query opened and 3 (9.7%) were filed before it. The three backward links are a recognisable subtype - RQ17003 and RQ17004, both titled "Scope and Timeliness", and RQ13002 - where the field evidently points at the campaign under examination. This is why every figure on this page says NHTSA "links" a query to a recall rather than that the query "produced" one.
  2. Does the rate survive the scope restriction? Yes: 65.0% unrestricted against 67.1% restricted, a 2.1-point move while the query count falls 34%.
  3. Is the year series monotonic? No, and it is not characterised as a trend. Every year 1997-2026 is published on both bases.
  4. Does a maker-level link-rate ranking hold up? No, and it is not published. Seven filers have five or more closed queries; across them the rate correlates −0.331 with query count and +0.480 with median query year.
  5. Era split, for completeness: queries opened before 2005 closed with a linked recall 74.3% of the time (55 of 74), those from 2005 on 59.1% (39 of 66). This is not presented as a decline, because the 23 still-open queries skew heavily recent, so the recent closed set is biased toward queries that resolved quickly.

Known limits. The file records investigations, not outcomes on individual vehicles: nothing here says whether a given car's repair worked. CAMPNO carries no indication of whether a linked campaign expanded the original recall or replaced its remedy. Recall queries are rare by construction and NHTSA chooses which remedies to question, so this is a selected set and not a sample of recall repairs. And the 2026 rows are incomplete: NHTSA's recall record ran to 2026-11-06 on the day of retrieval, roughly five days behind the press, so recalls announced in the days before publication are not yet in the Part 573 file used for the date cross-check.

Frozen dataset. nhtsa-recall-query-remedy-adequacy-vin-check-2026-2026-11-11.csv - all 248 recall queries, one row each, with the open and close dates, the filer, a in_car_light_truck_basis flag so the boundary can be disputed, the linked campaign and its filing date, whether that campaign was filed after the query opened, days open, and a per-row note recording every caveat above that applies to that row.

Frequently asked questions

What is an NHTSA recall query? It is the investigation NHTSA opens when an existing recall's own fix looks inadequate, or when the recall looks too narrow. NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation files it under an action number beginning RQ, separately from the PE and EA probes that investigate a defect in the first place. In its own words for the Ford Maverick query RQ26004, ODI opens it "to evaluate the remedy effectiveness and scope" of the earlier recall.

How often does a recall query end in another recall? Of the 163 car and light-truck recall queries NHTSA has opened since 1997, 140 are closed, and NHTSA links 94 of those closed queries (67.1%) to a recall campaign. On the unrestricted basis it is 119 of 183 closed queries, or 65.0%. The two bases agree closely, which is what makes the rate the figure we lead on.

Does that mean a completed recall repair cannot be trusted? No, and the number does not support that reading. A recall query is rare: 163 queries stand against many thousands of car and light-truck recall campaigns, so the overwhelming majority of recalls are never formally questioned. What the rate says is narrower and still useful - once NHTSA does take the step of questioning a remedy, the question usually turns out to have been warranted.

How long does a recall query take? The 140 closed car and light-truck queries ran a median of 246 days from opening to closing, with a range from 34 days to 1,679. So a query opened on the car you are looking at today will most likely still be open months after you buy, which is the practical reason to read the query itself rather than wait for its outcome.

What is the Waymo recall query everyone is reporting? RQ26006, opened 6 November 2026 and still open. NHTSA's own summary says a majority of the reported incidents of Waymo vehicles passing stopped school buses occurred after the 17 November 2025 remedy date for recall 25E084, and that ODI is opening the query "to assess the adequacy of the remedy." It sits outside this page's headline basis: Waymo LLC is not a passenger-car Part 573 filer on our 42-name list, so it is counted only in the unrestricted 248.

Does a VIN check show that a recall remedy failed? No. A VIN check shows that a recall is present and counts open recalls. Whether the repair was performed is not in it, and whether NHTSA later questioned that repair is not in it either - the recall-query record lives only in NHTSA's investigations file and on its website. This is a limit of the public data, not of one report.

Which carmakers have the most recall queries? Ford with 50, then General Motors and Chrysler/FCA with 32 each. Those three hold 114 of the 163 car and light-truck queries, or 70%. We are not publishing a ranking of which maker's queries most often end in a recall: only seven filers have five or more closed queries, and the rate correlates −0.331 with a filer's query count and +0.480 with its median year, two unstable and conflicting signals on seven data points.

Were there more recall queries in 2026 than usual? 2026 has 6 queries so far on the unrestricted basis and 4 on the car and light-truck basis. The highest recent year is 2024, with 14 car-basis queries - the most since 1999. But the series is not a trend line: it runs heavy in 1997-2004, collapses through 2005-2023 and rises again recently, and the early years may reflect how ODI used the prefix then rather than how often remedies failed. Every year is printed in the table above so the shape can be judged rather than taken on trust.
