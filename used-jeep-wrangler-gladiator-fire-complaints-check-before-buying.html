Used Jeep Wrangler and Gladiator Fire Complaints by Model Year: What to Check Before You Buy

TL;DR: NHTSA recall 26V363 covers 1,076,999 US Jeeps — 787,887 Wrangler (JL) and 289,112 Gladiator (JT), model years 2021-2025 — for a power-steering pump connection that can overheat and cause a fire, even parked. In a July 29, 2026 pull of NHTSA complaints, the 2024 Wrangler drew the most fire-keyword complaints (170), then the 2021 Wrangler (100). A VIN check flags open recalls but can't confirm the fix was done.

Key facts

  • Recall: NHTSA campaign 26V363000 (shown as "26V363"); Chrysler (FCA US) manufacturer recall 21D, submitted June 4, 2026.
  • Scope: 2021-2025 Jeep Wrangler (JL, including the 4xe plug-in hybrid) and 2021-2025 Jeep Gladiator (JT).
  • US units: 1,076,999 total (787,887 Wrangler + 289,112 Gladiator). Estimated share with the defect: 0.1%.
  • Build windows (the real population): Wrangler June 24, 2020 – Dec 2, 2024; Gladiator Aug 18, 2020 – Dec 2, 2024.
  • Defect: the Electric Hydraulic Power Steering Pump (EHPSP) electrical connection can develop high resistance, overheat combustible material, and cause a fire — possibly with the ignition off and the vehicle parked. Component supplier: Dare Auto Inc.
  • Warning signs: loss of power-steering assist and/or a "Service Power Steering" message.
  • Reported harm: NHTSA is aware of 51 fires and 1 injury attributed to this issue.
  • Remedy: inspect, repair, and replace EHPSP parts as needed, free. Consumer advisory is Park Outside, not a full Do Not Drive. The remedy part was still being finalized at announcement (phased recall).
  • Owner letters: mailed in phases July 9 – Aug 3, 2026; VINs searchable on NHTSA since June 11, 2026.

Which used Wrangler and Gladiator model years get the most fire complaints?

Within the recalled 2021-2025 range, the 2024 Jeep Wrangler is the single largest cluster of fire-mentioning complaints — 170 records, about 20% of that year's 868 total complaints — with the 2021 Wrangler second at 100. The Gladiator's fire-keyword complaints are much lower across every year, peaking at 26 in 2021. The complaint curve concentrates on the Wrangler, which makes sense: the Wrangler is 73.2% of the recalled fleet and carries the overwhelming majority of fire complaints.

These are keyword matches over free-text NHTSA complaints, not NHTSA-adjudicated fire causes, so treat them as "complaints mentioning fire," not confirmed fires. They over-count somewhat and will keep drifting upward after the pull date.

Why the 2024 Wrangler number is easy to get wrong

If you query NHTSA's complaints database with model = "wrangler" for 2024 and 2025, you get zero — which is misleading. NHTSA renamed the Wrangler in the complaints database for those years into four body-style variants: WRANGLER 2-DOOR, WRANGLER 4-DOOR, WRANGLER 4-DOOR 392, and WRANGLER 4-DOOR 4XE. The 2024 figure of 170 and the 2025 figure of 12 above aggregate all four variants, de-duplicated by ODI complaint number. Any outlet querying the single "wrangler" name will wrongly report zero for the two newest years.

By the numbers

  • 1,076,999 US vehicles recalled under 26V363; 73.2% are Wranglers.
  • 51 fires, 1 injury in NHTSA's public tally. The Part 573 report separately lists 72 field reports (35 confirmed to originate at the EHPSP interface), 63 customer-assistance records, 0 accidents, and 1 injury; record dates span July 13, 2019 – March 28, 2026.
  • 170 fire-keyword complaints on the 2024 Wrangler — the largest single-year cohort.
  • 0.1% is Chrysler's estimated share of recalled vehicles that actually have the defect.

Is it the recall or the investigation PE24-024?

These are two linked but distinct things. NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation opened preliminary evaluation PE24-024 on September 6, 2024, into engine-compartment fires on 2021-2023 Wrangler and Gladiator vehicles. The recall (26V363) is the later manufacturer action: Chrysler determined a defect exists on May 28, 2026, and filed the recall on June 4, 2026, covering model years 2021-2025. FCA had earlier investigated EHPSP fires from May 2023 to April 2024, closed it for a low rate, then reopened in August 2024.

Should I avoid Jeeps built between mid-2020 and December 2024?

The recall population is defined by build date, not the model-year badge: Wranglers built June 24, 2020 – Dec 2, 2024 and Gladiators built Aug 18, 2020 – Dec 2, 2024. A vehicle in that window isn't automatically dangerous — Chrysler estimates only 0.1% have the defect — but any used 2021-2025 Wrangler or Gladiator should be VIN-checked against 26V363 before purchase, and the buyer should confirm whether the free remedy has been completed. Pre-recall years (2018-2020 Wrangler, 2020 Gladiator) show fire complaints too, but fall outside this campaign.

How to check a specific used Jeep by VIN

  1. Start with NHTSA's free VIN lookup at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows whether the exact VIN has open recalls, including 26V363, and whether the remedy is still open — the authoritative source for remedy status.
  2. Confirm remedy completion with a Jeep dealer if the NHTSA result is unclear, since the fix was phased and parts were still being finalized at announcement.
  3. Run a full history check — for example a Zilocar VIN check — to screen for recall presence and surface accident, airbag-deployment, salvage-auction, odometer, theft, ownership, and sales-listing history that a recall lookup alone won't show.

A Zilocar VIN check screens a used Wrangler or Gladiator for open-recall presence and surfaces accident and airbag-deployment records, junk/salvage-auction records, odometer and theft checks, ownership, sales-listing history, specs, NHTSA/IIHS ratings, and market valuation. It does not confirm whether recall 26V363 was remedied, track NHTSA investigations, or show firmware or legal title-brand status — verify those with NHTSA or a Jeep dealer.

Frequently asked questions

Which used Jeep Wrangler and Gladiator model years have the most NHTSA fire complaints? Among recalled years (2021-2025), the 2024 Wrangler leads with 170 complaints mentioning fire, then the 2021 Wrangler with 100 (NHTSA complaints data pulled July 29, 2026). Gladiator fire-keyword complaints are far lower, peaking at 26 in 2021.

Is my used Jeep included in the 26V363 power-steering fire recall? Recall 26V363 covers 2021-2025 Jeep Wrangler (JL, including 4xe) and 2021-2025 Gladiator (JT) built within specific date windows. The only reliable check is entering the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls, which shows open recalls for that exact vehicle.

Can a VIN check tell me whether the recall was already fixed? No. A VIN check screens for recall presence but cannot confirm whether 26V363 was remedied or the pump wiring replaced. Open-versus-closed remedy status must be verified through NHTSA's VIN lookup or a Jeep dealer.

Does the recall cover the Jeep Wrangler 4xe plug-in hybrid? Yes. Recall 26V363 includes the 2021-2025 Jeep Wrangler JL, and the Part 573 report specifies that this covers the Wrangler 4xe plug-in hybrid within the affected build-date window.

Can I still drive a recalled Jeep before the fix is available? NHTSA issued a Park Outside advisory, not a full Do Not Drive order, because a fire can start with the ignition off and the vehicle parked. Owners are told to park away from structures and other vehicles until the remedy is completed. Confirm current guidance with NHTSA or a dealer.

What are the warning signs of the power-steering fire defect? Per the recall, owners may notice a loss of power-steering assist and/or a "Service Power Steering" warning message (DTC). These can indicate the high-resistance electrical connection at the power-steering pump. Do not ignore them; contact a Jeep dealer.

Does a vehicle history report show if a Jeep had a fire or was in an accident? A VIN check surfaces accident and damage records (including severity and airbag-deployment status) and junk/salvage-auction records, which is where a fire-damaged unit may appear. It reports these as auction records, not as a legal title brand.
