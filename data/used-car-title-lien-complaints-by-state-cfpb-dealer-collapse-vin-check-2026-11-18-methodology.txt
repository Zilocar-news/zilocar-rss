TAXONOMY (current CFPB schema):
  Narrow "title" tier (headline) = Sub-issue "Unable to receive car title or other problem after the loan is paid off" (7,846) + Sub-issue "Did not receive car title" (108) = 7,954.
  Broader payoff/title cluster = narrow tier + Sub-issue "Problem with paying off the loan" (4,173) = 12,127.
  Full parent Issue "Problems at the end of the loan or lease" = 17,784.

Per-capita denominator: US Census Bureau July-1-2025 state population estimates (per 100,000 residents).

Reimplementable method line: unzip complete CCDB CSV -> keep Product=='Vehicle loan or lease' -> assert max(Date received) -> bucket by Sub-issue -> count by State -> divide by 2025 Census population x 100,000 -> rank all 50 states + DC.

Columns: state | state_abbr | pop_2025 | title_complaints_total | title_per_100k | title_rank_per_100k | cluster_complaints_total | cluster_per_100k | title_2024_jan_oct | title_2026_jan_oct

ROBUSTNESS (reversal): raw counts track state population. Per-100k normalization overturns the raw leaders — California (2.14/100k, rank 20), Texas (2.08, rank 21) and New York (1.97, rank 27) fall BELOW the national rate. The states elevated on BOTH raw and per-capita are Georgia (448 raw, 3.96/100k; #1 per-capita among n>=100), New Jersey (3.85), Maryland (3.73), Nevada (3.63), Florida (3.12), Virginia (3.12). DC (5.77, n=40) and Delaware (4.06, n=43) post higher per-100k rates on small samples and are flagged low-base.

SUPERLATIVE: tested against all 50 states + DC. No single "worst" state claimed; Georgia is the highest-rate state with a statistically stable base (n>=100).
