METHODOLOGY - Airbag-inflator rupture recalls by inflator position (Zilocar vehicle-data desk)

File state:  248,434 rows, 15,417 distinct campaigns. max(RCDATE) over all record types = 20261116
             (a single equipment campaign, 26E081000). max(RCDATE) restricted to RCLTYPECD='V' = 20261112.
             Frontier computed on the SAME basis as every figure published here.

Campaign filter (all three conditions):
  1. COMPDESC contains "AIR BAG"; AND
  2. the campaign text (DESC + REMEDY) names TAKATA, OR
     COMPDESC/text contains "INFLATOR" and the text contains "RUPTURE".
  This yields 166 campaigns across all record types.

Scope restriction - cars and light trucks only:
  RCLTYPECD='V' means "vehicle", not "car" (it carries motorcycles, buses, heavy trucks, motorhomes and
  trailers). Campaigns are kept only when the Part 573 filer (MFGTXT) is on the explicit allow-list of
  51 named car and light-truck manufacturers printed below. A name REGEX was deliberately not used.
  166 campaigns -> 132 car/light-truck campaigns; 34 excluded, of which 27 are the inflator supplier's
  own equipment filings (Takata Corporation, TK Global, Key Safety Systems/Joyson, Autoliv) and the rest
  are Daimler Trucks, Spartan Motors and Forest River.

  NOTE ON MFGTXT MATCHING: MFGTXT is not canonical. The same company appears as "General Motors, LLC" and
  "General Motors LLC", "Chrysler (FCA US, LLC)" and "Chrysler (FCA US LLC)", "Mercedes-Benz USA, LLC" and
  "Mercedes-Benz USA, LLC." (trailing period), "Tesla, Inc." and "Tesla Motors, Inc.". Matching is therefore
  done on a normalised key (lowercased, "&" -> "and", non-alphanumerics collapsed to single spaces).
  An exact string match drops real car campaigns silently - including this study's largest, 26V748000,
  whose filer is "Toyota Motor Engineering & Manufacturing".

Inflator position (the load-bearing variable):
  frontal                  = COMPDESC contains "FRONTAL"
  side_curtain_or_roof_rail = COMPDESC contains "SIDE/WINDOW", or the text contains "CURTAIN",
                              "ROOF RAIL" or "ROOF-RAIL"
  position_unspecified      = neither (COMPDESC is the bare label "AIR BAGS")

Aggregation: one row per campaign (not per make/model/year line); units are POTAFF as filed.

Eras compared: campaigns filed 2014-01-01..2017-12-31 (the Takata frontal wave) and 2023-01-01..2026-11-19.

REVERSAL CHECK AND RESULT:
  The finding is a composition claim, so the check that could overturn it is the treatment of the
  position_unspecified campaigns. In the 2014-2017 wave 10 of 87 campaigns are position_unspecified;
  in 2023-2026, 0 of 20 are. Assigning EVERY unspecified wave campaign to side/curtain - the most adverse
  possible assumption - moves the wave's side-inflator share from 2.3% to 13.8%, against 75.0% in
  2023-2026. The direction survives the worst case. (All 10 unspecified wave campaigns are 2014-2016
  Honda, Subaru, Chrysler, Ford, Nissan and Toyota filings, i.e. the classic frontal Takata recalls,
  so counting them as side-inflator campaigns is deliberately perverse.)

Independent verification: 26V748000, 25V748000, 24V198000 and 26V325000 were re-checked against
  api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber and matched the flat file exactly on filer, component,
  report-received date and POTAFF.

KNOWN LIMITS:
  1. POTAFF is the manufacturer's launch-time estimate of units POTENTIALLY affected. It is not a count
     of cars still on the road, still unrepaired, or still in the US.
  2. The file does not reliably identify the inflator SUPPLIER. Only 2 of the 20 recent campaigns name
     Takata in their text, and 4 of the 2014-2017 wave campaigns do - so the absence of the word "Takata"
     is NOT evidence that a campaign is not Takata, and no supplier-level claim is made here.
     Joyson/Key Safety Systems and Autoliv are named only in their own equipment filings (25E030000,
     24E035000), which this study's vehicle-campaign basis excludes.
  3. 2026 is an incomplete year, and NHTSA's published record lags filings by several days.
  4. 314,029 of the 379,157 units in 2026 (83%) come from one campaign, 26V748000. The recent stream is
     lumpy, so unit totals for a single recent year should not be read as a trend.
  5. Position is read from NHTSA's own COMPDESC label plus campaign text, not from engineering records.
