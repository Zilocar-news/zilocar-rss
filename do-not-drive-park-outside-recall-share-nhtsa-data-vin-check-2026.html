1 in 17 of 2026's Car Recalls Carries a Severe Advisory - but Only 1 in 60 Says Do Not Drive (NHTSA Data)

Of 301 car and light-truck recall campaigns filed with NHTSA between 2 January and 6 November 2026, 18 - 5.98%, about 1 in 17 - carry an explicit do-not-drive or park-outside advisory, read from NHTSA's own structured flags. But the two advisories mean different things, and separating them matters: only 5 campaigns (1.66%, about 1 in 60) actually say do not drive, while 13 (4.32%) say park outside. The other 283 (94.02%) are routine notify-and-schedule recalls. A VIN check can confirm a recall is present on a specific car; it cannot tell you which class it falls in.

Key facts

  • Dataset: NHTSA ODI Recalls flat file (FLAT_RCL_POST_2010.txt), retrieved 2026-11-12, deduplicated to campaign level on CAMPNO.
  • Headline basis - cars and light trucks, N=301: either severe advisory 18 (5.98%, ~1 in 17); do-not-drive 5 (1.66%, ~1 in 60); park-outside 13 (4.32%); routine 283 (94.02%).
  • Vehicles only (RCLTYPECD='V', N=708): either 36 (5.08%), do-not-drive 16 (2.26%), park-outside 21 (2.97%).
  • All record types (N=790, as NHTSA ships it): either 39 (4.94%), do-not-drive 19 (2.41%), park-outside 21 (2.66%).
  • Why the restriction changes the answer: the 790 includes 67 equipment, 11 tire and 4 child-restraint campaigns - one is an Evenflo child-seat recall - plus motorhome, bus, heavy-truck, motorcycle and trailer filers. Restricting to cars cuts the denominator by 62% and raises the severe share.
  • 2026 is not a record year on the car basis: 5.98% sits below 2023's 6.25%. It reads as a record only on the unrestricted basis.
  • Severity is a structured flag, not a word in the defect text: on the car basis a narrative-text parse catches only 12 of the 18 flagged campaigns and adds 2 false positives.
  • The two recalls driving this week's coverage - Honda 26V721000 and Hyundai 26V728000 - carry no do-not-drive or park-outside flag.

What does a do not drive recall mean on a used car?

A do-not-drive recall means the manufacturer and NHTSA judge the defect dangerous enough that the vehicle should not be operated at all until it is fixed - not merely scheduled for a dealer visit at the owner's convenience. It is the most severe advisory class NHTSA records, and on cars it is very rare: 5 of the 301 car and light-truck campaigns filed in 2026, about 1 in 60. An ordinary recall, by contrast, tells you to arrange a free repair and keep driving in the meantime. For a used-car buyer the practical point is that what an open recall on a used car means depends entirely on which class it falls in, and presence alone does not say.

The five 2026 car do-not-drive campaigns are small and mechanical rather than mass software events: a Ford brake-pedal linkage recall (26V090000, 15,965 units), a Ford lower ball joint (26V340000, 4,653), a Porsche wheel-stud recall (26V682000, 548), a GM transfer-case recall across Cadillac, Chevrolet and GMC (26V289000, 66) and a Subaru lower ball joint (26V685000, 8).

What is a park outside recall, and why is the car a fire risk even when off?

A park-outside recall is issued when a defect can start a fire with the engine off and the car unattended - a wiring short, an overheating motor, a leaking component. Owners are told to park away from buildings and other vehicles until the repair is done, because the risk is present while the car simply sits. It is the more common severe class on cars: 13 of the 18 in 2026. Eight of those 13 are electric-vehicle traction-battery or propulsion-system campaigns, which is the single clearest pattern in the severe set.

The largest severe campaign of the year is one of them: Jeep/FCA 26V363000, covering 1,076,999 2021-25 Wrangler, Wrangler 4xe and Gladiator vehicles whose hydraulic power-steering pump wiring can overheat and cause a fire even when parked. Buyers weighing should i buy a used jeep wrangler with an open recall should treat a park-outside flag as a fix-before-you-park instruction, and a fuller list of how to check if a used car has a park outside fire recall enumerates the active campaigns by model.

How rare are stop-driving recalls compared with all recalls?

Rarer than the usual framing, on every basis - and the choice of basis moves the number, so all three are printed here.

Two things to read off that table. First, the severe share rises as the basis narrows to cars, from 4.94% to 5.98% - the non-car filers are numerous and mostly routine, so including them dilutes the rate. Second, the do-not-drive and park-outside columns move in opposite directions: park-outside is proportionally much more a car phenomenon (4.32% against 2.66%), while do-not-drive is proportionally less so, because motorcycle, heavy-truck and equipment filers account for 11 of the 19 do-not-drive campaigns on the widest basis. Collapsing the two advisories into a single "do not drive" figure is what produces the familiar and wrong "1 in 20 recalls tells you to stop driving".

Is the finding an artifact of one mega-recall?

The campaign-count share is not; the unit-exposure share is. Both were recomputed on the car basis with the largest campaigns removed.

Remove largest campaign overall (Ford 26V104000, 4.38M units, routine)5.98% (18/300)6.63%

The share of campaigns carrying a severe advisory holds near 6% whichever single event you pull out. The share of units does not: strip out the one Jeep park-outside recall and exposure falls from 5.71% to 2.40%. That is why this page leads with the count-based number and treats the exposure figure as fragile. POTAFF is a census of how many vehicles a recall could touch at filing - not a count of cars still on the road, still unrepaired, or still for sale, and never a ranking of makes or models.

The two recalls in the news are both routine - which is the whole point

Two 2026 recalls drew "do not drive" coverage this week, and NHTSA's own flags say neither is in a severe class. This is the clearest illustration that recall presence is not the same as a stop-driving order.

Honda 26V721000 covers 2021-25 Acura TLX, 2022-24 Acura MDX and 2023-25 Honda Pilot; the electric power-steering control unit can enter a failsafe mode that illuminates warning lights and raises steering effort. The remedy is a dealer reprogramming of the EPS control unit. The flat file shows DO_NOT_DRIVE=No and PARK_OUTSIDE=No, and NHTSA's recalls API independently returns parkIt=false and parkOutSide=false. Hyundai 26V728000 (2019-22 Veloster N, 2019-23 Kona Electric, 37,901 units) is likewise a routine dealer software update and carries neither flag.

One limit on both, and on any same-week recall story: NHTSA's published record lags the press. On the day of retrieval the newest Part 573 report date in the file was 2026-11-06 and the highest populated campaign block in the recalls API was 26V730000, filed 2026-11-03 - roughly nine days behind. Both campaigns above sit comfortably below that frontier and are therefore verifiable against the agency's own record; a recall announced in the last week often is not.

What a VIN check can and can't tell you here

A VIN check can confirm that a recall is present on a specific car and how many; it cannot tell you whether that recall is the do-not-drive or park-outside class. That advisory is recorded in NHTSA's Part 573 report and campaign file, in the fields this analysis read - not in a VIN lookup, which returns presence only. To learn the class, open the campaign on nhtsa.gov/recalls or ask a franchised dealer.

QuestionA VIN check (Zilocar or NHTSA's free tool)Where the answer lives
Is a recall present on this car?Yes - presence and countVIN lookup
Is it a do-not-drive / park-outside recall?NoNHTSA campaign record (DO_NOT_DRIVE / PARK_OUTSIDE)
Has the recall been remedied/flashed?NoFranchised dealer
Is there an open NHTSA investigation on the model?NoNHTSA (a PE/EA probe is not a recall and maps to no VIN)

A recall's remedy status - open versus completed - is a separate question from its severity, and a VIN check settles neither; what recall closed means on a VIN check explains why presence and remedy are distinct records. Investigations are distinct again: whether an NHTSA investigation means a recall is coming is a model-level probability, not a per-VIN fact, and an open PE or EA probe must never be mapped to an individual car.

Where a VIN check earns its place is the history layer NHTSA has nothing on. A Zilocar VIN check screens for recall presence and also surfaces accident and damage records with airbag-deployment and severity, salvage and junk-auction records, odometer-rollback indicators, theft records, ownership history, and sales-listing history (past and current prices, mileage and days-on-market). It does not confirm a recall was remedied, track an investigation, or show per-unit firmware status - those stay with NHTSA and the dealer.

Method

Source file. NHTSA's ODI Recalls flat file FLAT_RCL_POST_2010.zip, downloaded from https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/ffdd/rcl/ on 2026-11-12 08:46 UTC (14,987,118 bytes; server Last-Modified: Thu, 12 Nov 2026 08:03:58 GMT). It expands to FLAT_RCL_POST_2010.txt, 313,571,938 bytes, member timestamp 2026-11-12 08:02:06; zipfile.testzip() returned None, so the archive is intact. 247,850 tab-delimited rows, 29 fields, no header.

Field identification. Field names and positions were confirmed against NHTSA's own layout file RCL.txt on the day rather than assumed from field order, because the last two fields are both three-character Yes/No advisories and reading the wrong one silently changes the study: field 28 is DO_NOT_DRIVE, field 29 is PARK_OUTSIDE. As a second check, the flags were compared with the recalls API's parkIt and parkOutSide fields on four campaigns - 26V721000 (No/No), 26V090000 (do-not-drive), 26V363000 (park-outside) and 26V682000 (do-not-drive) - with 4 of 4 agreement.

Universe, N, and why it is not all of the file. The file carries one row per campaign-and-make-and-model-year, so it is collapsed to one record per campaign number (CAMPNO, field 2); a campaign is flagged if any of its rows carries the flag. 790 campaigns have a 2026 Part 573 report-received date (RCDATE, field 16), spanning 2026-01-02 to 2026-11-06. That 790 is not a car universe: 67 are equipment recalls (RCLTYPECD='E'), 11 are tire recalls ('T') and 4 are child-restraint recalls ('C') - one of them an Evenflo child-seat recall - leaving 708 vehicle campaigns. And 'V' means vehicle, not car: it carries motorhomes (Winnebago, Tiffin, Grand Design, REV, Gulf Stream), buses (Crestline Coach), heavy trucks (Mack, Volvo Trucks, International, Kalmar), trailers (Great Dane, and the make ESCAPE, which is Escape Trailer Industries and not the Ford Escape), motorcycles (Yamaha) and fire apparatus (Rosenbauer). The headline basis therefore restricts to the 301 campaigns filed by an explicit allow-list of 42 named passenger-car and light-truck Part 573 filers, every one published in the frozen CSV so the boundary can be disputed rather than guessed at. It is the same allow-list used by our 2026-11-08 software-remedy analysis. A name-pattern classifier was not used; on that earlier analysis it leaked in both directions and was abandoned.

Aggregation. The published rate is a share of campaign counts, not of units. Exposure shares using POTAFF (field 12) are reported only in the robustness table and labelled fragile. POTAFF is the manufacturer's launch-time estimate of units potentially affected: it is not a count of cars still on the road, still unrepaired, or still in the US, so this file can support "how much exposure was opened" and cannot support "how many dangerous cars are out there", nor any model-level "worst car" ranking.

Reversal checks, reported either way.

  1. Does the headline survive the scope restriction? It changes, and in a direction worth stating. Either-severe share runs 4.94% on all record types, 5.08% on vehicles only and 5.98% on cars and light trucks, while the denominator falls 62% (790 to 301). Both wider bases are printed beside every car figure on this page.
  2. Do the two advisories degrade together? No - this is the check that most changes the story. Restricting to cars moves park-outside up (2.66% to 4.32%) and do-not-drive down (2.41% to 1.66%), because 11 of the 19 do-not-drive campaigns on the widest basis are motorcycle, heavy-truck or equipment filings. A combined "severe" figure therefore cannot be reported as a stop-driving rate.
  3. Is 2026 a record? No, on the honest basis. 2026's 5.98% is below 2023's 6.25% on the car basis, although 2026 does read as the highest year on the unrestricted basis. The claim "highest in the record" is not made.
  4. Is the series monotonic? No, and it is not characterised as a trend: the car basis falls in 2020, 2024 and at other points. Every year is published.
  5. Is the result an artifact of one mega-recall? The count share is not (5.98% to 5.67% when the largest severe campaign is removed); the exposure share is (5.71% to 2.40%). The count share is what the page leads with.
  6. Is the structured flag the right instrument? Yes. A narrative-text parse of the defect, consequence and corrective-action fields catches only 12 of the 18 flagged car campaigns and adds 2 campaigns that carry no flag, so text parsing both under- and over-counts.

Known limits. The advisory columns were codified in May 2025 and backfilled, so pre-2015 shares understate the past and cross-era comparison is weak. 2026 is a partial year through 6 November. NHTSA's published record ran roughly nine days behind the press on the retrieval date, so the most recent filings are absent. The flat file has no completion or remedy-status field, so nothing here says whether any recall was performed on any car. And the 42-filer allow-list is a judgement, not a fact: it is published in full for that reason.

Frequently asked questions

What does a do not drive recall mean on a used car?

It means the manufacturer and NHTSA judge the defect dangerous enough that the car should not be driven at all until it is repaired - not simply scheduled for a future dealer visit. It is the rarest advisory class NHTSA records: of 301 car and light-truck recall campaigns filed in 2026, just 5 (1.66%, about 1 in 60) carry it.

How rare are do-not-drive and park-outside recalls?

Rarer than the headlines suggest, and the two are not the same thing. Of 301 car and light-truck recall campaigns filed between 2 January and 6 November 2026, 18 (5.98%, about 1 in 17) carry either advisory - but only 5 (1.66%) say do not drive, while 13 (4.32%) say park outside. The other 283 campaigns (94.02%) are routine notify-and-schedule recalls. On NHTSA's unrestricted file the either-severe share reads 4.94% (39 of 790), but that denominator includes tire, child-restraint and equipment recalls as well as motorhomes, buses and heavy trucks.

What is a park outside recall?

A park-outside recall is issued when a defect can cause a fire even with the engine off, so owners are told to park away from buildings and other vehicles until the repair is done. It is the more common of the two severe classes on cars - 13 of the 18 severe car campaigns in 2026 - and it is not an instruction to stop driving, which is why collapsing the two into one "do not drive" number overstates how many recalls ground a car.

Is the Honda power-steering recall a do-not-drive recall?

No. Honda campaign 26V721000 (635,808 vehicles; 2021-25 Acura TLX, 2022-24 Acura MDX, 2023-25 Honda Pilot) carries DO_NOT_DRIVE=No and PARK_OUTSIDE=No in NHTSA's flat file, and the recalls API returns parkIt=false and parkOutSide=false. The remedy is a dealer reprogramming of the power-steering control unit.

Was 2026 the worst year for severe recall advisories?

No, and this is worth stating plainly because the unrestricted number suggests otherwise. On the car and light-truck basis 2026's 5.98% is below 2023's 6.25%, so 2026 is not a record. Only on the wider all-record-types basis does 2026 read as the highest year in the file. The advisory columns were also only codified in May 2025 and backfilled, so pre-2015 shares understate the past.

Should I buy a used car that has an open recall?

An open recall is not by itself a reason to walk away - repairs are free at a franchised dealer - but a do-not-drive or park-outside recall should be fixed before the car is driven or parked near structures. Confirm the class on the campaign record first, because a VIN check shows that a recall exists and not which class it is.
