What 156 NHTSA Suspension Complaints Reveal About Used Tesla Model 3 & Model Y (Before You Buy One)

On July 29, 2026, NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation opened Preliminary Evaluation PE26-006 into an estimated 1,198,300 Teslas — 2018-2020 Model 3 and 2021-2023 Model Y — after 156 owner complaints that the front lower lateral link can detach, potentially causing loss of vehicle directional control. This is an investigation, not a recall: as of August 3, 2026 there is no remedy, and no crashes, injuries, or fatalities were reported at opening.

Key facts

  • Campaign: Preliminary Evaluation PE26-006 (first ODI stage; not a recall, not an Engineering Analysis).
  • Opened: July 29, 2026, prompted by a review of owner complaints (VOQ Review). Broad news coverage followed on July 31, 2026.
  • Vehicles: 2018-2020 Tesla Model 3 and 2021-2023 Tesla Model Y; estimated population 1,198,300.
  • Defect mechanism: the front lower lateral link detaches, potentially causing loss of vehicle directional control; once it fails the vehicle "may not be drivable," often requiring a tow.
  • Complaints: 156, all from ODI owner complaints (0 from the manufacturer, 0 from early-warning data).
  • Harm reported at opening: 0 crashes, 0 fires, 0 injuries, 0 fatalities (as of the 07/29/2026 opening resume; an open investigation can accrue new incidents).
  • Warning: NHTSA says that "in most cases, there was no advance warning" before failure; some owners cite noises beforehand, but the car gives no visual warning.
  • Remedy: none. No recall, fix, or timeline exists for PE26-006.
  • Related prior recalls: 21V-835 (SUSPENSION:CRITICAL FASTENERS) and 23V-235 (SUSPENSION:FRONT). NHTSA states the PE26-006 failures exceed both recalls' scope and do not appear related to those earlier production issues.

Is there a recall on the used Tesla Model 3 or Model Y suspension yet?

No. There is no recall for this issue as of August 3, 2026. PE26-006 is a Preliminary Evaluation — the earliest stage of a federal defect investigation. From here it can close with no action, be upgraded to an Engineering Analysis, or eventually lead to a recall. Which of those happens, and when, is unknown, so a used buyer today cannot assume a manufacturer-funded fix will exist. Track remedy status only through NHTSA and Tesla, not through any history report.

Which model years and how many vehicles are covered?

PE26-006 covers 2018-2020 Tesla Model 3 and 2021-2023 Tesla Model Y, an estimated 1,198,300 vehicles. That six-model-year window is set by NHTSA in the investigation's opening resume. Being a given model year does not mean an individual car is defective; it means the vehicle falls within the population NHTSA is examining. No VIN tool assigns a specific car to this scope.

Which model years actually cluster in the complaints?

The link-detachment reports concentrate at the two ends of the age range: the oldest Model 3 (2018) and the newest in-scope Model Y (2023). That pattern comes from an independent Zilocar analysis of NHTSA's public ODI complaint database, pulled August 3, 2026, covering every complaint record for the six in-scope configurations — 4,990 records in total. Filtering complaint summaries for front-lower-lateral-link and lower-control-arm language surfaced 305 link mentions, of which 144 describe an actual detachment, separation, collapse, or failure. That 144 is close to but not the same as NHTSA's curated count of 156; the two are produced by different methods (a keyword filter of full public records versus NHTSA's manual complaint curation) and corroborate each other rather than being identical figures.

At what mileage do the links tend to fail?

Among the 31 detachment complaints that included a machine-parseable odometer reading, the median failure mileage was about 35,000 miles, with the middle 50% spanning roughly 24,700 to 58,000 miles (full range 7,000 to about 106,867 miles; mean ~42,200). Roughly 65% of these reported failures fell in the 20,000-60,000-mile band — well inside a typical used-car ownership window and often past the original basic warranty.

How the earlier recalls (21V-835 and 23V-235) fit in

Two prior suspension recalls exist and can appear on a VIN in scope, but NHTSA says the PE26-006 failures exceed both. Recall 21V-835 (component SUSPENSION:CRITICAL FASTENERS) addressed a production issue; the PE26-006 document scopes it as 2,791 MY2019-2021 Model 3 vehicles, while NHTSA's recalls database summarizes 21V835000 as "certain 2020-2021 Model Y and 2019-2021 Model 3." Recall 23V-235 (SUSPENSION:FRONT, 23V235000) covered 422 MY2018-2019 Model 3 vehicles that had experienced failures similar to 21V-835. NHTSA states the current investigation's failures do not appear related to those earlier production issues.

The distinction matters for buyers: PE26-006 will never show up as a recall in any VIN tool because it is an open investigation. Only the older 21V-835 and 23V-235 recalls — where a specific VIN is in scope — appear as recall presence.

What a VIN check can and can't tell you here

A VIN check screens the car's recall presence and history; it does not track this investigation or prove a repair happened. Use it to verify the exact car, then rely on NHTSA and Tesla for remedy and investigation status.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a recall on used Tesla Model 3 or Model Y suspension yet, or just an investigation? As of August 3, 2026, there is no recall for this issue. NHTSA opened Preliminary Evaluation PE26-006 on July 29, 2026 — the first, investigatory stage. It may close with no action, become an Engineering Analysis, or lead to a recall; none of those is decided yet.

Which model years are covered by the NHTSA suspension probe? PE26-006 covers 2018-2020 Tesla Model 3 and 2021-2023 Tesla Model Y, an estimated 1,198,300 vehicles. The scope is defined by NHTSA, not by any VIN lookup tool.

At what mileage do the Tesla front lower lateral links tend to fail? Among 31 detachment complaints that listed an odometer, the median failure mileage was about 35,000 miles, with the middle 50% between roughly 24,700 and 58,000 miles. This describes reported cases, not a population failure rate.

Will a VIN check tell me if a specific used Tesla is affected by the suspension investigation? No. VIN tools show recalls, not open investigations. Neither Zilocar nor NHTSA's free lookup maps a VIN into the PE26-006 population. A VIN check can show whether the older lateral-link recalls (21V-835, 23V-235) are present on that car.

Does a Tesla suspension failure mean the car was in an accident? Not necessarily. NHTSA is investigating link detachment as a potential defect, distinct from crash damage. A vehicle-history report showing prior accident, airbag-deployment, or salvage-auction records helps you separate a defect concern from collision-related suspension damage.

If there is no recall, who pays to fix a failed suspension link out of warranty? With no recall in place, there is no defined remedy or manufacturer-funded fix for PE26-006, so an out-of-warranty repair would typically fall to the owner. Confirm remedy status only through NHTSA's recall VIN lookup and Tesla service records.

How do I know if a used Tesla already had the earlier suspension recall done? A VIN check flags whether recall records like 21V-835 or 23V-235 exist on that car, but not whether the repair was completed. Confirm completed-remedy status through NHTSA's recall VIN lookup and Tesla's service history for that VIN.
