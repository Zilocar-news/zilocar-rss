Which Used Cars Rack Up the Most 'Stalled While Driving' Complaints? We Counted the NHTSA Data

A car quitting in traffic is the failure every driver fears — and it is what Ford's late-August 2026 Mustang recall (NHTSA campaign 26V547000, 148,663 vehicles) is about. Setting the headline aside, we counted the pattern across the database: of 1,617,067 distinct NHTSA complaints, 172,856 (10.7%) describe a stall or loss of motive power. A VIN check flags a recall on file and accident and salvage history — not whether one specific car has ever stalled.

Key facts

  • Recall (news hook): NHTSA campaign 26V547000 / Ford recall 26C40; 148,663 vehicles, 100% Ford Mustang, model years 2024–2026 (7th-gen S650), built at Flat Rock, MI between Sept 7, 2022 and June 9, 2026.
  • Component: ELECTRICAL SYSTEM:WIRING — a wiring/ground-terminal defect, not an engine or piston failure.
  • Defect: engine-compartment wiring-harness ground terminals can develop fatigue cracks from vibration and fracture; root cause per the Part 573 report is heat-shrink adhesive intruding into the ground-eyelet bolt-clearance area, bending the eyelet and raising bending stress.
  • Consequence: depending on which ground fails, loss of drive/motive power while driving, or loss of headlights, washer, A/C, or cooling fan (cited as noncompliance with FMVSS 108 and FMVSS 104).
  • Scope: NHTSA estimates ~1% of the population actually has the defect. Report received 2026-08-25; VINs searchable 2026-08-28; interim owner letters mailed ~Aug 31, 2026; remedy parts not available until ~March 2027.
  • Our computation: 172,856 distinct stall/loss-of-power complaints = 10.7% of all NHTSA complaints; 48.7% carry the VEHICLE SPEED CONTROL component. The Mustang itself sits mid-pack at 8.8% stall-share — the recall is the freshness hook, not the finding.

Is the Mustang recall an engine failure? What the filing actually says

No. Early social chatter conflated it with engine or piston failure, but NHTSA campaign 26V547000 is coded ELECTRICAL SYSTEM:WIRING. Engine-compartment wiring-harness ground connections can develop fatigue cracks from repeated vibration and fracture; the Part 573 report attributes this to heat-shrink adhesive intruding into the ground-eyelet bolt-clearance area during assembly, bending the eyelet and raising bending stress. Depending on which ground fails, the driver can lose drive power while moving, or lose headlights, washer, A/C or the engine-cooling fan. The remedy replaces the ground terminals with redesigned stepped-neck standoff eyelet pigtails, free of charge — but remedy parts are not expected until around March 2027, so many affected Mustangs will show an open, un-remedied recall in the meantime. It is the same nameplate covered by earlier used Ford Mustang problems to check before buying.

What a VIN check can and can't tell you here

A VIN check is a screening tool, not a substitute for NHTSA and dealer confirmation. It is strongest on history — accidents, salvage, odometer, ownership and listings — and on flagging whether a recall exists at all. It cannot show you a car's stalling complaints or whether a recall was actually fixed.

Frequently asked questions

How do I check if a used car has a history of stalling before I buy it? There is no VIN field that returns a stall history. Read NHTSA's free complaint database for that model and model-year, check nhtsa.gov/recalls by VIN for open recalls, run a vehicle-history report for accidents, salvage and odometer flags, and get a pre-purchase inspection with a test drive.

Does a VIN check show whether a car has actually stalled? No. It shows whether the car has a recall on file plus history records (accidents, salvage, odometer, ownership, listings). It does not reveal individual stalling complaints — those are filed against a model, not a VIN — and it does not show whether a recall was remedied.

Which used cars get the most "stalled while driving" complaints? By raw volume in our 2026-09-05 count: Ford Escape (4,881), Ford F-150 (4,515), Jeep Grand Cherokee (3,668). Normalized to each model's own stall-share, discontinued models lead: Ford Freestyle (49.3%), Jeep Commander (33.4%), Volvo S80 (33.0%).

Is the 2024–2026 Ford Mustang recall an engine failure? No. Campaign 26V547000 (Ford 26C40) is an ELECTRICAL SYSTEM:WIRING defect — ground terminals can fatigue-crack and fracture, causing loss of drive power or loss of lights and cooling fan. It is not an engine or piston failure.

What does it mean when a used car has a lot of NHTSA complaints — are those confirmed defects? They are unverified consumer reports, not confirmed defects. Counts are influenced by sales volume, model age and media attention. Use them as a signal to investigate a specific VIN, not as proof a given car is defective.

Why do old models like the Ford Freestyle top the stall list when the Mustang made headlines? The Mustang is the freshness hook, not the finding — it sits mid-pack at 8.8% stall-share. Discontinued mid-2000s models rise on the normalized list because their smaller complaint files are disproportionately about losing power.

Should I avoid a used car just because its model is high on a stalling list? Not automatically. The list ranks models, not individual cars, and complaints are unverified. A specific well-maintained example can be fine; a clean inspection, test drive, recall check and history report matter more than the model's rank.

If a car lost power because of a recall, will that show up on a vehicle-history report? Not as a stalling event. History reports and VIN checks surface recall presence, accidents, salvage and odometer records — not complaints, and not whether a recall was remedied. Confirm remedy status on NHTSA's VIN lookup or with a dealer.

Method

Source file. NHTSA Office of Defects Investigation Complaints flat file, FLAT_CMPL.txt, extracted from FLAT_CMPL.zip. The archive carries a file timestamp of 2026-09-04; we downloaded and processed it on 2026-09-05. NHTSA republishes this file continuously, so a later download will not reproduce these counts exactly — the frozen CSVs linked in Sources will.

Universe. 2,242,704 complaint-component rows. The file stores one row per cited component, so a single owner report naming two systems appears twice. We deduplicate on ODINO (the owner-report id) to 1,617,067 distinct complaints, and every figure in this article is a count of distinct complaints, not of component rows, vehicles or owners.

Filter. A complaint enters the stall universe when either (a) field 12 (COMPDESC) begins with VEHICLE SPEED CONTROL, or (b) its free-text narrative (field 20, CDESCR) matches a stall/loss-of-power expression — stall, shut off/down, lost or loss of power, cut out, died, quit, power loss, no power — and is corroborated by motion wording (while driving, at speed, in traffic, on the highway, mph, moving). Narrative matches that are pure oil-consumption or engine-teardown reports with no motion wording are excluded. That yields 172,856 distinct stall complaints, 10.7% of the file, of which 84,195 (48.7%) carry the VEHICLE SPEED CONTROL component outright.

Cleanup and aggregation. Make and model strings are trimmed and upper-cased and grouped as a make + model pair; no other normalisation is applied. Two rankings are published: (1) raw stall-complaint count per nameplate, floored at 50 stall complaints (411 nameplates); (2) stall-share — stall complaints as a share of that nameplate's entire complaint history — floored at 800 total complaints and 40 stall complaints so that tiny-volume models cannot top the list on a handful of reports (309 nameplates). Both full tables are in the frozen CSVs.

The check most likely to reverse this, and its result. NHTSA's MODELTXT is a free-text label, so one vehicle can occupy several strings — and the Ford Escape leads the F-150 by only 366 complaints, so a split label could flip the headline. We ran a consolidation sweep across all 411 ranked nameplates, grouping every sub-variant into its base nameplate and checking model-year overlap. The top two do not change, and the Escape's lead widens from 366 to 577: Escape + Escape Hybrid/HEV/PHEV = 5,213; F-150 + F150 + Lightning BEV + Hybrid = 4,636. Below that the order does move — the Toyota Camry (+ Camry Hybrid/Solara = 3,935) rises from 4th to 3rd past the Jeep Grand Cherokee (+ 4xe/L = 3,756), and the Honda Accord (2,407) enters the top ten ahead of the Jeep Cherokee (2,285). We publish the unmerged table because the hybrid and plug-in variants overlap model years with their base nameplate, which makes them genuinely different powertrains rather than a renamed nameplate; punctuation-only aliases, which are true label splits, exist further down (Toyota 4RUNNER/4 RUNNER = 335 merged, Saab 9-5/95 = 122) but none rank near the top. The headline survives the check; ranks 3 through 10 are sensitive to the merge rule, and the deltas are stated here so anyone can re-rank either way.

Known limits. (1) NHTSA complaints are self-reported and unverified — they are not confirmed defects. (2) The file carries no sales or registration denominator, so nothing here is a per-vehicle failure rate; this is a ranking of complaint composition, and the share cut is each model's share of its own complaints, not stalls per car on the road. (3) The narrative regex is a screen, not a human read: no power and power loss can appear in non-drivetrain contexts such as power windows or accessory outlets, so 172,856 is approximate — the 84,195 component-tagged subset is the conservative floor, and both are published. (4) The share table structurally favours older, discontinued models whose complaint files are small and symptom-concentrated. (5) Complaint volume tracks how many cars were sold, how old they are, and how much press a defect received; a recall in the news inflates its model's counts. (6) Counts are distinct complaints, not vehicles or owners.
