Used Nissan Rogue VC-Turbo Engine: What NHTSA Complaints Reveal Before You Buy

Across model years 2021-2026, the third-generation Nissan Rogue's 2023 model year is the decisive engine-complaint outlier: NHTSA holds 665 total complaints for MY2023, more than 2021 (271) and 2022 (246) combined, with 437 tagged to engine or propulsion. Three 1.5L VC-Turbo recalls apply (25V437, 26V080, 26V081). A VIN check can confirm whether a specific Rogue sits under those campaigns, but it cannot prove the repair was performed.

Key facts

  • Engine scope: The US Rogue's 1.5L VC-Turbo (KR15DDT, 3-cylinder, 201 hp / 225 lb-ft) began in model year 2022. The 2021 Rogue uses the 2.5L PR25DD naturally aspirated four and is not a VC-Turbo car.
  • Most-complained year: 2023 Rogue, 665 total complaints (retrieved 2026-07-30); 437 engine/propulsion-tagged; 102 narratives cite stalling, power loss, or seizure.
  • Recall 25V437000 (filed 2025-06-27): 2021-2024 Rogue plus 2019-2020 Altima, 2019-2022 QX50, 2022 QX55; 443,899 units; engine-bearing manufacturing defect risking failure and fire; remedy is an ECM software reprogram; owner letters mailed 2026-04-08.
  • Recall 26V080000 (filed 2026-02-12): 2023-2025 Rogue; 323,917 units; high oil temperatures degrade oil, bearings seize; remedy adds oil-pan inspection and engine replacement if needed.
  • Recall 26V081000 (filed 2026-02-12): 2024-2025 Rogue; 318,781 units; electronic throttle-body gears break, causing loss of drive power; remedy inspects/replaces the throttle body.
  • Warranty: Nissan extended VC-Turbo powertrain coverage to 10 years / 120,000 miles; it transfers by VIN.
  • Verification limit: Recall data shows presence, not remedy status. Confirm each recall is closed with a Nissan dealer and NHTSA.gov (Rogue VINs live from 2026-02-27).

Which used Rogue years have the 1.5L VC-Turbo, and which is riskiest?

The 1.5L VC-Turbo appears in model-year 2022 and later Rogues; the 2021 Rogue is a different, naturally aspirated 2.5L car. Among the turbo years, 2023 carries by far the most engine-related NHTSA complaints. That makes a used 2023 VC-Turbo Rogue the model year that warrants the closest pre-purchase scrutiny.

The engine change is documented: Nissan's newsroom (Nov 17, 2021) confirmed the all-new 1.5L VC-Turbo arrived for the 2022 Rogue, replacing the 2021 model's 2.5L PR25DD. The assignment premise of a "VC-Turbo Rogue (2021+)" is inaccurate — the turbo cars are 2022+. (Note: recall 25V437 lists a "2021-2024 Rogue" population; the 2.0L VC-Turbo in that same recall belongs to the Altima and Infiniti QX50/QX55, not the Rogue's 1.5L.)

Why does the 2023 Rogue draw so many more engine complaints?

The 2023 Rogue is an outlier on both raw volume and engine share. Of its 665 total complaints, 437 are engine/propulsion-tagged (66%), and its top component category is ENGINE (258), followed by ENGINE AND ENGINE COOLING (113) and FUEL/PROPULSION (95). By comparison, 2022 shows the highest engine share at 79% (195 of 246), but far lower raw volume.

What actually goes wrong — stalling, oil breakdown, bearings, or throttle body?

Owner narratives and the recalls describe distinct but related failure modes: engine-bearing failure, oil breakdown leading to bearing seizure, and a separate electronic throttle-body defect. The first two threaten catastrophic engine failure and fire; the third causes loss of drive power. Reported symptoms include a sudden "Engine Malfunction - Power Reduced" warning, stalling, engine knock, and metal shavings in the oil pan, with engine-replacement quotes near $9,000.

Can a VIN check tell me if the recall was fixed?

No. A VIN check — whether NHTSA's free tool or Zilocar's — surfaces whether a Rogue is subject to a recall campaign; it does not confirm the remedy was performed. To know that the ECM reprogram, oil-pan inspection, or engine replacement was actually completed, you must run the VIN on NHTSA.gov (Rogue VINs searchable from 2026-02-27) and have a Nissan dealer confirm each open recall is closed. Do not treat recall presence, or its absence from a third-party report, as proof of repair.

How do I check accident, salvage, and listing history before buying?

Start with the free authoritative tools, then layer history data. NHTSA.gov gives recall status by VIN and a Nissan dealer confirms remedy and extended-warranty eligibility. Because a stalled or failed-engine Rogue may have been towed, crashed, totaled, or quickly flipped, a vehicle-history report adds the accident, salvage-auction, odometer, ownership, and sales-listing signals those official tools do not carry. A Zilocar VIN check is one option here alongside NHTSA's free lookup: it screens for recall presence across the three VC-Turbo campaigns and surfaces the history red flags that matter most for an engine-failure-prone model.

Frequently asked questions

Which used Nissan Rogue years have the 1.5L VC-Turbo engine? US-market Rogues use the 1.5L VC-Turbo (KR15DDT, 3-cylinder, 201 hp) from model year 2022 onward. The 2021 Rogue uses the older 2.5L PR25DD naturally aspirated four, not the VC-Turbo. So 2022-2025 Rogues are the turbo cars covered by the engine recalls.

Which Nissan Rogue year has the most engine complaints? The 2023 Rogue is the clear outlier: 665 total NHTSA complaints, more than 2021 (271) and 2022 (246) combined, with 437 engine/propulsion-tagged and 102 stall or power-loss narratives. Counts retrieved 2026-07-30.

What are the three VC-Turbo Rogue recalls? 25V437000 covers 2021-2024 Rogues (plus some Altima/Infiniti) for an engine-bearing defect. 26V080000 covers 2023-2025 Rogues for bearing seizure from oil breakdown. 26V081000 covers 2024-2025 Rogues for a throttle-body defect causing power loss.

Can a VIN check confirm a Rogue's recall was actually fixed? No. A Zilocar VIN check surfaces recall presence and count, the same signal as NHTSA's free lookup, but not remedy status. Run the VIN on NHTSA.gov and have a Nissan dealer confirm each recall is closed before buying.

Does the 10-year/120,000-mile engine warranty transfer to a second owner? Yes. Nissan extended VC-Turbo powertrain coverage to 120 months / 120,000 miles, covering MY2021-2024 and, via a March 2026 extension, MY2023-2025. It transfers by VIN, but confirm it is active with a Nissan dealer using the specific VIN.

Is the 2021 non-turbo Rogue safer to buy than the turbo years? The 2021 Rogue uses the 2.5L naturally aspirated engine and is not in the VC-Turbo bearing or throttle-body recalls. It drew 271 complaints with a lower 31% engine share and avoids the specific VC-Turbo failure modes — though every used car still needs a full history check.

What warning signs point to VC-Turbo engine trouble on a test drive? Watch for an "Engine Malfunction - Power Reduced" warning, sudden stalling or power loss, engine knock, and metal shavings in the oil. Owners report these before bearing seizure or turbo failure, with engine-replacement quotes near $9,000.
