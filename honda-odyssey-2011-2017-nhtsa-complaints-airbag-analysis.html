We Counted the NHTSA Complaints on the 2011-2017 Honda Odyssey: The Model-Year Pattern Behind the New Airbag Petition

In July 2026, NHTSA confirmed it is reviewing a defect petition, filed May 30, 2026, asking it to investigate 806,963 model-year 2011-2017 Honda Odyssey minivans over 10 complaints of airbags deploying while driving. This is a petition under review, not a recall and not yet a granted investigation. A VIN check shows a used van's existing recalls and accident history, but cannot flag this petition or confirm any repair.

To put the news in context, we counted every NHTSA complaint on the 2011-2017 Odyssey by model year and by component. Below is the model-year pattern, an explainer on how a petition differs from a recall, and an honest guide to what a VIN check does and does not prove here.

Key facts

  • Action type: Defect petition under 49 CFR Part 552, received and under review by NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation. It is not a recall and had not been granted as a formal investigation as of publication. No public petition or investigation number was locatable as of July 24, 2026.
  • Scope of the petition: 806,963 vehicles (about 807,000), model-year 2011-2017 Honda Odyssey, the fourth-generation van.
  • Petition dated: May 30, 2026. Widely reported July 14-16, 2026.
  • Trigger: Exactly 10 complaints already in NHTSA's database alleging inadvertent airbag deployment while driving.
  • Alleged mechanism: Airbags can deploy without a sufficiently severe triggering event (no impact, rollover, or G-force threshold breach), with Diagnostic Trouble Codes conflicting with Supplemental Restraint System (SRS) data.
  • Honda's statement: Honda says it "is committed to the safety of our customers" and "respects this process and will await the agency's findings before making any additional comment."
  • Related recall (separate action): Recall 26V227, April 2026, covers 440,830 model-year 2018-2022 (fifth-generation) Odyssey vans for side and side-curtain airbags that can deploy unexpectedly. The 2011-2017 vans were excluded from that recall.
  • Our dataset: 1,492 NHTSA complaints on the 2011-2017 Odyssey; the 2014 model year draws the most (307). Airbags rank seventh among components (43, about 2.9%).

Is there a recall on the 2011-2017 Honda Odyssey airbags, or just a petition?

It is a petition, not a recall. The July 2026 news is that NHTSA received and is reviewing a defect petition (dated May 30, 2026) requesting an investigation into 806,963 model-year 2011-2017 Odyssey minivans. NHTSA can grant the petition, which would open a formal investigation, or deny it. As of publication no recall covers these specific vans for inadvertent airbag deployment.

The petition essentially asks NHTSA to extend the airbag scrutiny already applied to newer vans onto the older, uncovered ones. In April 2026 Honda issued recall 26V227 for 440,830 model-year 2018-2022 (fifth-generation) Odyssey vans, where a software programming error can cause side and side-curtain airbags to deploy unexpectedly over potholes, speed bumps, or road debris. The remedy is a free reprogram or replacement of the SRS ECU, with owner letters mailed May 27, 2026. That recall excluded the 2011-2017 (fourth-generation) vans, which is why the petition exists.

Which Honda Odyssey model year has the most complaints?

The 2014 Odyssey draws the most NHTSA complaints of the generation at 307, ahead of 2016 (263) and 2012 (241). The 2017 model year has the fewest at 39. Raw complaint counts are not normalized by the number of vehicles sold or still on the road, so a low count is not proof of reliability.

Read the 2017 figure with caution: it was the short, low-volume final year of the fourth generation, so fewer vehicles on the road produce fewer complaints. For that reason we describe 2014 as the "most-complained-about" year rather than the "worst," and we do not claim 2017 is the "most reliable" from raw counts alone. A used shopper should weigh a specific van's condition and history over generation-wide tallies.

How many airbag-deployment complaints are there, and is it widespread?

The petitioner cites 10 complaints of inadvertent airbag deployment while driving as the trigger for the requested investigation. Our own count of the "AIR BAGS" component for the 2011-2017 Odyssey finds 43 complaints total (67 if combination tags such as "AIR BAGS, SEAT BELTS" are included), of which about 23 describe an inadvertent or unexpected deployment by our keyword screen.

These figures measure different things and should not be read as the same number. The petitioner's 10 is its own tally of a specific allegation; our 43 is the defensible official-classification count of airbag-component complaints; and our 23 is an approximate keyword screen of complaint summaries, not an agency count. Within our screen, inadvertent-deployment reports peak in the 2015 and 2016 model years (5 each). In absolute terms the airbag issue is a small slice of the record, but by nature it is safety-critical, which is why NHTSA is reviewing the petition.

What is the difference between a defect petition, an investigation, and a recall?

A defect petition, an investigation, and a recall are three distinct stages, and only the last one forces a repair. A petition under 49 CFR Part 552 is a public request asking NHTSA to look into a suspected defect; the agency reviews it and decides to grant or deny. An investigation (a Preliminary Evaluation, or PE) is NHTSA formally studying the issue. A recall under 49 CFR Part 573 is a manufacturer's legal obligation to notify owners and repair a confirmed defect for free.

The Odyssey airbag story spans all three stages across two generations, which is easy to conflate. The table below separates them.

Which Odyssey recalls can you actually check by VIN right now?

Several genuine recalls already apply to 2011-2017 Odyssey vans and can be checked by VIN today, but none of them is the July 2026 airbag petition. The petition is a request under review, not a recall, and cannot be looked up by VIN anywhere.

Existing recalls a buyer can verify by VIN include:

  • 17V725: second-row seat may not latch (2011-2017).
  • 16V933 / 16V932 / 18V170: second-row seat release-lever issues (2011-2016, 2016, and 2014-2016 respectively).
  • 13V016: an air-bag recall covering 2011-2013 Odyssey (and 2009-2013 Pilot).
  • 14V175: 2014 Odyssey side-curtain airbag shorting terminal. Note this concerns a risk of failure to deploy, the opposite of the petition's inadvertent-deployment allegation.
  • 13V143: automatic transmission (2012-2013).
  • 23V858: Denso fuel-pump recall (2013-2023 Honda vehicles).

To check a specific van, start with NHTSA's free VIN lookup at NHTSA.gov/recalls, which returns open recalls by VIN. A Zilocar VIN report shows the same recall-presence data by VIN. Neither tool confirms whether a listed recall was actually repaired; for that, contact a Honda dealer with the VIN.

What a VIN check can and can't tell you here

A VIN check is useful for this story in a specific, bounded way: it reveals a used Odyssey's documented history and existing recalls, but it cannot see into NHTSA's petition or investigation process. Knowing that boundary keeps you from over-trusting or dismissing a report.

One critical distinction: a VIN report's "airbag deployment" field reflects a prior crash deployment recorded in the vehicle's history. It is not evidence of the alleged inadvertent-deployment defect and does not indicate the van is part of the petition. Use it to see whether this specific van already had an airbag event in a collision, not as a read on the NHTSA case.
