Used Tesla Headlight Recall: What to Check by VIN Before You Buy (2026)

TL;DR: On 2026-07-16 NHTSA denied Tesla's petition for inconsequential noncompliance (Docket NHTSA-2024-0019; Federal Register document 2026-14506, published 2026-07-17), legally forcing a free headlight recall remedy on roughly 19,917 vehicles — Tesla Model 3 (2017-2023) and Model Y (2020-2023) only. The defect is over-bright low beams exceeding federal limits. A VIN check can flag that a car falls in the affected build window and surface open-recall presence, but it cannot prove the fix was performed.

Key facts

  • What happened: NHTSA denied Tesla's petition to avoid a recall fix on 2026-07-16; the denial legally obligates a free remedy under 49 U.S.C. 30118 and 30120.
  • Documents: Docket NHTSA-2024-0019 (Notice 2); Federal Register document 2026-14506, published 2026-07-17.
  • Vehicles affected: approximately 19,917.
  • Models and years: Tesla Model 3 (MY 2017-2023) and Tesla Model Y (MY 2020-2023) — not Model S or Model X.
  • Build window: 2017-10-27 through 2023-12-24.
  • Defect: low-beam output exceeds the maximum photometric intensity in FMVSS No. 108, paragraph S10.14.6 — measured up to 230.1 candela versus the 125 cd limit (up to 105.1 cd over).
  • Supplier: Marelli Automotive Lighting.
  • Risk: NHTSA concluded the excess brightness could cause veiling glare to the Tesla driver and other road users in rain, snow, and fog. Tesla reported zero complaints, accidents, or injuries tied to the noncompliance.
  • Remedy: free fix mandated; method (software vs. hardware) and owner-notification schedule not yet announced.
  • Campaign number: none published at research time — do not rely on a campaign code yet.

Which used Teslas are actually covered by this headlight recall?

Only two models are covered: the Tesla Model 3 (2017-2023) and the Tesla Model Y (2020-2023). Together they account for about 19,917 vehicles built between 2017-10-27 and 2023-12-24. The Model S and Model X are not part of this action, despite early reporting that lumped all four models together.

Because the Model Y did not exist before 2020, its affected range begins with the 2020 model year, while the full 2017-2023 span applies to the Model 3.

What exactly is wrong with the headlights — too dim or too bright?

The headlights are too bright, not too dim. The low-beam (lower beam) output of the integral-beam headlamps exceeds the maximum photometric intensity permitted by paragraph S10.14.6 of FMVSS No. 108. In the regulated zone, the maximum allowed is 125 candela, but affected lamps measured as high as 230.1 cd — up to 105.1 cd over the limit.

Supplier Marelli Automotive Lighting tested 25 right-hand and 25 left-hand lamps; the trade outlet Teslarati reported measured maxima of 136.2-230.1 cd (right-hand) and 117.5-160.3 cd (left-hand). NHTSA concluded that in rain, snow, and fog the excess output could produce veiling glare affecting both the Tesla driver and oncoming road users. This is a glare/over-bright noncompliance, not a failed or dim headlamp.

How did a two-year-old issue become a recall in July 2026?

The underlying noncompliance is not new; the mandate is. Tesla filed its Part 573 noncompliance report dated 2024-03-15 and, in early April 2024, petitioned NHTSA for a decision of "inconsequential noncompliance" — an argument that the defect posed no meaningful safety consequence and therefore should not require a recall. Tesla amended the petition on 2024-05-03.

NHTSA published receipt of the petition on 2024-07-26 (89 FR 60682) and opened a comment window; two public comments arrived, at least one describing severe glare from these Teslas. On 2026-07-16 NHTSA denied the petition (Notice 2), published 2026-07-17. The denial ends the process and triggers Tesla's federal obligation to notify owners and provide a free remedy.

How do I check whether a specific used Tesla is included?

Check by VIN, and check more than one source. Start with NHTSA's free VIN lookup at nhtsa.gov/recalls, which is the authoritative source for open-recall status. You can also run the VIN through a history service such as a Zilocar VIN check, which screens for open-recall presence alongside the car's accident, salvage-auction, odometer, and listing history. For the definitive remedy status on this specific campaign, contact a Tesla service center with the VIN.

One timing caveat is critical here: because the recall was only mandated on 2026-07-16 and Tesla had not yet filed its amended remedy plan at research time, this recall may not yet appear in NHTSA's public VIN database or any VIN tool. Recall-presence screening — NHTSA's and any third party's alike — can lag the news by weeks until the campaign is populated. Re-check as the remedy rolls out.

What a VIN check can and can't tell you here

A VIN check can confirm that a used Model 3 or Model Y falls inside the affected 2017-2023 / 2020-2023 build window and can surface whether an open recall is present or counted against the VIN — the same signal NHTSA's free tool provides. It cannot confirm whether this specific headlight recall was remedied, cannot show open-versus-closed remedy status, and cannot reveal firmware or per-unit dealer service detail. Remedy confirmation belongs to NHTSA's VIN lookup and a Tesla service center.

Is this the same as the other Tesla recalls I've heard about?

No. This over-bright headlight action on the Model 3 and Model Y is distinct from other Tesla recalls reported elsewhere, such as the Cybertruck wheel-stud issue, Model 3 driver-assistance/accident matters, and a Model Y weight-label correction. Each has its own scope, models, and campaign paperwork; do not treat them as interchangeable.

Frequently asked questions

Which Teslas are covered by the 2026 headlight recall? Only the Tesla Model 3 (2017-2023) and Tesla Model Y (2020-2023) — about 19,917 vehicles built between 2017-10-27 and 2023-12-24. Model S and Model X are not affected.

Are the recalled headlights too dim or too bright? Too bright. Low-beam output exceeds the FMVSS 108 S10.14.6 maximum — measured up to 230.1 candela against a 125 cd limit — which NHTSA found could cause veiling glare in rain, snow, and fog.

Will NHTSA's free VIN lookup show this recall yet? Possibly not. The recall was mandated on 2026-07-16 and Tesla had not filed its remedy plan at research time, so it may not appear in NHTSA's VIN database or any VIN tool for weeks. Re-check as the remedy rolls out.

Does a VIN report prove the recall was already fixed? No. A VIN report can show that an open recall exists or is counted — the same signal as NHTSA's free tool — but cannot confirm the headlight fix was completed. Confirm remedy status with NHTSA's VIN lookup or a Tesla service center.

What is the NHTSA campaign number for this recall? None was publicly available at research time. The action stems from Docket NHTSA-2024-0019 and Federal Register document 2026-14506; a searchable campaign code will appear only after Tesla files its amended Part 573 remedy plan.

Is the fix free, and is it software or hardware? The remedy is mandated and free to owners. The method — over-the-air adjustment versus physical headlamp replacement or re-aim — was not stated by NHTSA or Tesla at research time.

Should I negotiate or walk away if the recall is still open? An open recall is not by itself a reason to walk away, since the fix is free. Verify it by VIN, factor unresolved status into your offer, and plan to have Tesla complete the remedy after purchase.
