RPO Codes by VIN: What a GM Option Code Is, Where the Sticker Is, and Why a VIN Decode Won't List Them

A VIN decode cannot return a GM car's RPO list. A VIN gives the build — make, model, year, trim, engine, drivetrain and GVWR class — but not the factory option list. The raw RPO (Regular Production Option) codes live on the car's SPID sticker, the 2018+ B-pillar QR code, or GM's build records. Verified live on 2026-11-10, NHTSA's vPIC decoder returned no RPO field and no axle ratio — so the honest answer is: partially.

RPO codes by VIN: the key facts

  • RPO = Regular Production Option. A GM-specific three-character alphanumeric code that names one factory-installed item — an option, equipment group, paint, trim or axle ratio. Used across Chevrolet, GMC, Buick and Cadillac (and historically Pontiac, Oldsmobile, Saturn and Hummer); not a cross-make standard.
  • A VIN decode does not return the RPO list. Checked live on 2026-11-10, NHTSA vPIC returned its full 154-field schema (make, model, year, trim, engine, drivetrain, GVWR, plant, base price, safety flags) and no RPO code, no factory-option list and no axle ratio. Its only axle field is a count, not a final-drive ratio.
  • The full RPO list is on the SPID label, which prints every RPO in alphanumeric order, standard and optional, with no prices. Its location varies by model.
  • 2018+ cars moved it to a QR code on the driver-side B-pillar Certification label — up to 48 RPO codes when scanned, but GM states that scan is still partial versus the full build record.
  • Axle ratio is itself an RPO. GU6 = 3.42, GT4 = 3.73 and GT5 = 4.10 are axle-ratio RPOs on GM trucks, which is why the sticker matters to anyone buying to tow.

What does RPO stand for, and what is an RPO code?

RPO stands for Regular Production Option — a three-character alphanumeric code General Motors assigns to a single factory-installed item: an option, an equipment package, a color, a trim level, or a drivetrain component such as an axle ratio. A single vehicle carries dozens of them, and together they describe exactly how that unit was built.

RPO codes are a GM-only convention, appearing on Chevrolet, GMC, Buick and Cadillac (and historically Pontiac, Oldsmobile, Saturn and Hummer). They do not decode a Ford, Toyota or any non-GM vehicle — those brands use their own build and window-sticker code systems.

Where is the RPO / SPID sticker located in a GM vehicle?

The RPO list is printed on the Service Parts Identification (SPID) label, which lists every RPO in alphanumeric order — standard equipment and options together, with no prices. Its location is not universal; it varies by model and body style, so check your model's owner's manual rather than assuming one spot.

GM applied SPID labels broadly beginning around the 1984 model year (some references cite applications as early as 1967). Because the location moves around, the glovebox is the right first place to look on most older GM cars, but not a guarantee.

SPID label vs. the 2018+ QR code

Beginning with the 2018 model year, GM added a QR code to the Certification label on the driver-side B-pillar (on the Corvette, the driver's door). Scanning it returns, in order: the VIN, model year, model, build month and year, engineering book, vehicle order number, up to 48 three-character RPO codes, and the paint code. It is faster than reading a paper label — but GM's own documentation states the scan "does not show every RPO code" that appears in its full build history. So even the factory QR code is a partial list; GM's build records remain the complete, authoritative source.

The rear axle ratio is itself encoded as an RPO, which is why RPO codes matter to anyone buying a GM truck to tow. On GM trucks the common codes are:

First, these codes reflect the factory build only — if a previous owner swapped the axle, the sticker's RPO no longer matches the hardware, so confirm the physical axle before trusting it. Second, the axle ratio is only one input to a tow rating; it does not by itself give you a number. Does a free NHTSA VIN decoder show my GM option codes?

No. NHTSA's vPIC decoder is free, keyless and needs no registration, and it is the right first stop for a VIN — but it does not return RPO codes. A live DecodeVinValues pull for a 2019 Chevrolet Silverado VIN on 2026-11-10 returned all 154 fields of the endpoint's fixed schema — VIN, make, model, year, trim, series, body class, drive type, engine model, displacement, GVWR, plant city, base price and a long list of safety and ADAS flags — none of which is an RPO list, a factory-option list, or an axle ratio. The only axle field, "Axles," is a count, and even that is thin: it read "2" on the 2019 Silverado and came back empty on a 2021 Silverado and a 2024 GMC Sierra checked the same day. "AxleConfiguration" was empty on all three. EngineModel may echo an engine-family descriptor such as "L84" as text, but that is descriptive, not a parsed option list.

That is the same honest limit behind a window sticker by vin search and a general check of what features and options a used car has by vin: free tools confirm the build and surface history, but the exact factory code list comes from the label or the manufacturer.

What a VIN check can and can't confirm here

A VIN check confirms a seller's claims and screens a car's past, but it is not a substitute for the SPID label when you need the exact codes:

  • Can, via the SPID label or GM build records (not the VIN): the complete, authoritative RPO list — every factory option, trim, color and axle code.
  • Can, via a free vPIC decode: make, model, year, trim, engine, drivetrain, GVWR class, plant and base price.
  • Can, via a vehicle-history report: options/features at a descriptive level, plus accident and damage records (including airbag-deployment status), junk and salvage auction records, odometer/rollback checks, theft (NICB), ownership timeline, sales-listing history, NHTSA and IIHS ratings, market value, and recall presence.
  • Cannot, from any VIN tool: whether a recall was actually remedied, any open NHTSA investigation (PE/EA), per-unit dealer firmware status, or the legal title brand itself — those belong to NHTSA, the dealer, NMVTIS and the state DMV.

What to verify by VIN before you pay on a used GM

  1. Read the SPID label or scan the B-pillar QR code and list every RPO — your ground truth for options, trim, paint and axle.
  2. Match the seller's claims (tow package, axle ratio, trim) to the RPO codes, then confirm the big-ticket items by eye.
  3. Confirm the axle physically if the RPO shows GT4/GT5/G80 — a swap invalidates the code.
  4. Run a free NHTSA vPIC decode to confirm make/model/year/trim/engine/drivetrain and base price.
  5. Run a vehicle-history report for accident, salvage-auction, odometer, theft, ownership and listing history, plus recall presence.
  6. Confirm any recall was remedied with a GM dealer and check NHTSA for open investigations — a report shows recall presence, not remedy status.

Frequently asked questions

How do I decode a GM RPO code? Read the three-character code off the SPID label (or the 2018+ QR scan), then match it against a GM RPO reference for that model year. The label lists them in alphanumeric order; a dealer can pull the complete, authoritative meaning from GM's build records.

How do I find my axle ratio by VIN? You can't directly — the VIN decode has no axle-ratio field. The ratio is an RPO (GU6 = 3.42, GT4 = 3.73, GT5 = 4.10, etc.) printed on the SPID label. Read the code there, and confirm the physical axle in case it was swapped.

Why doesn't my VIN decode list all my factory options? Because factory options are not encoded in the 17-character VIN. The VIN carries the WMI, vehicle attributes, check digit, model year, plant and serial — enough for build and safety data, but the option list is stored separately on the SPID label and in GM's records.

How can I confirm a used-car seller's claimed options are real? Read the SPID sticker (glovebox, trunk or console lid, or spare-tire cover) and match each claimed option to an RPO code, then verify equipment by eye. A VIN history report cross-checks specs and surfaces accident, salvage and ownership history, but the raw codes come from the label.

Is the RPO sticker accurate if the axle was swapped? No. The SPID label records the factory build. If a previous owner changed the axle, differential or other hardware, the original RPO no longer matches what's on the car, so verify the physical component before relying on the code.

Where can I look up GM build records by VIN if the sticker is missing? A GM dealer can retrieve the full RPO build for your VIN from GM's records — the authoritative, complete source. That's the fallback when the SPID label is gone, painted over, or unreadable.
