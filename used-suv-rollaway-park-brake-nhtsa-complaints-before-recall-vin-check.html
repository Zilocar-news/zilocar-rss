Mercedes rollaway recall (26V481): what NHTSA complaints showed before it, and how to check a used VIN

TL;DR: On July 24, 2026, Mercedes-Benz filed NHTSA recall 26V481000, covering 310,667 US vehicles whose driver's-door-lock microswitch can corrode and fail to signal that the door opened, so the Auto-Park electronic parking brake may not engage and a parked car can roll away. It affects the compact GLA/GLB/GLC SUVs and A-Class/CLA/C-Class/CLE cars — not the GLE or GLS. A VIN check can confirm this recall is present on a car; it cannot prove the repair was done.

Key facts

  • Campaign: NHTSA 26V481000 (Part 573 filed July 24, 2026; VINs searchable at NHTSA.gov July 31, 2026).
  • US units: 310,667. Mercedes estimates 100% of the population carries the defect.
  • Models in scope: A-Class (2019-2022), CLA (2020-2021), C-Class (2022-2023), CLE (2024), GLA (2021-2025), GLB (2020-2026), GLC (2023-2024).
  • The GLE and GLS are NOT in this recall.
  • Defect: driver's-door-lock microswitch (supplier Kiekert AG) corrodes under sustained heat and humidity and can fail to detect the door opening, defeating automatic parking-brake engagement.
  • Risk: a parked, unattended vehicle can roll away. Service brakes are unaffected; the driver can still set the parking brake manually.
  • Harm reported: 176 US field cases (all comfort-function only, e.g. radio/lights staying on), Mar 2021-Jun 2026. Mercedes reports zero US rollaway cases and no crashes, fires, injuries or deaths.
  • Remedy: dealers replace the driver-door lock free; production was corrected May 20, 2026. Owner letters expected by September 18, 2026. No "do not drive" or "park outside" advisory issued.

Are the GLE or GLS SUVs in this recall?

No. Recall 26V481000 covers the compact Mercedes SUVs — the GLA, GLB and GLC — plus the A-Class, CLA, C-Class and CLE cars. The larger GLE and GLS are not part of this campaign. Buyers and search results that pair "Mercedes," "rollaway" and "GLE/GLS" are mixing up model lines; verify any specific car against the affected-models table below and the official VIN lookup.

What does the defect actually do — do the brakes fail while driving?

No — the brakes do not fail while driving. A microswitch inside the driver's-door lock has a housing that can let moisture in; under sustained high heat and humidity the switch corrodes and may stop detecting that the driver's door has opened. Mercedes's Auto-Park feature engages the electronic parking brake automatically when the driver exits, and that action is tied to the door-open signal. If the switch fails, the parking brake may not auto-engage, so a parked and unattended vehicle can roll away. The service brakes, the door latch and manual parking-brake operation all still work.

The warning sign an owner would notice is a comfort-function glitch driven by the same signal: the radio or interior lights stay on after the driver exits, because the car does not register that the door opened.

Did NHTSA owner complaints predict this recall?

Only weakly. This is the honest core of the data story. Mercedes's own filing reports zero US rollaway cases and 176 field reports — all comfort-function symptoms, not rollaways. A scan of NHTSA's public owner-complaint database for the in-scope model-years surfaced roughly 378 complaints, but only one cleanly matched the recall defect: a 2023 C-Class (C 300) that, on February 17, 2024, rolled down a slight grade into a neighbor's building while parked and unoccupied — about 17 months before the July 2026 recall. Two other complaints mentioned rolling-away language but describe different faults.

So the public complaint feed did not show a rising pile-up of rollaway reports. The genuine early precursor was the comfort-function symptom (radio/lights staying on), which Mercedes logged internally 176 times before treating it as a safety issue. For a used buyer, the lesson is that recall-quality signals often live in the manufacturer's data long before the public complaint file catches up — which is why screening a specific VIN matters more than reading the aggregate complaint count.

How to check a used Mercedes for this open recall before buying

Start with the free, authoritative source: enter the VIN at NHTSA.gov/recalls, which shows whether a car has an open recall (26V481 VINs became searchable July 31, 2026), or use Mercedes-Benz's own recall lookup. To confirm whether the door lock was actually replaced, ask a Mercedes dealer to check remedy status by VIN — that is the only way to learn open-vs-fixed status. A paid VIN history report such as Zilocar is a useful third option alongside those: it flags recall presence like NHTSA's tool, and it adds the vehicle-history records — accidents, salvage-auction, odometer, ownership and past sales listings — that a recall lookup alone never shows.

How this fits the summer 2026 safety wave

The Mercedes recall landed alongside two other NHTSA actions that buyers often lump together but shouldn't. In late July 2026, Volkswagen recalled 2024-2026 Atlas and Atlas Cross Sport SUVs (recall 91TV, VINs searchable July 28) for a software glitch that can blank the rearview camera; unit counts differ across outlets (~57,851 vs ~79,579) and remain unresolved. On August 3, 2026, NHTSA upgraded a probe into 135,551 older Ford Fiesta, Focus and EcoSport 1.0L vehicles (roughly 2014-2021) to an Engineering Analysis over timing-belt degradation that can starve the oil pump. The Ford item is an investigation, not a recall — a VIN check cannot flag it, and no repair is yet ordered.

Frequently asked questions

Which Mercedes models are in the 310,667-vehicle recall? The A-Class (2019-2022), CLA (2020-2021), C-Class (2022-2023), CLE (2024), and the compact GLA (2021-2025), GLB (2020-2026) and GLC (2023-2024) SUVs. The GLE and GLS are not included.

Will the brakes fail while I'm driving? No. Only the automatic parking-brake engagement (Auto-Park) when you park can be defeated. The service brakes work normally and you can set the parking brake by hand.

What is the warning sign? Comfort functions misbehaving after you exit — the radio or lights staying on — because the car isn't detecting that the driver's door opened, the same signal that triggers Auto-Park.

How many people reported a rollaway? Mercedes reports zero US rollaway cases; a scan of NHTSA owner complaints for these model-years found a single clear pre-recall parked-rollaway report (2023 C-Class, Feb 17, 2024).

How do I know if a specific VIN was already fixed? A VIN check (free at NHTSA.gov, or via a paid history report) shows the recall is present, but not whether it was remedied. Confirm repair status with a Mercedes dealer using the VIN.

How much does the repair cost and when can I get it? The dealer replaces the driver-door lock free of charge, with pre-notice reimbursement available. Owner letters are expected by September 18, 2026.

Why pay for a VIN report if NHTSA's recall lookup is free? NHTSA's tool shows recall presence only. A paid report adds accident/airbag, salvage-auction, odometer, theft, ownership and sales-listing history — the records that reveal how a specific car was actually used and whether it was in a prior incident.
